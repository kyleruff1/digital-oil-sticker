## Outcome

A short product constitution defines which constraints are promises, which are preferences, and how success is measured. It becomes the precedence document when later tickets conflict: every subsequent issue cites the invariant it implements or is bound by, and conflicts are resolved by the constitution, not by whoever edited last.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

Architecture, UX, QA, and data work cannot be estimated consistently while "offline," "local account," "exact vehicle," and "lightweight" mean different things to different people. This issue unblocks every other M00 issue and supplies scope-control tests for all later milestones. It is the single blocker for DOS-M00-002, DOS-M00-003, and DOS-M00-006, and (transitively) for the persistence and notification proofs.

## Included

- Define production v1 as native-packaged iOS and Android only. Browser execution is permitted solely as a local development convenience and is not a production promise; PWA/web distribution requires a separate future architecture decision.
- Define “no online accounts” as: no registration, login, remote profile, identity provider, password reset, cloud synchronization, server-side personal record, or analytics identifier tied to a person. A “local profile” is a set of records on one installation, not an authenticated identity.
- Define “offline-first” precisely: the app bundle includes a usable baseline vehicle catalog, so first launch, vehicle selection, status, oil-change logging, driving-usage editing, history, and due calculation work in airplane mode without a provisioning download. A later optional catalog update may require connectivity but cannot be required to begin using the installed app.
- Define the rolling catalog target (“last 30 model years”), the meaning and update cadence of “major manufacturers,” and the supported markets. Record that trim/build completeness depends on authoritative source coverage and cannot be inferred.
- Establish measurable targets for cold start, on-device Phoenix readiness, WebView connection, common LiveView interaction latency, packaged app size, bundled catalog size, offline readiness, accessibility, and minimum supported iOS/Android versions. Numerical values may be provisional until the physical-device proof, but each must have an owner and ratification date.
- Define the core user promise: the app estimates when an oil change may be due from recorded mileage, elapsed time, manufacturer guidance when available, product guidance when licensed/verified, and user-entered driving samples. It does not read the odometer or diagnose the vehicle.
- Classify every constraint with RFC 2119 language (MUST/SHOULD/MAY) and separate release promises from aspirations.
- Produce the one-page traceability table mapping each invariant to the milestone that verifies it.

## Excluded

- Choosing storage libraries, implementing screens, writing production code, buying datasets, or claiming comprehensive manufacturer/oil/filter coverage.
- Online sync, social features, fleet administration, telematics/OBD integration, repair scheduling, or guaranteed notification delivery.

## Functional requirements

- FR-1: The constitution defines production v1 as native-packaged iOS and Android only; browser execution is documented as a local development convenience only, with PWA/web distribution explicitly deferred to a separate future architecture decision.
- FR-2: The constitution defines "no online accounts" exactly as: no registration, login, remote profile, identity provider, password reset, cloud synchronization, server-side personal record, or analytics identifier tied to a person; and defines a "local profile" as a set of records on one installation, not an authenticated identity.
- FR-3: The constitution defines "offline-first" such that first launch, vehicle selection, status, oil-change logging, driving-usage editing, history, and due calculation all work in airplane mode from the bundled baseline catalog, with no provisioning download; any later optional catalog update MAY require connectivity but MUST NOT be required to begin using the installed app.
- FR-4: The constitution fixes the rolling catalog target ("last 30 model years"), defines "major manufacturers" including its update cadence, lists supported markets, and records that trim/build completeness depends on authoritative source coverage and cannot be inferred. The §5 planning default (market: United States; 30 model years; for the 2026 baseline, 1997–2026 inclusive) is the starting position unless M00 ratifies a different matrix.
- FR-5: The constitution establishes measurable targets — cold start, on-device Phoenix readiness, WebView connection, common LiveView interaction latency, packaged app size, bundled catalog size, offline readiness, accessibility, and minimum supported iOS/Android versions — each with a named owner and a ratification date; values may be provisional until the physical-device proofs (DOS-M00-002/003/004) supply measurements.
- FR-6: The constitution states the core user promise: the app estimates when an oil change may be due from recorded mileage, elapsed time, manufacturer guidance when available, product guidance when licensed/verified, and user-entered driving samples; it does not read the odometer or diagnose the vehicle.
- FR-7: Every constraint uses RFC 2119 language: MUST for release blockers, SHOULD for defaults that require an explicit exception, MAY for optional behavior. Later issues must cite the relevant invariant.
- FR-8: The constitution records the strict-loopback runtime promise: the app endpoint MUST bind only to `127.0.0.1`; it MUST NOT listen on `0.0.0.0`, a LAN address, or a public interface.
- FR-9: The constitution fixes safety-adjacent copy rules: results are called an "estimate" or "reminder," preserve the source and effective model-year context, and direct the user to the owner's manual/qualified service provider when data is missing or conflicting.
- FR-10: The constitution forbids any interface implying that a local profile is backed up or recoverable unless an explicit export/import feature has shipped.
- FR-11: Prohibited scope is listed explicitly, including remote accounts and hidden server storage, drawing on master prompt §5 post-MVP exclusions.
- FR-12: A one-page traceability table maps each invariant to its verification milestone, and a tabletop review of the three required scenarios is recorded.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

The deliverable is a version-controlled constitution document plus a one-page traceability table, committed to the repository's planning/decision documentation area (exact path fixed when the issue is scheduled; it must be a stable, linkable location). The Product workstream owns the document; product, engineering, design, data, and QA representatives are co-signers. Amendments after ratification require a new revision and re-sign-off — never in-place edits that keep an old sign-off attached.

### Data and persistence

N/A — decision document; no application data stores are created. The document itself codifies persistence-relevant promises (bundled-catalog-at-first-launch, local-only records, no server-side personal record) that DOS-M00-004 must implement and measure.

### Interfaces and events

N/A — no software interfaces. The constitution is the precedence interface for the roadmap: later issues MUST cite the invariant they implement or constrain, and scope disputes resolve against the ratified revision.

### Failure and recovery behavior

If a later ticket conflicts with the constitution, the constitution prevails; the discovery becomes a new issue or ADR per master prompt §3 rule 10 (change control) — never a silent enlargement of an active ticket. If a physical-device proof invalidates a provisional target, the target is revised through an explicit re-ratification recorded in the document history, not by quietly editing the number.

### Security and privacy

The constitution must encode as MUST-level invariants: strict `127.0.0.1` binding (never `0.0.0.0`, LAN, or public interfaces), no online accounts, no telemetry or third-party analytics by default, no personal data leaving the device, and no backup/recovery claims before an export/import feature ships. These derive from master prompt §3 and §7.

### Accessibility and performance

The measurable-target table (FR-5) must include accessibility and performance rows — cold start, Phoenix readiness, WebView connection, LiveView interaction latency, packaged app size, bundled catalog size, offline readiness, accessibility, minimum supported OS versions — each with an owner and ratification date. Provisional values are permitted until DOS-M00-002/003/004 measurements exist, but a row without an owner fails review.

## UX and content states

The constitution ratifies the required terminology from master prompt §6 for all later UI work: say "Meets the recorded requirements," not "manufacturer approved," unless the source explicitly documents an OEM approval; say "Estimated due date" with a confidence label; say "Source unavailable" or "Exact configuration not verified" rather than supplying a generic value; keep oil-life monitor, calendar interval, mileage interval, normal service, and severe service distinct. Safety-adjacent copy calls results an "estimate" or "reminder" and directs users to the owner's manual/qualified service provider when data is missing or conflicting. No interface may imply a local profile is backed up or recoverable unless export/import has shipped.
