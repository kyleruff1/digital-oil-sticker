## Outcome

An architecture decision record (ADR) and reproducible proof confirm the binding design: `mix mob.new digital_oil_sticker --liveview`; BEAM/Phoenix runs inside the installed iOS/Android application; its endpoint listens strictly on `127.0.0.1`; the native WebView connects to that loopback LiveView; all core state is on-device. Netlify hosts only a separate static marketing/help/privacy site and, in a later milestone, downloadable catalog artifacts.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

This runtime is unusual enough that it must be proven on physical devices before feature work. It also prevents a recurring architecture mistake: Netlify cannot run the BEAM or the LiveView socket and must never be treated as the app host. This issue unblocks repository foundation, endpoint hardening, native lifecycle work, persistence, packaging, and the separate support-site deployment. Within M00 it is a direct blocker for DOS-M00-004 and DOS-M00-005, and its ADR must absorb the DOS-M00-003 runtime verdict.

- Master prompt §4.1 (Mob and LiveView are on-device): Mob `0.7.20` embeds BEAM and Phoenix in each app, runs the endpoint on `http://127.0.0.1:4000`, and displays it in a native WebView; the two mutually exclusive JavaScript bridges (native WebView bridge vs. the `MobHook` LiveView bridge that replaces `window.mob` after the LiveView socket connects); the `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket` vs. `Mob.Socket` distinction; the planned typed `DeviceCommandBroker` with dispatch through the documented `Mob.Screen.dispatch/3` path; the M00 go/no-go spike mandate.

## Technical implementation contract

### Hypothesis

A Mob `0.7.20` application generated with `mix mob.new digital_oil_sticker --liveview` can, on physical iOS and Android hardware: run BEAM/Phoenix entirely inside the installed app; bind its endpoint strictly to IPv4 `127.0.0.1` with no fallback; serve LiveView to the native WebView across cold launch, background/resume, force-quit/relaunch, offline launch, and clean shutdown; support both documented bridges including a typed DeviceCommandBroker round trip through the registered root `Mob.Screen` with correlated acknowledgement; and remain hardened (origin/CSP/debug restrictions) in a production-like build — while Netlify remains a fully independent static surface the app never needs.

### Timebox

The timebox duration is assigned at refinement (estimates stay blank at bootstrap per the roadmap metadata rules). When the timebox expires without the success criteria met, the spike stops and the stop-gate path activates: document the failed or unproven invariant and open the replacement-architecture decision before M01. The timebox may not be silently extended to avoid the verdict.

### Alternatives considered

- LiveView Native or a remote Phoenix server: explicitly rejected by master prompt §4.1 — this design is not LiveView Native and not a hosted Phoenix; they are not fallback options within this spike.
- Netlify as application runtime (Functions/Edge Functions): rejected — Netlify cannot run the BEAM or the LiveView socket (§4.3); it remains a static boundary.
- PWA, remote Phoenix host, static client, or browser production target: named as prohibited silent substitutions; any of them may only be adopted through an explicitly approved replacement ADR after a documented spike failure.
- Mixed native + Phoenix navigation stacks: constrained by §4.1 — prefer one Mob root WebView and Phoenix route stack; native screens only for device functions the LiveView bridge cannot safely expose.

### Success and failure criteria

Success: every acceptance criterion AC-1 through AC-7 passes on both physical platforms with attached evidence, and the ADR is ratified. Failure: any binding invariant fails (loopback-only binding, on-device BEAM/Phoenix, offline operation, WebView hardening, either bridge, or the DeviceCommandBroker round trip) or cannot be demonstrated within the timebox. Failure outcome per AC-8: M01 is blocked pending an explicitly approved replacement ADR; the failed invariant is documented; no silent substitution occurs.

### Mandatory ADR and follow-ups

- The final ADR distinguishes `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket`, `Mob.Socket`, the `MobHook` page-message bridge, and root-screen plugin command dispatch; it includes process, request, storage, command/acknowledgement, and trust-boundary diagrams plus startup/failure/recovery sequences, the Netlify separation statement, and a replacement trigger if Mob cannot sustain the contract.
- The DOS-M00-003 physical-device runtime verdict must be reflected in this final ADR before ratification.
- Follow-ups on success: release the DOS-M00-004 and DOS-M00-005 blockers; feed endpoint-hardening and readiness-handshake specifics into the M01 foundation issues; feed the Netlify static-site boundary into the support-site issue set.
- Follow-ups on failure: open the replacement-architecture decision issue, block M01, and record the failed invariant in the DOS-M00-000 gate register.

## UX and content states

The proof itself needs no product UI, but it fixes runtime UX contracts: the native launcher owns process readiness and presents a recoverable startup state if BEAM fails — it must not redirect to a remote fallback; the WebView shows the app only after the readiness handshake, so users never see a race or a blank remote error; offline launch behaves identically to online launch for core startup. The Netlify proof page is plain static content (marketing/help/privacy/attribution placeholders) with no login, session, or app dependency.

## Test plan

### Automated

- Scripted, documented build-and-run commands reproducing the proof from a clean checkout with pinned versions (Mob `0.7.20`, Elixir/Erlang, native toolchains).
- Bridge negative tests that must fail correctly when: `MobHook` is absent; the root screen restarts with pending commands; a command times out; an acknowledgement is duplicated; a `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket` is mistakenly passed where `Mob.Socket` is required.
- Endpoint binding assertions: configuration/runtime checks that the listener address is IPv4 `127.0.0.1` and never `0.0.0.0`, `::`, or a LAN address.
- Netlify static-site build check confirming the deploy contains no Functions, Edge Functions, Blobs, or runtime API surface.

### Manual/device matrix

- One physical iPhone and one physical Android phone (models and OS versions recorded): cold launch, LiveView connect, background/resume, force-quit/relaunch, airplane-mode cold launch, clean shutdown — captured on video.
- From another device on the same LAN, scan/attempt the selected port while the app runs; show that it is unreachable. On-device, show the loopback connection succeeds.
- Attempt disallowed WebView navigation, a forged Host/Origin, non-loopback binding, remote script loading, and production WebView debugging; attach the blocked results.
- Exercise the harmless bridged capability end-to-end on both platforms and observe the correlated acknowledgement.

## Dependency rationale

Blocked by DOS-M00-001: the constitution supplies the ratified invariants (strict loopback, offline-first, native-only v1) this spike exists to verify, and its provisional readiness/latency targets shape what the proof measures. Coordinates with DOS-M00-003 (not a blocker): the two spikes share the disposable shell and physical devices, and DOS-M00-003's runtime verdict must be reflected in this issue's final ADR. ## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: Mob `0.7.20` (pre-1.0) cannot sustain a binding invariant on real hardware. Mitigation: the stop-gate rule — documented failure, replacement ADR before M01, no silent substitution.
- Risk: WebView/OS differences make the readiness handshake or reconnection behavior platform-divergent. Mitigation: per-platform evidence and explicit handshake specification in the ADR.
- Risk: confusing the two bridges leads later tickets to pass a Phoenix socket to plugin APIs. Mitigation: AC-7's negative tests and the ADR's explicit `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket` vs. `Mob.Socket` distinction.
- Decision produced here: the binding runtime ADR, including port-selection policy and the DeviceCommandBroker registration/recovery contract.
- Open question (resolved by the spike): whether release-like builds behave identically to debug builds for binding, hardening, and bridge behavior on both platforms.
