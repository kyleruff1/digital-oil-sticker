> **RESCOPED FOR BROWSER MVP — 2026-08-01.** [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) established the browser-first MVP. This card is retained with the same identity; its Outcome and functional requirements below are the rescoped spec. The pre-pivot spec is preserved at the bottom under "Superseded pre-pivot spec (historical record)". Disposition per [PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md](../../docs/governance/PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md#issue-by-issue-classification): _Becomes the browser-MVP architecture ADR: Fly.io as the sole app host, Netlify as static-content-only, browser storage as the sole personal-data store, and the hydration protocol as the ratified seam._

## Outcome

The browser-MVP architecture is ratified and binding: [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) is closed as the accepted architecture ADR for the MVP, superseding [ADR-0001](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0001-mob-liveview-on-device.md) for MVP and amending [ADR-0002](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0002-netlify-static-boundary.md); [ADR-0003](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0003-data-boundaries.md) survives unchanged, with one new obligation about hosted-serving licence review captured against it. At ratification the record binds four things that were previously ambiguous or contradicted: (1) Fly.io in region `ord` with `min_machines_running = 1` is the sole application host and serves Phoenix LiveView from the apex `digitaloilsticker.com` and `www.` behind HTTPS/WSS with an origin-restricted `check_origin` list (INV-1, INV-22, INV-27); (2) Netlify (team `cDiscourse`) is a **static-content-only** boundary — marketing, support, privacy, source attribution on subdomains such as `help.` and `legal.`, plus post-MVP versioned catalog artefacts — and hosts no Phoenix, no LiveView socket, no Functions, no Edge Functions, no Blobs, no personal data (INV-27, INV-4); (3) the browser's IndexedDB is the sole home for personal data (garage, service history, odometer readings, reminder intent, preferences), and the deployed application runs exactly one Ecto repo, `DigitalOilSticker.CatalogRepo`, opened read-only (INV-3, INV-23, INV-4); (4) the ADR's hydration protocol — envelope, mount-and-hydrate sequence, first-paint rules, server-side validation, schema versioning, mutation round trip, quota and eviction handling, private-browsing ladder, multi-tab compare-and-set, export/import — is the ratified seam between LiveView's server-side state and the client-only personal record (INV-24.1 through INV-24.8, INV-25, INV-26). The card also records that INV-1, INV-2, INV-6, INV-7, INV-17, and INV-19 were re-ratified in [CONSTITUTION.md](../../docs/product/CONSTITUTION.md) revision 2.0.0 §0 so no ticket still cites their 1.0.0 forms, and it releases the M09 gate: `DOS-M09-*` implementation issues may be marked Ready only after this card closes (per CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2a).

Producing a fresh on-device Mob/LiveView ADR is **out of scope** and remains so unless a revival trigger in ADR-0004 §"Trigger conditions that revive the native track" fires — Mac/Xcode obtained, Mob 1.0 with documented Windows/WSL2 support and CI, a repeatable WSL2 build host that satisfies DOS-M00-003's Android half, product evidence demanding native capabilities (measured browser-storage-eviction loss or user demand for OS notifications), or the browser MVP validating the data product enough to make native a distribution decision. A trigger firing produces **ADR-0005**, not a rewrite of ADR-0001 or of this card.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

This card ratifies the architectural decision that changes *where the entire product runs* — server rather than device, browser storage rather than SQLite, deploy rather than store release. Until this ratification closes, three things are stuck: (a) `DOS-M09-*` cannot start under CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2a, because they implement what this ADR ratifies (the pivot doc's "note on non-duplication" makes this card the M09 gate rather than creating a new M09 architecture card); (b) rescoped cards in every other milestone continue to cite an ADR that ADR-0004 has already invalidated, so a scheduler picking up an "on-device Mob/LiveView ADR" outcome would produce work the accepted architecture contradicts; and (c) the constitution's re-ratified invariants (INV-1, INV-22, INV-23, INV-24, INV-25, INV-26, INV-27) have no acknowledged ADR-level record wiring them to a deployed shape, which leaves downstream tickets free to interpret them freely.

Ratifying this card also formalises what is *deliberately not owned by an implementation card yet*: the hard licence gate ADR-0004 records against ADR-0003 (no `documented-facts` or `permission-needed` source is served from the hosted app until `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` and `LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md` are re-reviewed for web-serving rights specifically), and the eleven risks R1–R11 that ADR-0004 lists. Closing this card gates M09 in; leaving it open means the browser MVP starts building against an unratified architecture.

Downstream: this card blocks the ten `DOS-M09-*` implementation issues (`DOS-M09-001` schema, `DOS-M09-002` adapter, `DOS-M09-003` protocol, `DOS-M09-004` UX, `DOS-M09-005` query layer, `DOS-M09-006` Fly deploy, `DOS-M09-007` domain and boundary, `DOS-M09-008` hardening, `DOS-M09-009` conformance matrix, `DOS-M09-010` PWA/Push spike) and unblocks the rescoped `DOS-M02-000`, `DOS-M04-000`, `DOS-M05-000`, and `DOS-M07-000` epics that need the ratified seam to specify against. It does not unblock the deferred native cards (`DOS-M00-003`, `DOS-M00-005`, `DOS-M06-006`, `DOS-M08-004`, `DOS-M08-007`) — those wait on a triggered ADR-0005, not on this closure.

## Technical implementation contract

### Ratification act

Ratification is an act of record, not a build. The act is: (a) confirm ADR-0004's Status line reads `Accepted — 2026-08-01`; (b) confirm every FR-2 through FR-9 above is cited to a specific ADR-0004 section or constitution 2.0.0 clause; (c) confirm the ADR-0001 and ADR-0002 header updates are made and the ADR-0003 obligation is recorded against `SOURCE_REGISTER.md`; (d) confirm constitution 2.0.0 §0.3/§0.4 line up with ADR-0004's "Constitution invariants requiring re-ratification" table; (e) record acceptance in the `DOS-M00-000` gate register with the eleven-risk R1–R11 register and the M09-gate-release note; (f) update `planning/roadmap.yml` to release the M09 blocked_by edges.

### What ratification does not do

It does not draft new architecture. Every hydration-protocol clause, every hosting parameter, every security-boundary rule, every consequence, and every risk is already written in ADR-0004; this card only binds them. If ratification discovers a gap in ADR-0004, the correct response is to amend ADR-0004 (or add an ADR-0004a) before ratification, not to fold the missing clause into this card's body.

### Alternatives considered (for the ratification act itself)

- **Author a new on-device Mob/LiveView ADR anyway.** Rejected: ADR-0004 explicitly supersedes ADR-0001 for MVP; producing a fresh on-device ADR under this card would create two contradictory architecture ADRs. If the native track revives it revives as **ADR-0005** with fresh evidence and a fresh DOS-M00-003 run.
- **Fold ADR-0004's normative clauses into this card's body.** Rejected: it would duplicate the ADR and create drift between the two records. The card cites the ADR by section; the ADR is the single source of truth.
- **Ratify by silent close (mark accepted, no gate-register note).** Rejected: CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2a requires an ADR before dependent implementation issues may be marked Ready, and the M09 gate release must be explicit and dated.
- **Ratify without the ADR-0001/ADR-0002 header updates.** Rejected: leaving ADR-0001 as an active decision alongside ADR-0004 would let a future reader treat the on-device track as a live option; the header updates prevent that misreading.

### Success and failure criteria

**Success:** every acceptance criterion AC-1 through AC-9 passes; `DOS-M09-000` and its ten children are cleared to Ready in `planning/roadmap.yml`; the eleven-risk register is filed with owners; the ADR-0001 and ADR-0002 headers are updated; the ADR-0003 hosted-serving obligation is recorded. **Failure:** a gap in ADR-0004 is discovered that a `DOS-M09-*` card cannot proceed against, or a constitution 2.0.0 clause contradicts ADR-0004. Failure outcome: raise the gap as an ADR-0004 amendment (or ADR-0004a) and re-open ratification against the amended ADR; do **not** ratify against a known gap and do **not** paper over the contradiction in this card's body.

## UX and content states

This is a ratification act, not a product surface, and it ships no UI. Its user-facing consequences are inherited from ADR-0004: the browser MVP is a hosted Phoenix LiveView application whose first paint is a determinate skeleton rather than a false empty state, whose reconnect is a re-hydration under a stable shell (`phx-disconnected` styling), whose storage-status UI honestly reports `navigator.storage.persist()`'s answer, whose empty-vs-evicted-vs-unavailable-vs-newer-than-server states are each rendered differently, whose mutation "saving" affordance never claims a write is durable until the client ack arrives, and whose privacy copy states plainly that data lives in this browser only and that clearing browser data deletes it permanently (INV-5, INV-24.3, INV-24.4, INV-24.6, INV-24.8, INV-25). The prohibited words for the storage-status and privacy surfaces ("backup", "synced", "safe", "we'll remember", "your account") are recorded here as inheriting from ADR-0004 §"Export and import".

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- **Risk (inherited from ADR-0004 R1 — browser eviction of infrequently-used site storage):** a quarterly-usage product is exactly the profile most at risk of ITP-style eviction. Mitigation: this ratification binds ADR-0004's `navigator.storage.persist()`, storage-status UI, export nudges, and `:data_missing` state; measured per-browser eviction behaviour is owned by `DOS-M09-009`. If measured loss is unacceptable, that is one of the four revival triggers for ADR-0005.
- **Risk (inherited from R2):** hydration adds latency to first meaningful paint on every mount and every reconnect. Mitigation: budget explicitly under `DOS-M09-003` and `DOS-M09-005`; measure against constitution 2.0.0 §9's provisional targets.
- **Risk (inherited from R3):** the strict CSP is unverified against LiveView's runtime (transitions, JS commands, uploads). Mitigation: verified in `DOS-M09-006` / `DOS-M09-008`; a per-request nonce plug is the only permitted resolution to inline requirements — `'unsafe-inline'` is not acceptable.
- **Risk (inherited from R4):** last-write-wins-with-loss-made-visible at record granularity is the ratified multi-tab conflict rule; re-examine on evidence.
- **Risk (inherited from R5) — satisfied:** invariant re-ratification is complete under constitution 2.0.0.
- **Risk (inherited from R6):** web-serving rights for licensed catalog sources are unreviewed. Mitigation: the hard licence gate in FR-8; no `documented-facts` or `permission-needed` source ships from the hosted app until the review completes.
- **Risk (inherited from R7):** catalog endpoints are publicly scrapable in a way an app bundle was not. Mitigation: rate limiting in FR-6 and `DOS-M09-005`; open question whether the licence review (R6) changes the product surface.
- **Risk (inherited from R8):** single region, single always-on machine → Fly outage or zone failure = total outage; deploys drop all sockets. Mitigation: reconnect and re-hydration robustness under `DOS-M09-003`; a second machine is a bounded cost decision.
- **Risk (inherited from R9):** §9 latency budgets are void; new provisional budgets must be measured and re-ratified rather than shipped against.
- **Risk (inherited from R10):** cost growth under traffic or abuse is unmeasured; recorded monthly cost is owned by `DOS-M09-006`.
- **Risk (inherited from R11):** the core product bet — whether users accept a product that does nothing offline and never notifies them — is honestly unknown; the browser MVP exists to find out.
- **Decision produced here:** ratification of ADR-0004 as the binding browser-MVP architecture ADR, with the M09 gate released and the on-device Mob/LiveView track deferred behind the four ADR-0004 revival triggers.
- **Open question:** whether the licence-gate review (R6) constrains `documented-facts` and `permission-needed` sources enough to change the vehicle-coverage promise. Resolved by the review, not by this card.

### Technical implementation contract (pre-pivot)

#### Hypothesis

A Mob `0.7.20` application generated with `mix mob.new digital_oil_sticker --liveview` can, on physical iOS and Android hardware: run BEAM/Phoenix entirely inside the installed app; bind its endpoint strictly to IPv4 `127.0.0.1` with no fallback; serve LiveView to the native WebView across cold launch, background/resume, force-quit/relaunch, offline launch, and clean shutdown; support both documented bridges including a typed DeviceCommandBroker round trip through the registered root `Mob.Screen` with correlated acknowledgement; and remain hardened (origin/CSP/debug restrictions) in a production-like build — while Netlify remains a fully independent static surface the app never needs.

#### Timebox

The timebox duration is assigned at refinement (estimates stay blank at bootstrap per the roadmap metadata rules). When the timebox expires without the success criteria met, the spike stops and the stop-gate path activates: document the failed or unproven invariant and open the replacement-architecture decision before M01. The timebox may not be silently extended to avoid the verdict.

#### Alternatives considered

- LiveView Native or a remote Phoenix server: explicitly rejected by master prompt §4.1 — this design is not LiveView Native and not a hosted Phoenix; they are not fallback options within this spike.
- Netlify as application runtime (Functions/Edge Functions): rejected — Netlify cannot run the BEAM or the LiveView socket (§4.3); it remains a static boundary.
- PWA, remote Phoenix host, static client, or browser production target: named as prohibited silent substitutions; any of them may only be adopted through an explicitly approved replacement ADR after a documented spike failure.
- Mixed native + Phoenix navigation stacks: constrained by §4.1 — prefer one Mob root WebView and Phoenix route stack; native screens only for device functions the LiveView bridge cannot safely expose.

#### Success and failure criteria

Success: every acceptance criterion AC-1 through AC-7 passes on both physical platforms with attached evidence, and the ADR is ratified. Failure: any binding invariant fails (loopback-only binding, on-device BEAM/Phoenix, offline operation, WebView hardening, either bridge, or the DeviceCommandBroker round trip) or cannot be demonstrated within the timebox. Failure outcome per AC-8: M01 is blocked pending an explicitly approved replacement ADR; the failed invariant is documented; no silent substitution occurs.

#### Mandatory ADR and follow-ups

- The final ADR distinguishes `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket`, `Mob.Socket`, the `MobHook` page-message bridge, and root-screen plugin command dispatch; it includes process, request, storage, command/acknowledgement, and trust-boundary diagrams plus startup/failure/recovery sequences, the Netlify separation statement, and a replacement trigger if Mob cannot sustain the contract.
- The DOS-M00-003 physical-device runtime verdict must be reflected in this final ADR before ratification.
- Follow-ups on success: release the DOS-M00-004 and DOS-M00-005 blockers; feed endpoint-hardening and readiness-handshake specifics into the M01 foundation issues; feed the Netlify static-site boundary into the support-site issue set.
- Follow-ups on failure: open the replacement-architecture decision issue, block M01, and record the failed invariant in the DOS-M00-000 gate register.

### UX and content states (pre-pivot)

The proof itself needs no product UI, but it fixes runtime UX contracts: the native launcher owns process readiness and presents a recoverable startup state if BEAM fails — it must not redirect to a remote fallback; the WebView shows the app only after the readiness handshake, so users never see a race or a blank remote error; offline launch behaves identically to online launch for core startup. The Netlify proof page is plain static content (marketing/help/privacy/attribution placeholders) with no login, session, or app dependency.
