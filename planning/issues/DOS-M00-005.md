> _`mob_notify` is a native OS-notification binding with no browser equivalent; its truthful-delivery-fallback language is inherited by the in-app due-state work in the re-scoped M06._
>
> The original scope below is preserved intact for the historical record.

## Outcome

A capability matrix and physical-device proof establish exactly what `mob_notify` can schedule locally on iOS and Android without accounts, push subscriptions, or a server. Product copy and fallback behavior are fixed for every unsupported lifecycle case.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

Local-notification permission, background, reboot, and scheduling semantics differ between iOS and Android and may differ across OS versions. The app cannot promise that the OS will deliver at an exact instant. This issue unblocks reminder domain design, `mob_notify` integration, permissions UX, QA, and release claims. It is the M00 proof behind the M06 forecasting-and-reminders milestone and the honest delivery language required by the constitution.

## Included

- Pin and test the exact `mob_notify` release resolved for Mob `0.7.20`; document its Elixir API, native configuration, entitlements/manifest entries, identifier format, payload limits, and platform-specific behavior.
- Invoke the plugin from the root `Mob.Screen` with its `Mob.Socket` through the M00 DeviceCommandBroker proof. Define typed schedule/cancel/permission commands, correlation IDs, acknowledgement/error messages, duplicate/timeout behavior, and root-screen restart recovery. The Phoenix LiveView socket is never passed to `MobNotify`.
- Exercise permission states (not asked, granted, denied, later revoked), schedule/edit/cancel, multiple vehicles, app foreground/background/terminated, device restart, low-power mode, timezone/DST change, clock change, and an app update.
- Determine OS scheduling limits and whether reminders must be rehydrated on launch. Specify stable notification IDs, per-vehicle cancellation, and deduplication.
- Define a no-notification fallback: the app always computes and displays due/overdue status locally; settings show that system delivery is unavailable or disabled; the user can change the reminder date or rely on in-app status.
- Define just-in-time permission copy. Never request notification permission on first launch before the user creates a vehicle and asks for a reminder.
- Record that mileage-based alerts are forecasts derived from driving samples; without telematics the app cannot know that actual mileage crossed a threshold in the background.

## Excluded

- APNs/FCM remote push, web/PWA notifications, a notification server, email/SMS, calendar integration, exact-alarm entitlement work, or a guarantee that the OS will deliver at an exact minute. Browser/PWA notification support requires a separate future ADR and issue set.

## Technical implementation contract

### Hypothesis

The `mob_notify` release resolved for Mob `0.7.20`, invoked exclusively from the root `Mob.Screen` with its `Mob.Socket` via the DeviceCommandBroker path proven in DOS-M00-002, can schedule, deliver, edit, and cancel OS-local notifications on physical iOS and Android devices across all required permission and lifecycle states — without any server, push subscription, or account — and every case it cannot support can be covered by an honest, approved in-app fallback in which due/overdue status is always computed and displayed locally.

### Timebox

Duration is assigned at refinement (estimates stay blank at bootstrap). If the timebox expires with lifecycle cells unproven, those cells are recorded as unverified with the OS-documented expectation noted (never conflated with verified behavior), and any product-required capability still unknown becomes a blocking decision at the DOS-M00-000 gate rather than an assumed capability.

### Alternatives considered

- APNs/FCM remote push or a notification server: rejected — master prompt §3 rule 8 forbids push infrastructure, device tokens, servers, and accounts in v1; these are explicitly excluded.
- Exact-alarm entitlement work (Android): excluded — the product does not promise exact-minute delivery; copy says "remind"/"estimate," never "monitor."
- A perpetual background GenServer or Oban for deadlines: rejected by §7 — mobile operating systems suspend normal processes; recompute happens on mutation and at foreground while the OS holds the scheduled requests.
- Calling `MobNotify` with a `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket` or from arbitrary LiveView processes: rejected — the root `Mob.Screen` is the only production caller because it owns `Mob.Socket`; the negative test from DOS-M00-002 AC-7 guards this.
- Email/SMS, calendar integration, web/PWA notifications: excluded; browser/PWA notification support requires a separate future ADR and issue set.

### Success and failure criteria

Success: acceptance criteria AC-1 through AC-5 pass — a complete recorded state matrix on both physical platforms; at least one reminder scheduled, delivered, edited, and canceled per platform where supported; approved honest fallbacks for unsupported/denied behavior; reminder intent surviving restart even when OS schedule reconstruction is required; and no server, push subscription, or online account anywhere in the flow. Failure: a product-required scheduling capability is absent on a platform with no honest fallback, or intent cannot survive restart — either finding becomes a blocking decision at the M00 gate and reshapes M06 reminder scope before feature commitment.

### Mandatory ADR and follow-ups

- Deliverable: the `mob_notify` capability matrix (per-platform, per-lifecycle-state verdicts distinguishing verified behavior from OS-documented expectation) plus the fixed fallback and permission copy. If the findings change the reminder architecture (for example mandatory rehydration on launch), that is recorded as an ADR-level decision feeding the M06 reminder design.
- Follow-ups on success: the matrix baselines the `NotificationAdapter` behaviour, `reminder_rules`/`scheduled_notifications` design (stable IDs, per-vehicle cancellation, deduplication), M02 permission-primer copy, and QA's lifecycle regression list.
- Follow-ups on failure/limits: each unsupported case gets a named fallback or blocking decision in the DOS-M00-000 limitation register with owner, user-facing fallback, and review date.
- Copy follow-up: the ratified language ("remind," "estimate," never "monitor"; forecast disclosure for mileage-based alerts) is handed to the M02 content contract.

## UX and content states

- Permission states: not asked (no prompt until the user creates a vehicle and asks for a reminder; just-in-time primer copy), granted, denied (non-blocking; no repeated nagging; settings show system delivery is disabled with a path to in-app status), and later revoked (detected and reflected in settings without data loss).
- No-notification fallback state: due/overdue status is always computed and displayed in-app; the user can change the reminder date or rely on in-app status.
- Notification content: contains the local vehicle nickname and due context but avoids sensitive free text on the lock screen.
- All copy says "remind" or "estimate," never "monitor"; mileage-based alerts are presented as forecasts from driving samples, not odometer readings.
- The user can disable reminders and inspect current permission/scheduling state at any time.

## Test plan

### Automated

- Typed command tests through the DeviceCommandBroker path: schedule/cancel/permission commands with correlation IDs, asserting acknowledgement/error handling, duplicate suppression, timeout behavior, and root-screen restart recovery (extending the DOS-M00-002 negative tests).
- Identifier discipline tests: stable notification IDs, per-vehicle cancellation, and deduplication logic exercised against multi-vehicle fixtures.
- Adapter isolation check: the proof code demonstrates that no call path hands a `Phoenix.LiveView.Socket` to `MobNotify`.

### Manual/device matrix

- Physical iOS and physical Android devices (models and OS versions recorded), covering the full state matrix: permission not asked/granted/denied/revoked; schedule/edit/cancel; multiple vehicles; foreground/background/terminated; device restart; low-power mode; timezone/DST change; clock change; app update in place.
- Demonstrate permission denial and revocation without loss of vehicle, history, or due-status functionality.
- Run timezone/DST and device-reboot scenarios; distinguish verified behavior from OS-documented expectation in the recorded matrix.
- Verify delivered notification content (nickname and due context; no sensitive free text on the lock screen) and capture screenshots/video per platform.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: OS scheduling limits (pending-notification caps, reboot behavior) force rehydration-on-launch architecture. Mitigation: FR-4 measures the limits; the finding is recorded as a design decision for M06 rather than discovered during feature work.
- Risk: platform divergence in permission revocation or low-power behavior yields an inconsistent product story. Mitigation: per-platform matrix cells with honest fallback copy per cell.
- Risk: delivery timing variance tempts exact-time promises. Mitigation: excluded exact-alarm work; copy fixed to "remind"/"estimate"; no exact-minute guarantee anywhere.
- Decision produced here: the capability matrix, fallback contract, permission copy, and identifier/deduplication rules.
- Open question (resolved by the proof): whether both platforms support edit-in-place for scheduled notifications or require cancel-and-reschedule via stable IDs.
