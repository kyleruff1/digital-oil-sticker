## Outcome

A data-coverage decision and small end-to-end proof establish which fields can be populated from NHTSA vPIC, which require other licensed/authoritative sources, and how the product behaves when oil interval, oil specification, or filter compatibility is absent.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

vPIC is appropriate for vehicle identity/decoding data, but it must not be presumed to supply manufacturer maintenance schedules, oil-brand recommendations, or filter fitment. Treating all of those as one dataset would create false precision and potential safety/liability problems. This issue unblocks schema, sourcing, ingestion, UX confidence labels, and realistic release scope. Its artifact is the factual foundation for the entire M03 data-acquisition milestone and informs the DOS-M00-004 persistence fixtures.

## Context and source of truth

## Included

- Define canonical entities and their evidence requirements: model year, make, model, vehicle/trim/series, engine/fuel/drivetrain attributes, manufacturer oil-change interval, severe-service interval, oil viscosity/specification, oil brand/product claim, filter brand/part fitment, effective dates, market, source URL/document, source revision, license, and retrieval time.
- Use `https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/` (or an officially offered bulk form where appropriate) for a small, cached, reproducible slice spanning several model years, makes, ambiguous trims, and missing fields. Preserve source identifiers and raw responses for traceability.
- Define confidence tiers such as verified exact configuration, verified model/engine family, general model guidance, and user-entered/unverified. Do not collapse these tiers into a single “compatible” boolean.
- Produce a source-gap matrix for manufacturer schedules, oil products, and filters. Each non-vPIC source must have authority, licensing/redistribution rights, update method, provenance fields, and conflict policy before its values ship.
- Define fallbacks: allow a user to create a custom vehicle or manually record manual-derived intervals/specifications; label the data as user-entered; keep it isolated from curated catalog claims.
- Define “major manufacturer,” supported market, rolling 30-model-year window, discontinued/renamed makes, and catalog refresh policy in testable terms.

## Excluded

- Scraping owner manuals or retailer fitment pages without permission, inferring filter compatibility from text similarity, representing brand marketing as manufacturer guidance, downloading the full catalog, or shipping production recommendations.

## Functional requirements

- FR-1: Canonical entities and their evidence requirements are defined for: model year, make, model, vehicle/trim/series, engine/fuel/drivetrain attributes, manufacturer oil-change interval, severe-service interval, oil viscosity/specification, oil brand/product claim, filter brand/part fitment, effective dates, market, source URL/document, source revision, license, and retrieval time.
- FR-2: A small, cached, reproducible slice is drawn from `https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/api/` (or an officially offered bulk form where appropriate), spanning several model years, makes, ambiguous trims, and missing fields; source identifiers and raw responses are preserved for traceability, and the import from the cached responses is deterministic.
- FR-3: Confidence tiers are defined — at minimum verified exact configuration, verified model/engine family, general model guidance, and user-entered/unverified — and are never collapsed into a single "compatible" boolean.
- FR-4: A source-gap matrix covers manufacturer schedules, oil products, and filters; every non-vPIC source row records authority, licensing/redistribution rights, update method, provenance fields, and conflict policy before its values may ship.
- FR-5: Fallbacks are defined: a user can create a custom vehicle or manually record manual-derived intervals/specifications; such data is labeled user-entered and kept isolated from curated catalog claims (and manual history is never relabeled as verified compatibility, per §9).
- FR-6: "Major manufacturer," supported market, the rolling 30-model-year window, discontinued/renamed makes, and the catalog refresh policy are defined in testable terms, starting from the §5 planning default (market: United States; for the 2026 baseline, 1997–2026 inclusive).
- FR-7: Every recommendation/fitment record in the proof is provenance-bearing and effective-dated; unknown is stored as unknown — not zero, a default interval, or an invented trim; source precedence is visible and deterministic, and conflicting sources remain inspectable.
- FR-8: Layering is explicit: raw source data, normalized catalog data, and user-entered overrides are separate layers, and UI language distinguishes "manufacturer guidance," "product-maker claim," and "your setting."

## Technical implementation contract

### Hypothesis

A small, cached vPIC slice plus a rigorously separated source-gap matrix can demonstrate end to end that: vehicle identity fields (year/make/model and VIN-coded attributes when submitted) are reliably populatable from vPIC; maintenance intervals, oil specifications/capacities, oil-product claims, and filter fitments are not populatable from vPIC and each requires an authoritative, licensed source with provenance and conflict policy; and the product can behave honestly (unknown/unsupported/user-entered states) wherever those facts are absent — establishing a truthful, testable coverage contract before any acquisition or ingestion ticket opens.

### Timebox

Duration is assigned at refinement (estimates stay blank at bootstrap). If the timebox expires before every matrix cell is evidenced, unproven cells are recorded as open gaps that keep the corresponding M03 acquisition tickets blocked; the ratification of the coverage definitions (FR-6) may not proceed on assumed coverage.

### Alternatives considered

- Treating vPIC as a one-stop dataset for identity plus maintenance/oil/filter facts: rejected — vPIC has no engine-oil interval, viscosity, capacity, oil-product, or filter-fitment fields (§4.4); presuming otherwise creates false precision and safety/liability exposure.
- Embedding the vPIC standalone downloads to build the year/make/model selector: constrained by §4.4 — those downloads are explicitly limited to VIN-decoding functionality and do not replace catalog API calls.
- Scraping owner manuals or retailer fitment pages without permission, or inferring filter compatibility from text similarity: rejected and explicitly excluded; the documented-facts and permission-needed lanes of §4.4 govern instead.
- Buying commercial data before measuring free coverage: deferred by the free-first, field-by-field strategy — M03 must investigate the free candidate matrix (FuelEconomy.gov, EPA certification data, API EOLCS directory, supplier application files) before opening a commercial procurement ticket.
- A single "compatible" boolean instead of confidence tiers: rejected — tiers are mandatory and never collapsed (FR-3, §9).

### Success and failure criteria

Success: acceptance criteria AC-1 through AC-6 pass — deterministic import from cached official responses; a field/source matrix explicitly showing vPIC coverage does not imply maintenance, oil, or filter coverage; per-fact traceability to source record and revision; approved storage and UX behavior for missing/ambiguous/conflicting cases; ratified 30-year/major-manufacturer/market definitions and refresh rule; and zero unlicensed production data in the repository or any distributable artifact. Failure: the slice cannot be imported deterministically, a required entity lacks any lawful candidate source (making the coverage claim unratifiable), or legal review rejects the redistribution/attribution assumptions — each failure blocks the dependent M03 tickets and returns to the M00 gate as a scope decision.

### Mandatory ADR and follow-ups

- Deliverable: the data-coverage decision record — canonical entity definitions with evidence requirements, confidence tiers, the source-gap matrix, fallback rules, and the ratified 30-year/major-manufacturer/market/refresh definitions. This is the data-boundary ADR that M03 executes against.
- Legal/data-owner review of redistribution and attribution assumptions is a mandatory gate: it must complete before any full import ticket is unblocked.
- Follow-ups on success: hand the entity/evidence model and slice fixtures to DOS-M00-004 (fixture realism) and to M03's source-register, acquisition, and ingestion tickets; hand the confidence tiers and unknown/conflict UX contracts to M02.
- Follow-ups on gaps: each unfilled matrix cell becomes an M03 investigation or procurement candidate under the three-lane approval model; nothing ships from an ambiguous source until reviewed.

## UX and content states

The proof fixes honest content behavior for the recommendation surface: unknown is displayed as unknown or unsupported — never a generic value, default interval, or invented trim; ambiguous configurations ask the user to confirm/manual-enter instead of guessing; conflicting sources produce no automatic product recommendation while both facts remain inspectable; user-entered data is always labeled as such and never mingled with curated claims. UI language distinguishes "manufacturer guidance," "product-maker claim," and "your setting," consistent with §6 required terminology ("Source unavailable," "Exact configuration not verified"). Battery-electric vehicles may appear for honest identification with "engine oil service not applicable."

## Test plan

### Automated

- Deterministic rebuild: rerun the slice import from the cached fixtures and compare hashes/row counts against the recorded baseline.
- Traceability check: every normalized fact in the slice resolves to a preserved source identifier, raw response, source revision, and retrieval time.
- Unknown-preservation check: fields absent in the source remain null/unknown after import — never zero, a default, or an inferred value.
- License hygiene check: the proof repository and any built artifact contain only cached official vPIC responses and licensed/synthetic examples; no unlicensed production data.

### Manual/device matrix

- Review the five normalized examples required by the catalog: one exact match, one ambiguous variant, one missing recommendation, one conflicting recommendation, and one user-entered override — each with its storage form and intended UX behavior.
- Walk the source-gap matrix with data and product reviewers: each non-vPIC source row shows authority, licensing/redistribution rights, update method, provenance fields, and conflict policy.
- Complete legal/data-owner review for redistribution and attribution assumptions before any full import ticket is unblocked.
- Ratification review of the "major manufacturer," market, rolling-window, discontinued/renamed-make, and refresh-policy definitions in testable terms.
- No device matrix applies — this proof runs in the build/import pipeline context, not on phones; its architecture constraint (vPIC never called during the user's offline vehicle search) is verified by design review.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: vPIC's trim/engine enumeration gaps make "exact configuration" rarer than hoped, shrinking the verified tier. Mitigation: confidence tiers and the §5 support statuses keep identity support separate from recommendation support; the honest fallback path is first-class.
- Risk: legal review rejects a redistribution assumption late. Mitigation: the review is a mandatory gate before any full import ticket is unblocked; ambiguous sources stay out of shipped catalogs.
- Risk: conflicting sources (for example OEM manual vs. product claim) have no resolution policy. Mitigation: FR-4 requires a conflict policy per source and §9's `conflict` result keeps both facts inspectable with no automatic recommendation.
- Decision produced here: the ratified coverage contract (30-year window, major-manufacturer and market definitions, refresh rule), the entity/evidence model, the confidence tiers, and the source-gap matrix.
- Open question (resolved by M03, framed here): which free candidates (FuelEconomy.gov, EPA certification files, API EOLCS, supplier application guides) actually cover each gap cell, and what remains for permission-based or procured acquisition.
