> **RESCOPED FOR BROWSER MVP — 2026-08-01.** [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) established the browser-first MVP. This card is retained with the same identity; its Outcome and functional requirements below are the rescoped spec. The pre-pivot spec is preserved at the bottom under "Superseded pre-pivot spec (historical record)". Disposition per [PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md](../../docs/governance/PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md#issue-by-issue-classification): _Delete the OS permission-prompt and notification-delivery UX; the MVP specifies in-app due/overdue presentation only, plus honest copy that nothing arrives while the app is closed._

## Outcome

An implementation-ready specification for **in-app due and overdue presentation** on the browser MVP. Due status is a pure function of the user's own client-held records and the server-resolved catalog interval; it is recomputed on every hydration and after every acknowledged mutation, rendered on the vehicle card, dashboard, and any surface that references reminder state, and is accompanied by honest copy that the app does not alert the user while it is closed.

The deliverable specifies the due-state model (states, thresholds, tie-breaks), where and when it is computed, how it survives reconnect and multi-tab writes, its accessibility contract for browser assistive technology, and the in-app content rules that satisfy the amended INV-17 (no background alerting) and INV-25 (honest storage-loss messaging) together. OS local notifications, permission prompts, notification payload rules, deep-link contracts, and reconciliation of scheduled OS notifications are explicitly out of scope for the MVP and are retained in the pre-pivot section below for the revival trigger recorded in [ADR-0004 §"Trigger conditions"](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md#trigger-conditions-that-revive-the-native-track).

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

The 2026-08-01 pivot removed OS local notifications from the MVP surface. Something must still tell the user that a change is due — that promise is the entire product — and the only mechanism left is what the app renders while the user is looking at it. This card is what makes that renderable behavior honest, testable, and consistent across every surface that shows reminder state.

It also enforces the amended INV-17 at the UX level: **the app does not notify the user when it is closed**, and no copy, control, or affordance may imply otherwise. Getting that wrong on a hosted URL — where users implicitly expect a server to remember and reach them — is the single most likely place the pivot could break the product's honesty promise (INV-5), so the language and empty/loading/loss states are as load-bearing as the arithmetic.

Downstream: this spec is a consumer of DOS-M02-005 (card-level reminder-state field) and DOS-M02-007 (forecast confidence and recomputation triggers), and a driver for the browser platform work — DOS-M09-003 (hydration and mutation round trip; due-state must recompute deterministically on every remount) and DOS-M09-004 (pre-hydration, storage-denied, quota, eviction UX; the neutral/loss states apply to due-state rendering too). It is a source for DOS-M06-005 and DOS-M06-007 (the M06 rescope that carries the "desired reminder plan" as a pure domain output and the in-app reminder preferences / due-state correctness gate).

## Included

- Define the **due-state model** as a small, closed enum with explicit thresholds and tie-breaks: `unknown_pre_hydration`, `unknown_no_data`, `on_track`, `due_soon`, `due_now`, `overdue`, `not_applicable` (INV-11 `not_applicable`), and `stale_estimate` (forecast confidence too low per DOS-M02-007). Every state has a specified visual treatment, copy template, and screen-reader label.
- Specify **when** due-state is computed: on hydrate (INV-24.1), after each acknowledged mutation (INV-24.2), on catalog-query completion when the interval side changes, on reconnect (INV-7), and on multi-tab notification of a newer `seq` (INV-24.7). Recomputation MUST NOT run against un-hydrated assigns.
- Specify **where** due-state renders: vehicle card (per DOS-M02-005), dashboard summary, per-vehicle detail view, and any list/aggregate that the IA (DOS-M02-001) exposes. All surfaces read the same computed state; no surface computes its own.
- Specify the **in-app content contract**: plain-language copy that the app does not notify the user when it is closed, reachable at any time (not only first visit), that the user's data lives in this browser (INV-25, INV-21), and that estimates are estimates (INV-20). A prohibited-words list is enforced (see FR-6).
- Specify the **preferences surface** replacing the OS-permission flow: an in-app "reminder preferences" panel offering per-vehicle lead-time choices (in days/miles/km, subject to unit prefs) that shift the boundary between `on_track` and `due_soon`. The panel MUST NOT offer a "send me a notification" control, MUST NOT collect an email or phone, and MUST NOT expose a browser permission prompt.
- Specify the **pre-hydration, storage-loss, and storage-unavailable rendering** for every due-state surface: the surface renders `unknown_pre_hydration` (never `on_track`, never an empty "no reminders" state) until hydration resolves, and adopts the DOS-M09-004 loss/denied/unavailable state when applicable — the due badge disappears with an explicit honest reason, never silently.
- Specify the **connectivity-degraded rendering**: when the catalog side of the computation is unreachable (INV-7), the surface shows the honest connection state and any due-state derived purely from client-held records (e.g., a user-entered calendar reminder) remains readable; catalog-dependent thresholds render as `stale_estimate` with the reason.
- Specify **accessibility**: due-state changes announce through a live region; the badge is not color-only (INV-11 rule inherited via DOS-M02-002); focus and keyboard order are defined for the preferences panel; copy is usable at 200% zoom and at 320 CSS px reflow.

Deferred per amended INV-17 / INV-19 and ADR-0004 §Consequences.Lost; requires its own ADR and constitution revision.
- Email or SMS reminders, an accounts/login layer, or any cross-device sync. Prohibited scope for the MVP (INV-3, INV-19).
- The card-level `reminder_state` field definition (owned by DOS-M02-005), the forecast confidence model (owned by DOS-M02-007), event editing that triggers recomputation (owned by DOS-M02-006), and the reminder-plan domain function itself (owned by the rescoped DOS-M06-005). This card specifies how those outputs are rendered and when they recompute, not how they are calculated.

All were retired for the MVP by ADR-0004 §Consequences.Lost and are preserved under their original names in the historical section only.

## Functional requirements

Each FR below addresses the sweep finding that the pre-pivot card was authored against OS-scheduled local notifications delivered through the pre-pivot OS-notification bridge (contradicting ADR-0004 §Consequences.Lost lines 420–421 and Constitution 2.0.0's amended INV-17; the historical section below preserves the exact retired names). Where an original FR is retired, that is stated explicitly rather than paraphrased away.

- **FR-1 (replaces pre-pivot FR-1):** The per-vehicle reminder-preference surface offers three intent shapes, all pure client-side settings held in IndexedDB per INV-23: (a) off (no reminder threshold; `not_applicable` renders), (b) lead relative to the forecast-derived due date (owned by DOS-M02-007) with the lead expressed in days and, where the interval is mileage-driven, in miles/km subject to unit prefs, and (c) a user-selected calendar date. The surface displays the underlying target (mileage/date) and its confidence label (INV-20, DOS-M02-007). The lead-time input is bounded only by the client-side computation; there is no OS scheduling limit to enforce.
- **FR-2 (retires pre-pivot FR-2):** No OS permission is requested. Ever. The application MUST NOT call `Notification.requestPermission()`, MUST NOT register a service worker for push, and MUST NOT display a browser permission prompt on any code path in the MVP (INV-17 amended, INV-19). The pre-pivot enumeration of iOS/Android permission states (granted, denied, permanently-denied/settings-required, revoked, scheduling-limit-reached, OS-notification scheduling-error) is retired as unmeetable in a browser and preserved in the historical section only under its original names.
- **FR-3 (replaces pre-pivot FR-3):** The due-state model is a closed enum — `unknown_pre_hydration`, `unknown_no_data`, `on_track`, `due_soon`, `due_now`, `overdue`, `not_applicable`, `stale_estimate` — with a specified visual treatment, copy template, and screen-reader label per state. Every surface (vehicle card, dashboard, per-vehicle detail, any aggregate) reads the same computed state; no surface derives its own. Notification title/body privacy, tap/deep-link behavior, and stale-notification handling from the pre-pivot FR-3 are retired — there are no notifications to write copy for, and links in the app are ordinary in-app navigation covered by DOS-M02-001.
- **FR-4 (replaces pre-pivot FR-4):** Due-state is recomputed at exactly these moments: (a) on socket join, after `local_store:hydrate` sets assigns (INV-24.1); (b) after each acknowledged `local_store:ack` for a mutation touching vehicles/events/readings/reminders (INV-24.2); (c) on catalog-query completion when the resolved interval or oil-requirement version changes; (d) on reconnect after `phx-disconnected`, which re-runs hydration (INV-7); (e) on a `BroadcastChannel` message from another tab indicating a newer `seq` (INV-24.7). Recomputation MUST NOT run against un-hydrated assigns; the render for that gap is `unknown_pre_hydration`. Reconciliation of OS-scheduled notifications from the pre-pivot FR-4 is retired — there are no platform schedules to reconcile.
- **FR-5 (replaces pre-pivot FR-5):** In-app copy is the entire delivery surface. It MUST include, at first visit and reachable at all times from the reminder-preferences panel and the dashboard, an honest statement that the app does not notify the user when it is closed, that estimates are estimates (INV-20), and that data lives in this browser (INV-21, INV-25). The pre-pivot FR-5's "in-app attention list/badge as universal fallback" survives, but is no longer a fallback — it is the whole promise.
- **FR-6 (replaces pre-pivot FR-6):** The preferences surface exposes only client-side controls: per-vehicle enable/disable, per-vehicle lead-time (days and, where applicable, miles/km), and global units/time-zone (consumed from DOS-M02-005/007 prefs). It MUST NOT expose a "send test notification", "notify me by email", "notify me by push", or "install to receive alerts" control. Prohibited words in this UI, enforced by a copy lint: "notify", "notification", "push", "alert (you)", "we'll remind you", "sends you", "text you", "email you", "backup", "synced". Required words on the reminder-preferences panel and on the dashboard's reminder surface: "in this browser", "only when the app is open", "estimate". The pre-pivot FR-6's quiet-time / exact-alarm / platform-review-gated test notification is retired.
- **FR-7 (retained, restated):** Reminder intent in browser storage (IndexedDB `reminders` store per ADR-0004 §"Storage layout") is authoritative for the client; there is no server-side projection to reconcile, so the intent-vs-projection contract collapses to intent-vs-render. Server assigns hold the intent only for the socket lifetime and are rebuilt from the client on every hydrate (INV-24.1).
- **FR-8 (retained, restated):** A user who has any reason to expect due-state — whether because of degraded connectivity, storage denial, or a browser permission the app has never asked for — retains complete in-app visibility of what the client can compute. Degraded rendering never hides an overdue state; it labels it with the honest reason (`stale_estimate`, `unknown_no_data`, `storage_unavailable`).
- **FR-9 (retires pre-pivot FR-9):** There are no lock-screen or system-tray notifications to privacy-scope. The equivalent risk in a browser is the page title: the app MUST NOT put a VIN, mileage value, nickname beyond what the user typed, or free-form note into `document.title`. If the title reflects due-state at all (e.g., a bracketed count), it uses only counts and status words.
- **FR-10 (retires pre-pivot FR-10):** No tap-to-stale-notification flow exists to handle. Ordinary in-app navigation to a vehicle whose forecast has since changed follows the DOS-M02-001 stale-link rules; the vehicle detail view surfaces the current due-state via FR-3, with the change reason noted where DOS-M02-007 supplies it.
- **FR-11 (retained, restated):** Time calculations use the persisted IANA time zone from the client `prefs` store (ADR-0004 §"Storage layout"). Any DST transition that would shift `due_soon` to `due_now` or `overdue` around midnight is handled by computing thresholds in the user's local calendar, not in UTC, so the state visible on the vehicle card matches the user's calendar day.
- **FR-12 (new, per INV-26):** Due-state computation MUST NOT send personal selectors to the server. The catalog side of the comparison (interval, oil requirements, filter fitment) is resolved through the impersonal catalog-query surface owned by DOS-M09-005 using only the vehicle configuration selectors permitted there; the personal side (odometer readings, service history, user-entered dates) never leaves assigns or IndexedDB. This is verified by the log-scan test defined in Test plan.
- **FR-13 (new, per INV-24.3 and INV-24.7):** Every due-state surface renders `unknown_pre_hydration` until hydration resolves — never `on_track`, never a "no reminders" empty state, never `overdue` for zero. On multi-tab notification of a newer `seq`, the surface re-reads its due-state from freshly hydrated assigns and, if the visible state changes, announces the change through a live region so a screen-reader user is not surprised.
- **FR-14 (new, per INV-25):** The reminder-preferences panel exposes the honest storage-loss message (INV-25) reachable at any time, not only at first visit, and the copy is coordinated with DOS-M09-004 so a user reads one voice across surfaces. A detected empty-or-evicted store renders the storage-loss variant of the preferences panel, not a "let's set up your first reminder" flow.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

The surface is composed from DOS-M02-002 primitives — status badge, live-region announcement, focus-visible controls, WCAG-conformant target sizes — and from DOS-M02-005's vehicle-card contract. Due-state is a pure function of hydrated assigns plus a resolved catalog response; it lives in a single `DigitalOilSticker.Reminders.DueState` module in the domain layer (client-agnostic per ADR-0004 §Decision.6). The LiveView calls this module during the render pipeline and after receiving each of the FR-4 events; no view template computes state inline. The **`NotificationAdapter` behaviour, `DeviceCommandBroker`, and any OS-notification plugin bridge are not part of the browser MVP** and MUST NOT be introduced by this card (see historical section for the retired pre-pivot integration names). If DOS-M01-002's rescope keeps a domain-level `ReminderPlan` port for future client reuse, this card consumes its output and does not care where it came from.

### Data and persistence

Reminder intent lives in the IndexedDB `reminders` object store (ADR-0004 §"Storage layout"): `reminder_id` (UUIDv4, client-generated), `vehicle_id`, intent shape (`off` | `relative_to_forecast` | `calendar_date`), lead value/unit, calendar date where applicable, and enabled flag. Multi-vehicle-safe from the first write per INV-18. Server-side there is **no** `scheduled_notifications` table, **no** `reminder_rules` table, and **no** personal row of any kind (INV-23, ADR-0004 §Decision.4). Intent held in `socket.assigns.reminders` for the socket lifetime only; discarded on socket close; re-hydrated on every remount (INV-24.1). Times: UTC instants plus the IANA time zone from `prefs`, exactly as ADR-0004 §"Storage layout" specifies (FR-11).

### Interfaces and events

The client-side JS hook is the existing `LocalStore` hook from ADR-0004 §"The client-storage + LiveView hydration protocol". This card introduces **no** new hook, **no** new plugin, and **no** new socket channel. LiveView events consumed: `local_store:hydrate` (recompute after assigns fill), `local_store:ack` for reminder/vehicle/event/reading mutations (recompute after commit), `phx-disconnected` → `reconnected` (recompute after the hydration re-run), and a `BroadcastChannel("dos_local_store")` message forwarded through the hook (recompute after a fresh hydrate). Catalog resolution rides the DOS-M09-005 impersonal query surface with the vehicle configuration selectors DOS-M02-005 already carries in assigns.

### Failure and recovery behavior

Every failure state has a specified surface: **hydration timeout** (5 000 ms deadline per ADR-0004) → surface stays in `unknown_pre_hydration` and adopts DOS-M09-004's `:storage_unavailable` variant of the preferences panel; **quota-exceeded** on saving a reminder-intent write → FR-1 write path uses DOS-M09-004's quota copy and does not roll the reminder intent forward silently; **catalog query failure** → intervals render as `stale_estimate` with the honest connection reason (INV-7), and any calendar-date reminder still computes because it depends on no catalog fetch; **multi-tab stale write** → the losing tab re-hydrates and announces the change (FR-13). No failure state loses reminder intent, because reminder intent lives in IndexedDB and the server is not the system of record. There are no OS schedules to drift, no OS-notification scheduling-error state to handle, and no reboot-persistence concern — the historical section preserves those under their original pre-pivot names, they are not part of the MVP.

### Security and privacy

There is no lock-screen surface, no notification payload, no deep-link token, and no OS permission prompt to design. The privacy risks unique to the browser are: (a) the page title (`document.title`) — FR-9 restricts what may appear there; (b) the URL — the router (DOS-M02-001) never places personal values in path or query, and this card never adds any; (c) the LiveView socket payload — reminder intent flows client→server on hydration only, held in assigns only, never persisted, never logged (INV-4, INV-23, ADR-0004 §Enforcement); (d) the catalog-query surface — FR-12 forbids personal selectors on it (INV-26). No third-party push service, no VAPID keys, no server-held subscription — Web Push is deferred per amended INV-17.

### Accessibility and performance

The due-state badge is not color-only, uses text and iconography per DOS-M02-002, and announces changes through a live region (FR-13). The preferences panel is keyboard-operable with a visible focus indicator, has target sizes ≥ 24×24 CSS px (≥ 44×44 where touch is primary), renders at 200% zoom and at 320 CSS px reflow, and its copy is readable to screen readers in the order sighted users see it. Recomputation is a pure function call and completes well under the constitution §9 hydration budget (p95 ≤ 300 ms end-to-end); it MUST NOT block the LiveView reply, so the after-mutation recompute runs before the reply is sent and the after-hydration recompute rides the first render post-hydrate.

## UX and content states

- **Due-state states (FR-3):** `unknown_pre_hydration` (skeleton, no icon, no words that assert status); `unknown_no_data` (user has vehicle but no reading/interval — plain-language prompt to log the last change); `on_track` (badge, no numeric urgency); `due_soon` (badge, lead-time reason surfaced); `due_now` (elevated badge, plain instruction); `overdue` (elevated badge with the calendar/mileage delta and INV-20 estimate framing); `not_applicable` (INV-11 semantic — e.g., BEV — plainly stated, no oil-plan invented); `stale_estimate` (DOS-M02-007 low-confidence or catalog unreachable — badge dimmed, reason surfaced, INV-15/INV-20 respected).
- **Preferences states (FR-1, FR-6):** off; relative-to-forecast with a valid forecast; relative-to-forecast with no valid forecast (control disabled with reason); calendar-date; edited-but-unacked (FR-4/INV-24.2 pending write with the DOS-M09-004 "not saved to this browser" affordance if the ack times out).
- **Loss/degradation states (FR-8, FR-13, FR-14):** `hydrating` (surface reads `unknown_pre_hydration`); `storage_unavailable` (surface hidden, preferences panel adopts the DOS-M09-004 variant); `data_missing` (INV-24.8 detected — surface shows the honest loss message, offers import); `connection_down` (INV-7 — catalog-dependent thresholds render `stale_estimate`; client-only calendar reminders still compute).
- **Content rules (FR-5, FR-6):** prohibited words enforced by copy lint; required words on the preferences panel and dashboard; "Estimated" framing on every numeric urgency claim (INV-20); "Stored in this browser" as the required framing for user data (INV-21); the honest-loss message reachable at any time (INV-25).

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- **Risk (highest for this card):** a hosted URL implicitly reads to users as "the server will remember and reach me." FR-5 / FR-6 / FR-14 copy discipline is the entire mitigation, and the copy lint is a release gate, not a nicety. Failure mode: a well-meaning caption edit reintroduces "we'll remind you", the lint catches it in CI; a well-meaning designer adds a bell icon, the review catches it against FR-6's prohibited-affordance rule.
- **Risk:** hydration latency spikes on a slow network stretch `unknown_pre_hydration` visibly. Mitigation: constitution §9 provisional hydration budget (p95 ≤ 300 ms) plus the honest-degradation coverage row; the skeleton is designed to be non-alarming rather than hidden. This is a DOS-M09-003 concern surfaced here because it changes what a due-state surface looks like.
- **Risk:** multi-tab stale writes race a due-state read. Mitigation: FR-13 / AC-7 re-hydrate + announce; the compare-and-set on `meta.seq` in ADR-0004 §"Multi-tab consistency" prevents the corruption case, this card handles the surprise case.
- **Risk:** a future PWA/Web Push decision (DOS-M09-010) revives some background alerting. Handling: any revival requires an ADR and a constitution revision (amended INV-17), at which point this card's FRs are re-opened; nothing here silently prepares for it, and no code path is left in "off but ready".
- **Open question:** whether the reminder-preferences panel should offer a "remind me tomorrow when I open the app" nudge for `due_now` items — a purely client-side snooze with no infrastructure. Deferred to a follow-up on this card once the base surface ships; the option is recorded here so the panel's information architecture leaves room.
- **Decision to record:** the honest storage-loss message (INV-25) is authored **once** with DOS-M09-004 and referenced from the reminder-preferences panel, so a voice change does not fork.
- **Decision to record:** no "test notification" ships. Retired outright — there is nothing to test, and the pre-pivot's platform-review-gated test notification is preserved only in the historical section.
- **Decision to record:** the pre-pivot card body is preserved verbatim below as the evidence a future ADR-0005 would re-adopt under the [ADR-0004 §"Trigger conditions"](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md#trigger-conditions-that-revive-the-native-track) revival path. It is not paraphrased or truncated, because the value of retaining it is exactly that it stays implementation-ready for the eventual re-entry.

### Pre-pivot Outcome

An implementation-ready reminder flow converts forecast or user-selected dates into local reminder intent, requests platform permission at the right moment, and remains useful when delivery is denied, unsupported, late, or impossible.

The deliverable specifies per-vehicle reminder choices, permission timing and every proven platform permission state, notification content/targeting/deep-link behavior, reconciliation rules, the in-app fallback surface, and global/per-vehicle controls — the complete UX contract for local reminders.

### Pre-pivot Why this matters and what it unblocks

Notification UX must reflect proven platform behavior instead of promising exact background monitoring. This issue unblocks the native adapter, scheduling rules, settings, notification deep links, and permission QA.

It also enforces §3 rule 8 (local notifications only in v1) at the UX level and keeps the product honest: the OS controls delivery timing, mileage reminders are estimates, and denial never breaks the product.

### Pre-pivot Context and source of truth

### Pre-pivot Technical implementation contract

#### Pre-pivot Components and ownership boundaries

The flow spans reminder setup (per-vehicle), `SettingsLive` (global controls), `PermissionPrimer`, and notification-status surfaces, composed from DOS-M02-002 components. Reminder intent and reconciliation are owned by `DigitalOilSticker.Reminders` behind the `NotificationAdapter` behaviour; actual scheduling crosses the `DeviceCommandBroker`/root `Mob.Screen` boundary (the only path to `mob_notify`, which requires `Mob.Socket` per §4.1). The UI consumes typed permission/scheduling states; no screen calls the plugin or interprets platform errors directly.

#### Pre-pivot Data and persistence

`reminder_rules` stores intent (UUID, vehicle ID, reminder kind, lead value/unit, preferred local time, enabled flag — multi-vehicle-safe from the first migration). `scheduled_notifications` stores projections (stable notification ID primary key, vehicle/cycle/rule IDs, desired and scheduled instants, due-date snapshot, payload version, platform state, last error/reconciliation time). Intent is authoritative; platform schedules are rebuildable from it (FR-7). Times persist as UTC instants plus the IANA time zone used to derive local notification time (§8).

#### Pre-pivot Interfaces and events

Reconciliation consumes `forecast_changed`, vehicle archive/delete effects (per DOS-M02-005), and app-launch/foreground comparisons, and emits `reminders_reconciled`. The spec defines the desired-vs-actual comparison semantics (create/update/cancel per stable notification ID), duplicate-prevention via stable IDs, and the deep-link payload contract (stable local IDs only, validated before navigation per DOS-M02-001's stale-link rule). Scheduling operations are correlated command/acknowledgement pairs through the broker with redacted acknowledgements.

#### Pre-pivot Failure and recovery behavior

Every proven failure state has UX: denied (in-app fallback with non-coercive re-enable guidance), permanently denied/settings-required (settings deep-link guidance), revoked (detected at reconciliation; status shown), scheduling-limit-reached (prioritization/explanation per the M00-005 proof), `mob_notify` scheduling errors (recorded in `scheduled_notifications.last_error`, surfaced in troubleshooting/status), reboot/relaunch drift (launch-time reconciliation). Stale notifications open a safe current-state view (FR-10). No failure state loses reminder intent; projections are rebuilt from intent.

#### Pre-pivot Security and privacy

Lock-screen content is minimized: vehicle nickname and estimate context only — no free-form notes, oil history, odometer values, or VIN (FR-9). Deep links carry stable local IDs, never personal data. No remote push infrastructure, tokens, or accounts exist (§3 rule 8). Permission prompts are honest and non-repetitive; coercive re-prompting is excluded. All scheduling data stays on-device.

#### Pre-pivot Accessibility and performance

Permission explanations, status surfaces, and the attention list follow the DOS-M02-002 contract (non-color-only status, screen-reader announcements, 200% text, target sizes). Reconciliation runs synchronously/on a supervised foreground task at mutation and foreground (§7 — no background daemon); the spec defines any visible reconciliation status as bounded. Copy avoids technical jargon: delivery-timing and estimate caveats are plain language.

### Pre-pivot UX and content states

- Setup states: reminders off, relative-to-estimated/due-date (only where a forecast exists per DOS-M02-007), user-selected calendar date, lead-time configuration within proven OS limits, underlying target mileage/date display.
- Permission states: pre-permission explanation (iOS/Android variants), granted, denied, permanently denied/settings-required, revoked, scheduling-limit-reached, scheduling-error.
- Notification states: scheduled (with status visibility), delivered, stale (post-change), tapped → current-state view with changed-status explanation.
- Fallback states: in-app attention list/badge, vehicle due state, denied/unsupported full-visibility mode with delivery-limitation explanation.
- Controls: global toggle/status, per-vehicle rules, quiet-time (only if supported, without exact-alarm claims), troubleshooting/status view, optional safe test notification (platform-review-gated).
- Content rules: "estimated" framing per §6; OS-controls-delivery caveat; nickname-plus-context payload template with privacy limits.
