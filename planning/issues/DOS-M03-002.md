## Outcome
A versioned data dictionary defines every catalog entity, null meaning, unit, enumeration, source ranking, stable identifier, and provenance rule before adapters are implemented.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks
Without this contract each feed will invent incompatible vehicle keys and semantics. This issue establishes the common target for all adapters, crosswalks, repositories, UI labels, and QA. It blocks every other numbered M03 issue and DOS-M04-002 (the physical catalog schema), and it operationalizes non-negotiable rule 4 (provenance for every fact) and the requirement that missing data is shown as unknown or unsupported, never coerced into a fabricated value.

## Context and source of truth
- Master prompt section 8 "Database and data-contract baseline": the `CatalogRepo` table baseline this dictionary must cover (`catalog_metadata`, `data_sources`, `makes`, `models`, `vehicle_configurations`, `maintenance_schedules`, `oil_requirements`, `oil_brands`/`oil_products`/`oil_product_claims`, `filter_brands`/`filter_products`/`filter_fitments`, `aliases`, optional FTS tables), including "Unknown is nullable, not false" for `vehicle_configurations`.
- Master prompt section 5 "Coverage contract to freeze in M00": U.S. market, rolling 30-model-year window, vehicle classes, support statuses (`identity_only`, `schedule_supported`, `full_product_supported`, `not_applicable`, `unsupported`).
- Master prompt section 4.4 and section 11.2 "Research-backed source policy": vPIC absence means unknown, not a negative assertion; no "manufacturer recommended" claim without OEM-resolving field-level provenance; source precedence must respect licensed-provider authority.
- Master prompt section 6 "Required terminology": distinct oil-life monitor, calendar interval, mileage interval, normal service, severe service; provenance identifiers on every recommendation detail view.
- Section 11.2 catalog entry for DOS-M03-002 (this issue) and the required dependency order for GitHub Projects (vocabulary approval precedes DOS-M03-003 implementation).

## Included
- Define `unknown`, `not_applicable`, `not_provided`, and `conflicting` as distinguishable states where the distinction affects safety or display; never coerce missing values to zero, `false`, empty text, or "not compatible."
- Define canonical units: miles as integer miles; months as positive integer calendar months; capacity stored as decimal liters plus source unit/value; displacement as integer cubic centimeters; timestamps in UTC ISO-8601; user-facing conversion is presentation only.
- Stable catalog IDs use UUIDv5 (or an equivalently specified namespaced digest) from immutable source namespace + provider record key. If a source lacks an immutable key, use a documented canonical natural key and normalization version. Never derive stable IDs from row order, SQLite rowid, display spelling, or current catalog version.
- Define source precedence by field, not whole record: licensed OEM schedule/fluids provider; explicit supplier application; standards directory; vPIC identity; user override. Conflicts are retained and surfaced to QA rather than silently won by last import.
- Define the rolling scope as model years `build_calendar_year - 29` through `build_calendar_year`, inclusive. A separately configured future-model-year lane may stage data but is excluded from the advertised 30-year coverage until product approval.
- Define `major make` by a committed allowlist with business owner and review date. Do not infer "major" from vPIC row counts because vPIC includes many non-consumer and specialty manufacturers.

## Excluded
- Physical schema and migrations (M04).
- Choosing UI copy beyond required provenance labels.
- Joining records based only on fuzzy names.

## Functional requirements
- FR-1: The data dictionary enumerates every catalog entity and every adapter output field with type, unit, null semantics, enumeration values, and provenance requirements, covering all proposed M04 catalog columns.
- FR-2: The four missing-value states `unknown`, `not_applicable`, `not_provided`, and `conflicting` are defined with usage rules wherever the distinction affects safety or display, and the dictionary forbids coercing missing values to zero, `false`, empty text, or "not compatible."
- FR-3: Canonical units are fixed exactly as: integer miles; positive integer calendar months; capacity as decimal liters plus preserved source unit/value; displacement as integer cubic centimeters; UTC ISO-8601 timestamps; presentation-only conversion.
- FR-4: The stable-ID specification (UUIDv5 or an equivalently specified namespaced digest over immutable source namespace + provider record key; documented canonical natural key + normalization version when no immutable key exists) is committed, with an explicit prohibition on deriving IDs from row order, SQLite rowid, display spelling, or current catalog version.
- FR-5: Field-level source precedence is specified in the order: licensed OEM schedule/fluids provider; explicit supplier application; standards directory; vPIC identity; user override — and the conflict rule (retain and surface to QA, never silent last-import wins) is normative.
- FR-6: The rolling scope formula (`build_calendar_year - 29` through `build_calendar_year`, inclusive) and the future-model-year staging lane (excluded from advertised 30-year coverage until product approval) are specified.
- FR-7: The `major make` allowlist exists with business owner and review date, and the dictionary documents why vPIC row counts must not define "major."
- FR-8: Versioned schemas (JSON Schema or equivalent) exist for normalized intermediate records and final catalog records.
- FR-9: Every normalized record carries `_provenance`: `source_key`, `source_version`, `source_record_key`, `retrieved_at`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `raw_object_sha256`, `transform_version`, `license_version`, and optional `field_path`.
- FR-10: Crosswalk decisions carry `crosswalk_status` (`exact`, `qualified`, `ambiguous`, `rejected`, `manual_review`), `method`, `score` only when a documented scoring system exists, reviewer/evidence, and timestamps.
- FR-11: Service code `engine_oil_and_filter` is defined now and is not overloaded with inspections or other fluids.

## Technical implementation contract
### Components and ownership boundaries
- Data dictionary document (versioned, e.g., `docs/data/catalog-data-dictionary.md`): owned by the data workstream with product/data/engineering reviewer sign-off.
- Versioned JSON Schema (or equivalent) files for normalized intermediate records and final catalog records: owned by the data workstream; consumed by every adapter (DOS-M03-004/005/007/008/009), the crosswalk engine (DOS-M03-006), and the compiler QA (DOS-M03-010).
- Stable-ID generator specification plus golden vectors: the reference implementation and its namespace UUID are committed and shared; no adapter may reimplement ID derivation.
- Major-make allowlist file with business owner and review date: owned by product; consumed by DOS-M03-005.
### Data and persistence
No runtime database in this issue. The dictionary is the semantic contract that DOS-M04-002 turns into physical `CatalogRepo` schema; it must preserve the section 8 boundaries and semantics (M03/M04 may refine names but not meanings). Schemas and golden vectors are Git-versioned; every schema change bumps a schema version consumed by `_provenance.transform_version` policy.
### Interfaces and events
- Adapters emit normalized records that validate against the intermediate schema and carry complete `_provenance`.
- The crosswalk engine consumes and emits `crosswalk_status` values per FR-10.
- The compiler (DOS-M03-010) validates final catalog records against the final-record schema; QA reads the precedence/conflict rules from this contract.
- No runtime events; contract changes flow through reviewed pull requests with version bumps.
### Failure and recovery behavior
- Schema validation is fail-closed: a record that violates unit, null-semantics, provenance, or enumeration rules is rejected with a reason, never silently coerced.
- Conflicting facts are retained as `conflicting` and surfaced to QA; no precedence rule may drop the losing fact.
- A dictionary change that would alter existing stable IDs requires an explicit supersession/alias rule (see DOS-M03-006) rather than in-place re-keying.
### Security and privacy
- The dictionary and schemas contain no secrets, no licensed payload excerpts beyond permitted factual examples, and no personal data. Golden examples use synthetic or clearly permitted values.
- Provenance fields reference `source_key`/`license_version` rather than embedding contract text.
### Accessibility and performance
N/A — specification deliverable. Downstream note: canonical-unit and enumeration decisions must permit index-friendly storage (integers, normalized codes) so cascading selectors work without FTS, per section 8.

## UX and content states
No UI is built here, but the dictionary fixes the vocabulary that UI states must use: distinguishable `unknown` / `not_applicable` / `not_provided` / `conflicting` presentations, the section 5 support statuses, provenance labels required on recommendation detail views, and the rule that no UI label may claim "manufacturer recommended" without qualifying provenance (schema validation rejects unqualified claims per AC-4).

## Test plan
### Automated
- Schema validation suite: positive cases for each golden example; negative cases for unitless capacity, nonpositive interval, missing/unsupported `_provenance`, unknown enumeration values, and unqualified "manufacturer recommended" claims.
- Stable-ID golden vectors: fixed inputs produce byte-identical IDs across machines, shuffled input order, and repeated runs (CI runs the generator at least twice and diffs).
- Lint that every enumeration referenced by the dictionary has a schema definition and vice versa.
### Manual/device matrix
- Structured review by product, data, and engineering reviewers of the dictionary, precedence rules, major-make allowlist, and rolling-year policy, with recorded approval (ADR).
- Domain spot-check of the seven golden examples for automotive plausibility and correct state usage.
- No device work: N/A — specification deliverable.

## Rollout and rollback
The dictionary, schemas, allowlist, and golden vectors are versioned in Git; adoption is by version pin in adapters and the compiler. A breaking contract change requires a version bump, migration notes for normalized data, and re-validation of golden vectors; rollback is re-pinning the previous version. Stable IDs never change retroactively — corrections use alias/supersession semantics.

## Documentation and evidence
Schema validation suite results; stable-ID golden vectors; conflict examples; approved data dictionary and ADR recording reviewer approval of the major-make allowlist and rolling-year policy.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions
- Risk: over-specifying before provider samples arrive could force churn; mitigated by versioned schemas and the rule that refinements preserve section 8 semantics.
- Risk: an adapter silently deviating from the stable-ID spec; mitigated by the shared committed generator and golden vectors in CI.
- Decision embedded here: field-level (not whole-record) precedence, with conflicts retained — this is the foundation for the M05/M06 `conflict` resolver state.
- Open question: exact major-make allowlist membership — owned by product with a recorded review date; the dictionary defines the mechanism regardless of membership.
- Open question: whether `score` gets a documented scoring system in v1; until one exists, `score` is omitted per FR-10.
