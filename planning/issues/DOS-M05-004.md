> **RESCOPED FOR BROWSER MVP — 2026-08-01.** [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) established the browser-first MVP. This card is retained with the same identity; its Outcome and FRs below are the rescoped spec. The pre-pivot spec is preserved at the bottom under "Superseded pre-pivot spec (historical record)". Disposition per [PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md](../../docs/governance/PIVOT-2026-08-01-ROADMAP-RECLASSIFICATION.md#issue-by-issue-classification): _Same write-path change, plus the append-only audit guarantees must be enforced in the client adapter because there is no server transaction to rely on._

## Outcome

Users can add, correct, and delete oil-change events and mileage observations for the single active vehicle in the hosted browser client, without corrupting the timeline and without silently rewriting derived forecast or reminder state. Every mutation is a `pushEvent` → server-computed change → `local_store:put` → IndexedDB `readwrite` transaction across the affected stores plus `meta` → `local_store:ack` round trip ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"Mutation round trip"), and is not rendered as committed until the acknowledgment arrives (INV-24.2). The append-only audit guarantees the pre-pivot card handed to `Ecto.Multi` — nonnegative odometer, deterministic `(performed_at DESC, id ASC)` and `(observed_at DESC, id ASC)` ordering, snapshot-plus-stable-id history, referential linkage of a service event's linked reading, no silent rewrite of a prior reading — are enforced instead by the client IndexedDB adapter inside the `readwrite` transaction and re-validated by the server-side hydration changeset stack, against the shared invariant catalogue and fixture suite ratified by the rescoped `DOS-M04-003`. Derived forecast and reminder staleness is **computed on the next hydration and after each mutation** from the current record set rather than "in the same transaction", because the browser client has no cross-store atomic write against server-persisted derived rows to piggyback on and the deployment holds no server-persisted derived rows in the first place ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision item 4; INV-3; INV-17 as rewritten).

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

The last completed service and the mileage trajectory are still the core facts for the digital sticker and the M06 forecast engine. Corruption of that trajectory — through a double-submit, an odometer discontinuity swallowed silently, or a delete that removes a linked reading collaterally — makes every downstream target date and every due/overdue label untrustworthy, and the hosted-browser architecture removes the two safety nets the pre-pivot card leaned on: there is no SQL `CHECK`/`UNIQUE` (the `UserRepo` does not exist and cannot; [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision item 4), and there is no cross-store transaction to bind history + derived state into a single commit. The write-path change is the same in kind as `DOS-M05-003`'s: mutations traverse a hook round trip and are ack-gated (INV-24.2). What is new here — and why the PIVOT disposition names this card specifically — is that the append-only audit contract must be enforced **in the client adapter**, verified against the shared fixtures of the rescoped `DOS-M04-003`, and re-validated on hydration by the server-side changeset stack of `DOS-M09-003`.

This issue blocks the rescoped `DOS-M05-005` (the dashboard reads the latest committed service and latest valid reading from the hydrated assigns and MUST render the pre-hydration state without flashing a false empty history — the safe-order-of-operations that INV-24.3 exists to protect); the rescoped `DOS-M06-002` and `DOS-M06-003` (the forecast engine consumes valid reading segments and event lineage from hydrated assigns and recomputes on hydration and after each mutation rather than reading a persisted forecast row); and the rescoped `DOS-M06-006`/`DOS-M06-007` (in-app due/overdue state per INV-17 rewritten, which has no OS notification to reconcile and depends entirely on the correctness of the history this card writes).

## Included

- Add, edit, and delete of oil-change events and independent odometer readings against the single active vehicle in IndexedDB, mediated by the `local_store:put` / `local_store:ack` round trip and rendered as committed only on ack (INV-24.2).
- Validation of dates, odometer values, ordering, and units — performed as the `DOS-M04-003` FR-11 integrity checks in the client adapter inside the `readwrite` transaction, and re-validated on hydration by the server-side changeset stack (`DOS-M09-003`).
- Oil/filter metadata per event: optional oil product ID or free-text note and optional filter product ID or free-text note, plus an optional general note. Product-ID references carry an immutable snapshot per `DOS-M04-003` INV-E so history renders after a catalog release moves the product (INV-11, INV-15).
- Service date and service odometer capture on each event.
- Independent odometer readings separate from service events.
- Chronological history view (newest-first default) exposing source (`service_event` / `manual`) and edit lineage, ordered per `DOS-M04-003` FR-5.
- Undo where safe: soft deletion with an in-session undo window, held in `socket.assigns` for the connected session plus a tombstone written to IndexedDB, followed by a permanent purge round trip when the window closes or the user explicitly discards.
- Documented odometer-reset/replacement path recording a discontinuity marker as a superseding record with reason (`DOS-M04-003` FR-8) so M06's forecast engine starts a new segment on recomputation without rewriting history.
- Forecast/notification staleness recomputed **on next hydration and after each mutation** from the hydrated record set (INV-17 rewritten; no persisted derived row exists on the server to invalidate in-transaction).
- Every mutation submits a compare-and-set on `meta.seq` inside its `readwrite` transaction so a stale write across tabs aborts and re-hydrates rather than silently overwriting (INV-24.7).

- Cross-store transactions bundling service events with a derived forecast row or a persisted staleness flag. There are no such persisted derived rows in the deployment; forecast recomputation is a pure function of hydrated inputs (M06 rescoped).
- Any full VIN or personal identifier in a note or metadata field (`DOS-M04-003` INV-G, INV-H; INV-3).

## Functional requirements

- FR-1: A service event record contains: `event_id` (client-generated UUIDv4), `vehicle_id` (referring to an existing hydrated vehicle; `DOS-M04-003` INV-F), local service date in the canonical local-noon/ISO-date form (`DOS-M04-003` FR-6), odometer in the integer base unit with the entered unit and original value retained, optional oil product ID plus an immutable product snapshot per `DOS-M04-003` INV-E, optional filter product ID plus an immutable snapshot, optional oil free-text note, optional filter free-text note, optional general note, `created_at` and `updated_at` UTC instants, and soft-deletion tombstone metadata during the undo window. No column identifies a person (INV-3; `DOS-M04-003` INV-H).
- FR-2: An odometer reading record contains: `reading_id` (UUIDv4), `vehicle_id`, `observed_at` local date (canonical rule) or UTC instant per capture mode, odometer in integer base unit with entered unit and original value retained, `source` enum (`service_event` | `manual`, with `import` reserved for a later card; `DOS-M04-003` INV-E enumeration rule), `source_reference` (the `event_id` when `source == :service_event`), and validity/supersession fields for the discontinuity path. Odometer values are nonnegative (`DOS-M04-003` INV-A).
- FR-3: Editing a service event updates the event record and its linked reading — the reading whose `source == :service_event` and `source_reference == event_id` — inside **one IndexedDB `readwrite` transaction spanning `events`, `readings`, and `meta`** ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"Mutation round trip"). Deleting the event tombstones the event and its linked reading in the same transaction, and MUST NOT touch any independently entered reading — one where `source == :manual` — even when the manual reading falls on the same local date. The transaction performs a compare-and-set on `meta.seq` (INV-24.7); a mismatch aborts, the ack reports the conflict, the server discards its optimistic assigns and triggers re-hydration. This FR replaces the pre-pivot FR-3 that assumed a same-database `Ecto.Multi` and is retired en bloc; the semantic guarantee (linked-reading atomicity, no collateral removal of an independent reading) survives, but the mechanism is the client `readwrite` transaction, not SQL.
- FR-4: Same-day readings for a vehicle are permitted only when their ordering and latest value are explicit. A reading whose value is below the immediately preceding reading, or above an immediately following reading, is **rejected** by the client adapter inside the `readwrite` transaction unless the user has invoked the documented reset/replacement path (FR-5). Rejection surfaces to the user through the ack as a typed adapter error naming the exact conflicting `reading_id`s; the prior state in `socket.assigns` is preserved and the unsaved form values are preserved (FR-11). The same rule is re-checked by the server-side hydration changeset on every mount so a corrupt payload cannot slip into assigns even if the adapter is bypassed (`DOS-M04-003` FR-3 two-point enforcement). This FR replaces the pre-pivot FR-4 that assumed a server transaction to reject inside; the semantic rule is unchanged.
- FR-5: The reset/replacement path is a distinct explicit user action that writes a **superseding odometer reading** with a reason (`DOS-M04-003` FR-8) rather than mutating prior readings. The record carries `source == :manual` and a discontinuity marker consistent with `DOS-M04-003` INV-E snapshot semantics, so the forecast engine's next recomputation (on hydration or after mutation) starts a new segment without reinterpreting history. No prior reading is ever silently rewritten; correction is always additive.
- FR-6: Future service dates and future observation dates are rejected by the client adapter and by the hydration changeset alike. Dates before the vehicle model year produce a warning and require correction. Date-only capture uses the canonical local-noon/ISO-date rule (`DOS-M04-003` FR-6) and is tested at a DST boundary to confirm no day-shift.
- FR-7: Notes are local plain text, length-limited (see decision to record below), rendered without HTML in the LiveView template. There is no rich-text surface; the CSP forbids inline script and `object-src 'none'` (ADR-0004 §"Content Security Policy") is the structural backstop.
- FR-8: The history list defaults newest-first and honors the `DOS-M04-003` FR-5 ordering contracts: service events by `(performed_at DESC, id ASC)`, odometer readings by `(observed_at DESC, id ASC)`. Each entry shows source (`service_event`/`manual`), edit lineage from correction records, and provenance mode (`catalog` or `manual`) — never "manufacturer verified" (INV-15; INV-21).
- FR-9: Delete requires an explicit confirmation and offers an in-session undo affordance for a documented window. The tombstone lives in IndexedDB (so a reconnect within the window sees it and can un-tombstone), and a `pending_undo` mirror in `socket.assigns` drives the UI. Expiry writes a permanent-purge round trip via `local_store:put` and does not silently resurrect. Independently entered same-date readings are never collaterally purged (FR-3).
- FR-10: **Any history mutation triggers derived-state recomputation on the next hydration and after the current mutation's ack**, from the current record set in `socket.assigns`. There is no persisted derived forecast or reminder row on the server to invalidate in-transaction (INV-3; INV-17 as rewritten), and there is no cross-store transaction between the browser stores and any server table. This FR replaces the pre-pivot FR-10's "in the same transaction as any history mutation" language, which assumed a same-database `Ecto.Multi` covering derived tables. The semantic guarantee — no stale forecast is presented after a mutation — is preserved by the recompute-after-ack rule; the mechanism is a pure recomputation over hydrated inputs, not a transaction.
- FR-11: Timeline-constraint violations returned by the adapter or the hydration changeset identify the exact conflicting `event_id` or `reading_id`s and preserve the user's unsaved form values in `socket.assigns` across the failed round trip. Optimistic UI is permitted per INV-24.2 only if a failed ack visibly reverts and reports the failure; a persistent "Not saved to this browser" state is rendered on the affected record with export offered (ADR-0004 §"Mutation round trip"; INV-25).
- FR-12: Recording a new oil change closes the previous cycle per §6 step 8. Cycle bookkeeping is a domain-context concern that reads current hydrated state and writes a new event; the forecast recomputation itself is M06 rescoped.
- FR-13: **Quota-exceeded (`QuotaExceededError`) during a write** MUST surface to the user in plain language, preserve the prior state in assigns, offer export, and MUST NOT drop data silently (INV-24.5; ADR-0004 §"Quota and eviction"). This is a new FR compared with the pre-pivot spec, which had no browser quota to consider.
- FR-14: **Cleared browser data at hydration time** (boot hint says this origin held data but the `events`/`readings` stores open empty or `meta` is absent) MUST render the `:data_missing` state and offer import, never the empty "record your first oil change" onboarding (INV-24.3, INV-24.8, INV-25). The client stores are the sole system of record and there is no server-side backup to fall back to (INV-3, INV-5). This is a new FR compared with the pre-pivot spec, which had a durable on-device database.
- FR-15: **Multi-tab writes** for the same vehicle MUST NOT silently overwrite. Every mutation performs a compare-and-set on `meta.seq` inside its `readwrite` transaction (INV-24.7); a mismatch aborts and the losing tab re-hydrates, replacing its assigns and re-applying in-flight optimistic edits or surfacing a per-record conflict when the same record changed. A `BroadcastChannel("dos_local_store")` post-commit notice from the writing tab shortens the time to re-hydration in the receiving tab. Under the single-user assumption, last-write-wins at record granularity is accepted for MVP with the loss made visible (ADR-0004 §"Multi-tab consistency"; R4).
- FR-16: **Reconnect-mid-mutation** (network blip, LiveView redeploy) MUST NOT lose an in-flight write. A mutation whose ack did not arrive before the socket dropped is either (a) already committed in IndexedDB and observed on the next hydration, in which case the round trip finishes cleanly, or (b) not committed, in which case the record is re-driven from the hydrated inputs and the user's unsaved values in assigns are re-presented rather than silently discarded ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"WebSocket and LiveView considerations", §"Mutation round trip").
- FR-17: Mutating context APIs accept the `DOS-M05-001` idempotency token and `mutation_id`/`seq` guard; a duplicate submission with the same `mutation_id` returns the original result without producing a second write.
- **Retired pre-pivot FRs.** The pre-pivot FR-3 ("linked mutations run in one `Ecto.Multi` wholly inside `UserRepo`") and FR-10 ("marks derived forecast and notification plans stale in the same transaction") are retired en bloc by [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision items 3 and 4. Their semantic content is preserved by the new FR-3 (client `readwrite` transaction spanning `events` + `readings` + `meta`) and FR-10 (recompute on next hydration and after each mutation). The other pre-pivot FRs are carried forward with browser-first mechanics as noted above.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

- `OilChangeLive` (event add/edit), `MileageLive` (readings and check-ins), and the history surface render typed `Maintenance` context results from hydrated `socket.assigns`. They never open IndexedDB directly; they call the `Maintenance` context, which in turn produces the `local_store:put` envelope. Validation and timeline rules live in the domain, never in the LiveView modules — this preserves ADR-0004 §Decision-item-6 (the domain contexts are client-agnostic; a future native client is a second client over the same contract, differing only in its storage adapter).
- The client adapter (`DOS-M09-002`) is the sole enforcer of `DOS-M04-003` invariants on the client side and owns `readonly`/`readwrite` transactions, quota/persistence probes, and typed errors.
- The hydration changeset stack (`DOS-M09-003`) is the sole enforcer on the server side; it validates every hydration payload and every `local_store:put` echo so that a bypassed client adapter cannot corrupt assigns.
- `Maintenance` owns service events, cycles, and linked-reading atomicity semantics against the hydrated state and produces the mutation envelopes; `Garage` supplies the active `vehicle_id`; `Clock` (§7) supplies injected time so future-date validation is testable.
- Undo/soft-delete state is domain-owned: the tombstone lives in IndexedDB (durable across reconnect within its window), a `pending_undo` mirror in `socket.assigns` drives the UI, and the permanent-purge trigger is a domain event, not a LiveView side effect.

### Data and persistence

- Writes target the `events`, `readings`, and `meta` object stores per `DOS-M09-001`; odometer values persist in the canonical integer base unit with the entered unit and original value retained (`DOS-M04-003` FR-6). No personal row is ever written server-side (INV-3; `DOS-M04-003` FR-14).
- Linked mutations (event plus its linked reading plus `meta.seq` bump) run in **one IndexedDB `readwrite` transaction** across `events`, `readings`, and `meta` — the client-side substitute for the pre-pivot `Ecto.Multi`. The transaction commits atomically per the IndexedDB spec; abort is honored and no partial commit is possible. Compare-and-set on `meta.seq` inside the same transaction (INV-24.7) prevents cross-tab lost-update corruption; the failed-ack path (ADR-0004 §"Mutation round trip") handles a rejected commit.
- Soft deletion uses tombstone/validity fields during the undo window; permanent purge is a separate explicit round trip that also runs in a `readwrite` transaction against `events` + `readings` + `meta`. Independently entered readings at the same local date are never collaterally removed.
- The odometer-reset path writes an explicit discontinuity marker as a superseding reading per `DOS-M04-003` FR-8, consistent with the segment semantics the M06 forecast engine consumes.
- Snapshot-plus-stable-id on oil-product and filter-product references (`DOS-M04-003` INV-E) means history renders even after a catalog release marks the identifier `unsupported` (INV-11).
- Server transient state: hydrated `events`/`readings` in `socket.assigns` for the lifetime of one LiveView connection, discarded at socket termination and never written to disk, database, cache, ETS table, external store, or log ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision item 4).

### Interfaces and events

- Context APIs (`Maintenance` module): `create_service_event/2`, `update_service_event/3`, `delete_service_event/2`, `create_odometer_reading/2`, `update_odometer_reading/3`, `delete_odometer_reading/2`, `list_history/2` (paged, newest-first, honoring `DOS-M04-003` FR-5 ordering), `record_discontinuity/2` (the reset path), and `undo_last_delete/2` within the window. Every mutating call assigns a `mutation_id` (UUID) and `seq = current_seq + 1`, records it in `assigns.pending_writes`, renders optimistically per INV-24.2 with a "saving" affordance, and produces one `local_store:put` envelope.
- Committed mutations do **not** publish a server-side event bus message. Instead, on ack the domain recomputes derived forecast and reminder state from hydrated inputs (FR-10); M06's engine subscribes to the ack via the assigns-update path, not to a persisted `service_event_recorded` row (there is no such row). The pre-pivot events (`service_event_recorded`, `odometer_recorded`) are retired for the browser MVP; their semantic role is replaced by "recompute on ack".
- Every mutation envelope carries the mutation-id/`seq`/idempotency-token contract from `DOS-M05-001`; a duplicate `mutation_id` returns the original result without producing a second IndexedDB write (FR-17).
- The hydration envelope shape is owned by `DOS-M09-001` FR-17 and consumed unchanged by `DOS-M09-003`; this card only specifies the semantic contents (a valid event, a valid reading) that must pass `DOS-M04-003`'s FR-11 rules.

### Failure and recovery behavior

- **Concurrent double-submit** produces at most one committed record because `mutation_id` de-duplication (FR-17) rejects the second submission before it opens a `readwrite` transaction, and because the compare-and-set on `meta.seq` (FR-15) rejects the second commit if it somehow reached the adapter. Both paths surface a typed error to the LiveView.
- **App termination during save** — the ack path either completes before the tab closes (record committed, observed on next hydration) or does not (nothing committed; the user's unsaved values in assigns are gone with the socket, and the record simply does not exist). There is no half-committed state because IndexedDB `readwrite` transactions commit atomically. This is the browser-first substitute for the pre-pivot device-termination guarantee, and it is stronger in kind because there is no server-side row to be inconsistent with — there is no server-side row.
- **Validation failures** (returned by the adapter or by the hydration changeset) surface as typed field-level errors naming the exact conflicting records (FR-11). Unsaved form values are preserved in assigns across the failed round trip.
- **Failed ack** within the 2 000 ms timeout, or an ack reporting an error, renders a persistent, non-dismissible "Not saved to this browser" state on the affected record, keeps the optimistic value visible but visibly marked as unsaved, and offers export ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"Mutation round trip"). No silent retry, no invisible rollback.
- **Quota exceeded** (FR-13) follows the failed-ack path with a quota-specific message; data is never dropped silently to make room (INV-24.5).
- **Multi-tab conflict** (FR-15) aborts the losing write; the losing tab re-hydrates and shows a visible "reloaded from this browser's newer data" notice; last-write-wins at record granularity with the loss made visible.
- **Reconnect after LiveView redeploy** re-runs the mount-and-hydration sequence; in-flight `pending_writes` are re-driven from the hydrated inputs (FR-16). Because the tombstone lives in IndexedDB, the undo window is preserved across reconnect within its documented duration.
- **Cleared site data at hydration** (FR-14) yields `:data_missing` per INV-24.8 and INV-25, not a fresh onboarding.

### Security and privacy

- **Personal data is transient in `socket.assigns` for the lifetime of one LiveView connection** — populated by the hydration protocol at mount, discarded at socket termination, and never written to disk, database, cache, ETS table, external store, or log ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision item 4; INV-3). This replaces the pre-pivot claim that "History never leaves the device" verbatim: in a hosted app that phrasing is false, because the values transit the WSS socket into assigns on every mutation (bounded by INV-24) and are visible in the running process for the connection lifetime — so the honest claim is scoped precisely to *no server persistence*, not to *no server touches the data*.
- INV-4 binds: personal data leaves the browser only through user-initiated export, and the export/import path is `DOS-M04-009` (rescoped) plus `DOS-M09-001` FR-17.
- Notes are local plain text, length-limited, rendered without HTML (FR-7). The CSP (`default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; object-src 'none'`; ADR-0004 §"Content Security Policy") is the structural backstop against a stored-note script injection surface; no third-party origin can be reached from a rendered page (INV-4, INV-22).
- Transport is HTTPS/WSS with `check_origin` restricted to the exact production hostnames and CSRF verified on socket connect (INV-22; ADR-0004 §"Security and privacy boundary for a hosted application"). This is the hosted substitute for the retired loopback-only INV-2, and it is the boundary this card's mutations traverse.
- Logs MUST NOT contain VIN fragments, odometer values, service dates, note text, custom vehicle names, or any hydration/mutation payload or fragment ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"Logging"). Enforcement includes: request-param logging disabled, `filter_parameters` configured, and the scripted-session log-scanning release-gate test from ADR-0004 §"Enforcement". `debug_errors: false` in production so assigns never reach an error page.
- Source and edit exposure (FR-8) is an audit surface **for the user in their browser**; nothing about it is transmitted for telemetry (INV-4).
- Third-party error reporting is prohibited (`socket.assigns` contains the user's garage; any serializer would exfiltrate exactly the data this architecture exists to keep off servers). If ever added, it requires an ADR plus a proven assigns-scrubbing layer.

### Accessibility and performance

- Validation summaries and field errors are announced to browser assistive technology (screen readers, focus management) — the WCAG 2.2 AA contract from the rescoped `DOS-M02-002` binds, verified against the browser/engine/AT matrix from `DOS-M09-009` and the rescoped `DOS-M07-002`. The accessibility snapshot of the validation summary is required evidence.
- Delete confirmation and undo controls are keyboard and screen-reader operable with visible labels and adequate target sizes; the standards are the browser AT contract, not native OS controls.
- The mutation round trip's ack time is bounded by the 2 000 ms timeout in ADR-0004; interactive controls MUST NOT accept input while `local_state == :hydrating` (INV-24.3).
- The history list is paged; rendering stays responsive with multi-year histories under the rescoped `DOS-M07-003` browser budgets (page-load, time-to-hydrated, LiveView round-trip latency, IndexedDB footprint), not device budgets.

## UX and content states

- **Pre-hydration** (`local_state == :hydrating`): renders a determinate skeleton with interaction disabled (INV-24.3). Never a "record your first oil change" prompt while assigns have not been filled.
- **Empty history** (`local_state == :loaded` with zero events and zero manual readings for the active vehicle): tells the user the next useful action (record the first oil change).
- **Data missing** (`dos_boot_state == "has_data"` but `events`/`readings` opened empty or `meta` absent; INV-24.8, INV-25): renders the explicit warning that this browser's stored data appears to have been cleared, and offers import. This is a distinct state from `:empty` and says so in words.
- **Storage unavailable** (private-browsing/blocked storage; INV-24.6): a persistent banner states nothing is being saved and that closing the tab loses everything; export is offered prominently. Session-only mode is functional for viewing already-hydrated state but any mutation surfaces the failed-ack state immediately.
- **Populated history**: newest-first per `DOS-M04-003` FR-5 ordering, each entry showing source (`service_event` / `manual`), edit lineage, and provenance mode (`catalog` / `manual`) — never "manufacturer verified" (INV-15, INV-21).
- **Validation states**: future-date rejection; before-model-year warning requiring correction; ordering conflicts naming the exact conflicting `event_id` / `reading_id`s with unsaved input preserved.
- **Reset/discontinuity flow**: documented explanation that a new forecast segment starts on next recomputation and history is not rewritten.
- **Delete flow**: confirmation, then in-session undo affordance until the window closes; on expiry the permanent-purge round trip runs.
- **Optimistic-save affordance and failed-ack state**: per INV-24.2 and ADR-0004 §"Mutation round trip" — "saving" during the round trip, then either saved on ack or a persistent "Not saved to this browser" state visible on the record with export offered (INV-25).
- **Multi-tab reload notice**: after a compare-and-set abort or a `BroadcastChannel` post-commit re-hydration, a visible "reloaded from this browser's newer data" notice, per INV-24.7.

Copy for each state is owned by the rescoped `DOS-M02-003` and MUST satisfy INV-21 terminology: records are "stored in this browser", never "saved to your account", "synced", "backed up", or "restored" (INV-5, INV-25).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC-1: A user can create a first oil change, add later manual readings, edit all allowed fields, delete with an undo affordance, and let the undo window expire to permanent purge — in the hosted browser, with every mutation rendered as committed only after `local_store:ack` (INV-24.2), across one desktop and one mobile browser from the `DOS-M09-009` support matrix.
- [ ] AC-2: Timeline-constraint violations identify the exact conflicting `event_id` / `reading_id`s and preserve the user's unsaved form values in `socket.assigns` across the failed round trip.
- [ ] AC-3: Service-event and readings remain referentially consistent under concurrent double-submit (`mutation_id` de-duplication), across app termination (no half-committed IndexedDB transaction), and across LiveView reconnect mid-mutation (FR-16), with at most one committed record per intended mutation.
- [ ] AC-4: **A history mutation triggers derived forecast and reminder recomputation from the hydrated record set after the current mutation's ack and again on the next hydration** (FR-10). No stale forecast is presented after the ack completes. There is no persisted derived row on the server to invalidate; the recomputation is a pure function of hydrated inputs.
- [ ] AC-5: Empty history, pre-hydration, `:data_missing`, and storage-unavailable states are visually distinct and render the copy specified by the rescoped `DOS-M02-003`; a returning user never sees the empty "record your first oil change" prompt while `local_state == :hydrating`, and never sees it after cleared site data (INV-24.3, INV-24.8, INV-25).
- [ ] AC-6: Deleting a service event tombstones the event and its linked reading atomically in one `readwrite` transaction across `events` + `readings` + `meta`, and MUST NOT tombstone an independently entered same-date `source == :manual` reading; the shared `DOS-M04-003` fixture asserting this passes.
- [ ] AC-7: A same-day reading violating FR-4 is rejected by the client adapter and by the server-side hydration changeset alike, and the shared `DOS-M04-003` fixture-equivalence test asserts equal accept/reject outcomes on both sides for at least one valid and one violation fixture per FR.
- [ ] AC-8: A concurrent write from a second tab is rejected by the `meta.seq` compare-and-set (INV-24.7); the losing tab re-hydrates and shows the "reloaded from this browser's newer data" notice. Last-write-wins at record granularity with the loss made visible.
- [ ] AC-9: A write that fails with `QuotaExceededError` surfaces in plain language, preserves prior state in assigns, offers export, and MUST NOT drop data silently (INV-24.5).
- [ ] AC-10: The scripted-session log-scanning gate (ADR-0004 §"Enforcement") passes for a full CRUD session against this card's flows: no VIN fragment, odometer value, service date, note text, or custom vehicle name appears in any captured log line.
- [ ] AC-11: The odometer-reset/replacement path writes a superseding reading with reason and preserves the prior reading; no fixture demonstrates a silent rewrite.
- [ ] AC-12: A payload whose logical schema version exceeds the deployed server's puts the session into read-only mode (mutations disabled, banner, export offered) — no mutation from this card's flows is written in that state ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) §"Schema versioning and migration"; INV-24.3).

## Rollout and rollback

Ships in the normal browser MVP deploy through `DOS-M09-006` (Fly.io deploy pipeline + release provenance + rollback) with the log-scanning release gate (AC-10) and the fixture-equivalence gate (AC-7) required green. Rollback is a redeploy of the previous image; because the deployment holds no personal rows there is nothing to migrate down, and clients auto-reconnect and re-hydrate from their browsers. Committed `events` and `readings` records in a user's IndexedDB survive the redeploy because they were never on the server. Where an invariant tightened in this card's slice would refuse a record a prior release wrote, the failure surface is the `DOS-M04-003` FR-12 quarantine (valid subset hydrates; offender held aside; count surfaced; raw payload exportable), never silent deletion — the `DOS-M04-003` "additive on the read side" rollback obligation applies. The undo window is an in-session UX affordance held in `socket.assigns` plus an IndexedDB tombstone, not a schema behavior, so reverting code cannot corrupt stored history.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: the client adapter and the server-side hydration changeset drift silently and accept different fixtures. Mitigation: `DOS-M04-003` AC-3 (fixture-equivalence release gate) covers exactly this and is a release gate, not a convention.
- Risk: the failed-ack round-trip time (default 2 000 ms per ADR-0004) is user-perceptible on slow connections and can look like a hang. Mitigation: the optimistic "saving" affordance covers the common case; the persistent "Not saved to this browser" state is the honest surface for the failure case; the LiveView `phx-disconnected` styling covers a lost socket. Re-measure against the rescoped `DOS-M07-003` LiveView round-trip latency budget.
- Risk: `QuotaExceededError` behavior varies by browser and by whether persistent storage was granted. Mitigation: quota-specific test (AC-9) run across the `DOS-M09-009` matrix; the failed-ack path is uniform regardless of the cause.
- Risk: an odometer correction with a same-instant timestamp could tie-break unstably under the `(observed_at DESC, id ASC)` ordering. Mitigation: `DOS-M04-003` FR-5 pins the tie-break to `id ASC` (client-generated UUIDv4) and asserts stability across a same-instant fixture; ordering equivalence between the client adapter's read and the hydrated assigns is asserted (`DOS-M04-003` AC-7).
- Risk: multi-tab last-write-wins at record granularity (accepted per R4 of ADR-0004) is materially wrong when two tabs edit the same event. Mitigation: the loss is made visible via the reload notice; the compare-and-set surface prevents silent lost updates; re-examine on evidence if a real workflow makes this wrong.
- Risk: users interpret an unacknowledged optimistic save as saved, notice a "Not saved" state only after acting on it, and lose the intended write when the tab closes. Mitigation: INV-24.2 is normative; the "saving" affordance is distinct from the saved affordance; the failed-ack state is persistent and non-dismissible; export is offered. This is the honest surface; the alternative (silent retry) is prohibited.
- Decision to record: the in-session undo-window duration and the permanent-purge trigger, documented with the domain tests. Same open question the pre-pivot card raised, but now bounded by the LiveView session lifetime and the IndexedDB tombstone durability. Default proposal: a window measured in tens of seconds while the socket is connected, with the tombstone persisted so the window survives one reconnect; closes with the domain tests.
- Decision to record: the length limit for notes (FR-7), documented with sanitization tests. Same as the pre-pivot card; a documented value, not a magic number.
- Decision to record: the failed-ack timeout, `_max_reconnect_backoff`, and the specific compare-and-set retry policy, aligned with `DOS-M09-003` and the constitution §9 LiveView round-trip latency budget.
- Open question: whether the `import` reading source (future `DOS-M05-007` / M08 use, listed in `DOS-M04-003` INV-E's enumeration) is created now as an enum value in the fixture suite or added by a `DOS-M09-001` schema-version bump later. Mirrors `DOS-M04-003`'s open question on the invariant catalogue; the two are closed together.
- Open question: whether an in-flight optimistic edit in tab A survives a compare-and-set-driven re-hydration in tab A after tab B commits an unrelated record. Default: yes, re-applied on top of the fresh assigns per ADR-0004 §"Multi-tab consistency"; conflict on the same record surfaces per FR-15. Confirm in the multi-tab test (AC-8).

## Superseded pre-pivot spec (historical record)

> This section preserves the card as chartered before the 2026-08-01 pivot to browser-first, and is retained for historical readability of the ADR record. The Outcome above is authoritative.

### Outcome (pre-pivot)

Users can add, correct, and delete oil-change events and mileage observations without corrupting the timeline or silently rewriting forecasts.

### Why this matters and what it unblocks (pre-pivot)

The last completed service and mileage trajectory are the core facts for the digital sticker and the M06 forecast engine. If the history can be corrupted by an edit, a double-submit, or an odometer discontinuity, every downstream target date and reminder is untrustworthy. This issue blocks DOS-M05-005 (the dashboard reads the latest service and latest valid reading), DOS-M06-002 and DOS-M06-003 (the forecast engine consumes valid reading segments), and DOS-M06-006.

### Context and source of truth (pre-pivot)

- Master roadmap prompt (`digital-oil-sticker-claude-code-roadmap-prompt.md`), §11.3 catalog entry "DOS-M05-004 — Record oil changes and odometer readings as an auditable local history".
- §3 rule 3 (no telematics: users enter service mileage and later readings manually) and rule 10 (change control).
- §6 journey steps 4, 7, and 8: record last oil change; later odometer check-ins refine the estimate; recording a new oil change closes the previous cycle.
- §8 `UserRepo` baseline: `service_events` (UUID, vehicle ID, performed local date, normalized odometer plus unit, oil/filter snapshots, notes, provenance mode `catalog` or `manual`, timestamps and correction lineage) and `odometer_readings` (observed instant/local date, normalized odometer, input unit, source `service_event` or `manual`, validity/supersession fields, index `(vehicle_id, observed_at)`); integer base units for mileage.
- §9 Forecast reference behavior: reject or quarantine negative distance, duplicate-time contradictions, implausible values, and superseded readings — never silently delete them; the odometer-reset path starts a new segment.
- DOS-M05-001 context APIs, `Ecto.Multi` transaction rules, idempotency guards, and event contract; DOS-M05-003 profile and unit semantics.

### Included (pre-pivot)

- Add, edit, and delete of oil-change events and independent odometer readings.
- Validation of dates, odometer values, ordering, and units.
- Oil/filter notes: optional oil product ID/free-text note and filter product ID/free-text note per event, plus an optional general note.
- Service date and service odometer capture.
- Independent odometer readings separate from service events.
- Chronological history view (newest-first default) exposing source and edits.
- Undo where safe: soft deletion with an in-session undo window before permanent purge.
- Documented odometer-reset/replacement path recording a discontinuity and starting a new forecast segment.
- Forecast/notification staleness invalidation in the same transaction as any history mutation.

### Excluded (pre-pivot)

- Receipt images.
- Shop integrations.
- GPS.
- Automatic odometer capture.
- Shared history.

### Preconditions (pre-pivot)

- DOS-M05-001 is closed: `Maintenance` context, `Ecto.Multi` same-database transactions, idempotency guards, typed errors, and events exist.
- DOS-M05-003 is closed: an active vehicle profile with unit preference and (possibly incomplete) interval basis exists to attach history to.
- M02 copy for validation messages, undo, delete confirmation, and empty states is ratified.

### Functional requirements (pre-pivot)

- FR-1: A service event contains stable ID, vehicle ID, local service date, odometer, entered unit/original value, optional oil product ID/free-text note, optional filter product ID/free-text note, optional general note, created/updated instants, and soft-deletion metadata during the undo window.
- FR-2: Odometer readings contain observation date, mileage, source (`service_event`, `manual`, later `import`), and source reference.
- FR-3: Editing a service event updates its linked observation atomically. Deleting it removes or tombstones only its linked reading, never an independently entered reading at the same date.
- FR-4: Permit same-day readings only when their ordering and latest value are explicit; reject a reading below the preceding reading or above a later reading unless the user invokes a documented odometer-reset/replacement path.
- FR-5: The MVP reset path records a discontinuity and starts a new forecast segment rather than altering history.
- FR-6: Future service dates and future observation dates are rejected. Dates before the vehicle model year produce a warning and require correction.
- FR-7: Notes are local plain text, length-limited, and rendered without HTML.
- FR-8: The history defaults newest-first, exposes source and edits, and never calls a record "manufacturer verified."
- FR-9: Delete requires confirmation and offers an in-session undo before permanent purge.
- FR-10: Any history mutation marks derived forecast and notification plans stale in the same transaction (per the DOS-M05-001 invalidation contract).
- FR-11: Timeline-constraint violations identify the exact conflicting readings and preserve the user's unsaved form values.
- FR-12: All flows work fully offline; recording a new oil change closes the previous cycle per §6 step 8 (cycle bookkeeping via `Maintenance`; forecast recomputation itself is M06).

### Technical implementation contract (pre-pivot)

Retired en bloc. The pre-pivot contract assumed a `UserRepo` and `Ecto.Multi` transactions covering `service_events`, `odometer_readings`, and derived staleness marks in one commit; typed errors surfaced through Ecto's changeset stack; and "History never leaves the device" as a categorical privacy claim. None of these apply in a deployment with no `UserRepo` and no cross-store server transaction ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision items 3 and 4). The semantic content — linked-reading atomicity, no collateral deletion of an independent reading, ordering constraints, discontinuity as a superseding record, note sanitization, undo-and-purge semantics, no-silent-forecast-staleness — is preserved by the browser-first FRs above; the SQL/Multi mechanics are not carried over.

The pre-pivot "Security and privacy" section claimed that "History never leaves the device" and referenced logs and fixtures redacting mileage and note content per §7. In the hosted-browser deployment the phrase is literally false: values transit the WSS socket into `socket.assigns` on every hydrate and every mutation, bounded by INV-24. The accurate statement, retained above in the rescoped Security and privacy section, is that personal data is transient in `socket.assigns` for the lifetime of one LiveView connection and is never persisted server-side ([ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) Decision item 4; INV-3); log-scan enforcement (ADR-0004 §"Enforcement") is the release gate.
