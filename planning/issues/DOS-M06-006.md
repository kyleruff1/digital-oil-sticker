## Outcome

The set of OS-scheduled requests converges to the SQLite desired plan whenever the app can run, while already scheduled native notifications remain able to fire when the BEAM is suspended or killed. Reconciliation is idempotent, journaled, and correct across forecast edits, permission changes, daylight saving time, timezone travel, manual clock changes, app lifecycle transitions, reboots, and upgrades.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

The BEAM does not continue executing in the background. Scheduling must happen ahead of suspension, and stale reminders must be repaired after forecast, permission, clock, timezone, app, or OS changes. Master prompt §7 forbids Oban or a perpetual background GenServer for deadlines: the app must "recompute synchronously/on a supervised foreground task after a relevant mutation and at app foreground, then let the OS hold local notification requests." This issue makes that architecture actually reliable, and it blocks the reminder control center (`DOS-M06-007`), the M07 release gates `DOS-M07-004` and `DOS-M07-007`, and post-MVP capacity work in `DOS-M08-004`.

## Included

- Reconciliation diff: desired-versus-applied set computation with schedule, cancel, and cancel-then-reschedule operations.
- Lifecycle triggers: boot, foreground/resume, pre-background, post-commit changes, permission grant, app/package upgrade, locale/date/time/timezone changes.
- Timezone/DST conversion: semantic local date/time plus timezone policy converted through an IANA timezone database to UTC, with gap/overlap rules.
- Reboot/update behavior: verification of the selected `mob_notify` version's Android reboot/package-replacement rescheduling, with a scoped native remediation or documented release blocker.
- Partial-failure retry: journaled per-ID results, "Reminders need attention" state, retry at next foreground or explicit Retry.
- Schedule-ahead bounds: platform capacity constant and conservative slot use (at most three forecast reminders plus one test in M06).
- Stale notification tap handling: obsolete-revision taps open current truth.
- Crash recovery: unapplied operations remain safely retryable after root-screen restart or command timeout.

## Excluded

- Background polling.
- An Android foreground service.
- Remote push.
- Any promise that JavaScript/BEAM code runs while killed.
- Creating the desired plan, permission flow, or adapter (owned by `DOS-M06-005`).
- Cross-vehicle notification allocation (M08 defines it; this issue fixes the M06 capacity bound).

## Functional requirements

- FR-1 (diff semantics): The SQLite desired plan is the source of scheduling intent; the OS is the delivery mechanism. Reconciliation computes `desired IDs - applied IDs` to schedule, `applied IDs - desired IDs` to cancel, and changed payload/time for cancel-then-reschedule. Operations are idempotent and journal each per-ID result; mark the applied revision only after all operations succeed.
- FR-2 (triggers and worker): Trigger reconciliation after a committed forecast/settings/history change; after permission grant; at successful app boot; before backgrounding when lifecycle time permits; on foreground/resume; after app/package upgrade; and after observed locale/date/time/timezone changes. Coalesce triggers through one supervised foreground worker. That worker computes and journals the diff, then sends sequential typed commands to the DeviceCommandBroker; it never calls `MobNotify` or holds a `Mob.Socket` itself. Root-screen restart/command timeout leaves operations unapplied and safely retryable.
- FR-3 (timezone/DST conversion): Store reminders as semantic local date plus wall-clock time and timezone policy `follow_device_timezone`. At each reconciliation, convert through an IANA timezone database to UTC. If the chosen local time falls in a DST spring-forward gap, use the first valid local instant after the gap. If it is ambiguous during fall-back, choose the earlier occurrence. Store chosen timezone ID, UTC instant, offset, and tzdata version for evidence.
- FR-4 (clock and timezone changes): A timezone change preserves the user's wall-clock time and recomputes future UTC instants. A manual clock/date jump causes a full replan: past leads are removed and collapse under the due-now rule. Locale changes affect copy only, not schedule semantics.
- FR-5 (schedule-ahead, no background execution): Schedule all currently eligible M06 reminders ahead of time while the app is active. Do not rely on a Timer, GenServer, LiveView process, service worker, or network while suspended/killed. The native OS request is expected to display with the app killed; tapping/relaunching is handled through `mob_notify`'s notification event/launch handoff.
- FR-6 (capacity bounds): Maintain a conservative platform capacity constant and never fill all platform slots. M06 has at most three forecast reminders plus one test; M08 defines cross-vehicle allocation. When native pending-request enumeration is unavailable, trust only successfully journaled IDs and issue idempotent cancel/schedule operations; document this limitation.
- FR-7 (partial failure and revocation): Partial failure leaves desired state intact, reports a nonalarming “Reminders need attention” state, and retries at next foreground or explicit Retry. Permission revocation marks applied entries uncertain, attempts cancellation where permitted, and stops new scheduling.
- FR-8 (stale taps and tap journal): A tap for an obsolete forecast revision opens the current dashboard and displays current truth, not archived payload content. Record only a local, bounded tap/handling journal; absence of a tap is not proof of nondelivery.
- FR-9 (reboot/upgrade verification): Verify Android reboot/package-replacement rescheduling behavior of the selected `mob_notify` version. If the plugin/host does not restore alarms, add the smallest native boot/package receiver enhancement within scope or document an explicit release blocker; the BEAM cannot repair the plan until the next app launch.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

- One supervised foreground reconciliation worker inside `DigitalOilSticker.Reminders` (§7) owns trigger coalescing, diff computation, journaling, and sequential command dispatch to the `DeviceCommandBroker`. It never holds a `Mob.Socket` or calls `MobNotify` (FR-2).
- The root `Mob.Screen` adapter (from `DOS-M06-005`) executes schedule/cancel commands and returns correlated results; timeout/restart semantics follow the §7 broker contract.
- Timezone conversion is a dedicated, pure, tzdata-versioned module so DST rules are testable without devices.
- No new LiveView surfaces; user-visible "Reminders need attention" and Retry presentation are completed in `DOS-M06-007`, backed by state this issue maintains.

### Data and persistence

- Extends `scheduled_notifications` (§8) usage: per stable notification ID — desired and scheduled instants, due-date snapshot, payload version, platform state, last error/reconciliation time; plus per-reconciliation journal entries with per-ID operation results (FR-1).
- Stores semantic local date, wall-clock time, and timezone policy `follow_device_timezone`; at conversion time records chosen timezone ID, UTC instant, offset, and tzdata version (FR-3).
- The applied-plan revision advances only after all operations in a reconciliation succeed (FR-1); a partially applied reconciliation is visible as journaled per-ID state, never as an advanced revision.
- The tap/handling journal is local and bounded (FR-8); no unbounded growth.

### Interfaces and events

- Consumes: `forecast_changed(vehicle_id, revision)` and settings/history commit notifications; lifecycle signals (boot, foreground/resume, pre-background); permission-state changes; observed locale/date/time/timezone change signals; app/package upgrade detection.
- Emits: `reminders_reconciled` (§7 event contract) with the applied revision and per-ID summary reason codes after each completed reconciliation.
- Commands: sequential typed schedule/cancel envelopes with correlation IDs through the DeviceCommandBroker; replay-idempotent by stable notification ID.
- Tap/launch handoff: `mob_notify` notification event/launch handoff routes into current dashboard truth (FR-5, FR-8).

### Failure and recovery behavior

- Idempotent convergence: any finite sequence of edits, permission changes, retries, duplicate lifecycle messages, and app restarts converges to the same desired/applied mapping without duplicate notifications (AC-1).
- Partial failure: desired state intact, journaled per-ID errors, nonalarming "Reminders need attention", retry at next foreground or explicit Retry (FR-7).
- Broker timeout/root-screen restart: operations remain unapplied and safely retryable (FR-2); no half-marked applied revision.
- Permission revocation: applied entries marked uncertain, cancellation attempted where permitted, new scheduling stopped (FR-7).
- Enumeration-unavailable degradation: trust only successfully journaled IDs; issue idempotent cancels/schedules; the limitation is documented (FR-6).
- Reboot/upgrade: if the plugin/host does not restore alarms, the scoped native receiver enhancement or a documented release blocker applies; until next app launch the BEAM cannot repair the plan and must not pretend otherwise (FR-9).

### Security and privacy

- Journals and logs record IDs, instants, states, and reason codes only — never notification body text, vehicle nickname, mileage, notes, or VIN (consistent with `DOS-M06-005` FR-5/FR-6 and §7 redaction rules).
- The tap journal stores route/purpose/logical ID data only, bounded in size (FR-8).
- No network, no remote logging, no push: reconciliation is entirely local (rules 2 and 8).

### Accessibility and performance

- Accessibility: N/A — no new UI in this issue; the "Reminders need attention" state it exposes is rendered accessibly by `DOS-M06-007`.
- Performance: reconciliation completes quickly enough to run in the pre-background window when lifecycle time permits (FR-2); diffs are bounded by the M06 capacity constant (at most three forecast reminders plus one test), so command sequences are short; trigger coalescing prevents redundant native churn from event bursts.

## UX and content states

- This issue defines the state data behind user-visible reminder states (rendered in `DOS-M06-007`): applied-and-converged, needs-attention (partial failure with Retry), permission-revoked-uncertain, and reboot/upgrade-pending-repair.
- Stale-tap experience: tapping an obsolete notification opens the current dashboard showing current truth — no archived dates, no error screens (FR-8).
- Due-now collapse after clock jumps: users see at most one due-now intent, never a burst of missed leads (FR-4 with `DOS-M06-005` FR-3).
- Locale change: copy language may change; scheduled semantics (dates/times) do not (FR-4).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC-1: Any finite sequence of forecast edits, permission changes, retries, duplicate lifecycle messages, and app restarts converges to the same desired/applied mapping without duplicate notifications.
- [ ] AC-2: Spring gap, fall overlap, timezone travel, date-line crossing, leap day, month end, manual clock forward/back, and due-while-killed fixtures resolve to the specified UTC instant and user-visible local time.
- [ ] AC-3: On both physical platforms, a notification scheduled before termination displays while the app process is killed, and tapping it cold-launches to the current dashboard.
- [ ] AC-4: Editing service/mileage/interval or disabling reminders cancels or replaces obsolete requests before applied revision advances.
- [ ] AC-5: Android reboot and app upgrade behavior are tested and either pass or block release with a documented upstream/native remediation issue.
- [ ] AC-6: Every reconciliation writes a journal with per-ID results, and the stored timezone ID, UTC instant, offset, and tzdata version reproduce each conversion in evidence.

## Test plan

### Automated

- State-machine/property tests for reconciliation: random finite sequences of edits, permission changes, retries, duplicate lifecycle triggers, and restarts converge to the same desired/applied mapping (AC-1).
- Fake-adapter failure injection: per-operation failures, timeouts, and root-screen restarts leave desired state intact, journal errors, and remain retryable (FR-2, FR-7).
- tzdata golden suite: spring-forward gap (first valid instant after the gap), fall-back overlap (earlier occurrence), timezone travel, date-line crossing, leap day, month end, and manual clock forward/back fixtures resolving to specified UTC instants and local times (AC-2).
- Idempotency tests: replaying identical reconciliations makes no native calls and no database changes; duplicate events coalesce.
- Capacity tests: the plan never exceeds the conservative capacity constant (at most three forecast reminders plus one test in M06).
- Stale-tap routing tests: obsolete-revision payloads open current dashboard truth; malformed payloads open home safely.

### Manual/device matrix

- Physical iOS and Android matrix with timestamps and screen recordings: foreground, background, force-stop/killed, reboot (Android), and app upgrade states — including a notification scheduled before termination displaying while killed and cold-launch tap navigation (AC-3, AC-5).
- Device timezone change, manual clock forward/back, and DST transition walkthroughs verifying wall-clock preservation and full replan behavior (FR-3, FR-4).
- Native logs correlated to the local journal for each matrix cell (per-ID operation results matched to OS-visible behavior).
- Android reboot/package-replacement verification of the pinned `mob_notify` version, with the native receiver enhancement or documented release blocker as the outcome path (FR-9, AC-5).

## Rollout and rollback

- Ships inside the normal app build behind the existing reminders opt-in (off by default per `DOS-M06-005`); users without reminders enabled are unaffected.
- Rollback: reverting reconciliation logic must retain the idempotent-cancel capability so a subsequent build can clean up applied OS requests; journaled IDs make the applied set recoverable. Desired-plan and journal rows are inert local data under rollback and never corrupt `UserRepo` history.
- If AC-5 fails without a viable scoped native remediation, the documented release blocker stops the M07 release train — this is the designed rollback of the milestone-level claim, not silent degradation.

## Dependency rationale

- Blocked by `DOS-M06-003`: committed forecast revisions and coalesced `forecast_changed` events are the primary reconciliation triggers; without transactional revisions, convergence has no stable target.
- Blocked by `DOS-M06-005`: reconciliation diffs the desired plan against applied state using the deterministic IDs, adapter behaviour, and broker command paths that issue creates.
- Blocks `DOS-M06-007` (the reminder control center renders this issue's status and Retry), `DOS-M07-004` and `DOS-M07-007` (M07 release gates depend on proven reconciliation reliability), and `DOS-M08-004` (multi-vehicle allocation extends this capacity and convergence model).

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: the pinned `mob_notify` version may not restore Android alarms after reboot/package replacement; FR-9 pre-plans the two outcomes (smallest in-scope native receiver enhancement, or an explicit documented release blocker) so this cannot become silent scope creep.
- Risk: OS power-saving modes may delay display; out of scope to fix — the product guarantees correct scheduling intent and reconciliation, not exact display time (`DOS-M06-007` gate), and the matrix records power-saving mode per cell.
- Risk: native pending-request enumeration may be unavailable; FR-6's journal-trust degradation is the designed mitigation and must be documented.
- Decision (fixed by catalog): DST gap → first valid local instant after the gap; fall-back ambiguity → earlier occurrence; timezone change preserves wall-clock time; manual clock jumps force a full replan with due-now collapse.
- Decision (fixed by catalog): one supervised foreground worker; no background polling, foreground service, or killed-process execution promises.
- Open question: none — remaining unknowns (plugin reboot behavior) are structured as FR-9 verification outcomes, not open design questions.
