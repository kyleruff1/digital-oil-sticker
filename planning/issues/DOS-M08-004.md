## Outcome

Each eligible vehicle can have its own correctly routed reminders, while a deterministic global allocator stays below native pending-notification limits and makes unscheduled coverage visible.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

Naively scheduling three reminders per vehicle can exceed OS capacity, evict requests unpredictably, or route a reminder to the wrong vehicle. Capacity is an application-level resource: without a global allocator, adding vehicles silently degrades the core product promise (a reminder that fires for the right vehicle at the right time). This issue completes the multi-vehicle reminder story and blocks the multi-vehicle release.

## Functional requirements

- FR-1: Each active vehicle owns enabled/lead/privacy preferences and desired reminder candidates. Provide global master enable/disable and default settings for newly added vehicles, but changing defaults does not silently overwrite existing per-vehicle choices. One OS permission gates all.
- FR-2: Define a conservative configurable `platform_schedule_budget`, verified against current `mob_notify`/OS behavior before release; the initial design budget is 48 pending app requests with 4 reserved for test/recovery/system use, leaving 44 allocatable. If verified platform capacity is lower, use the lower value. Never rely on undocumented overflow eviction.
- FR-3: Allocation is pure and deterministic: discard past/ineligible candidates; sort vehicles by earliest estimated due date, then stable vehicle ID; allocate each vehicle's nearest future candidate once while slots remain; then sort all remaining candidates globally by fire instant, due date, lead priority, and stable key to fill remaining slots.
- FR-4: If eligible vehicles exceed the budget, earliest-due vehicles receive the slots.
- FR-5: Re-run allocation on every plan/garage/permission/time change.
- FR-6: Cap each vehicle at the three user-selected maintenance leads.
- FR-7: Archive/delete/disable immediately remove the vehicle's desired candidates and trigger idempotent cancellation.
- FR-8: Reused native IDs are forbidden until prior cancellation is journaled or an ID-generation namespace changes.
- FR-9: UI shows per vehicle Scheduled / Permission Needed / Not scheduled—device limit / Needs Attention, plus a global capacity summary.
- FR-10: Users with overflow receive a clear in-app list and can reduce leads; do not imply that unscheduled reminders will fire.
- FR-11: Notification payload contains stable vehicle ID/logical plan ID only; a privacy setting controls nickname/date copy.
- FR-12: Tap selects the exact vehicle and renders current truth. Deleted/archived/stale revisions use the safe garage path defined in `DOS-M08-002`.
- FR-13: Reconciliation extends `DOS-M06-006` and applies one global generation under a lock. Partial failure cannot mark unrelated operations successful incorrectly; retry converges.
- FR-14: The nearest-due allocation must remain stable under identical inputs to avoid cancel/reschedule churn.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

- `DigitalOilSticker.Reminders` owns candidate generation, the pure allocator, deterministic ID policy, the reconciliation generation, and status classification.
- The allocator is a pure function from (eligible candidates, budget, current instant) to an allocation plan; platform effects flow only through the `NotificationAdapter` behaviour and the `DeviceCommandBroker`/root-`Mob.Screen` path to `MobNotify` (never a Phoenix socket passed to a plugin).
- Settings UI (per-vehicle preferences, master switch, quiet time, capacity summary) lives in the reminder/settings LiveViews within the `DOS-M08-002` navigation contract.
- Capacity verification against `mob_notify`/OS behavior is a release-gated checklist item owned by this issue.

### Data and persistence

- `reminder_rules` remains the per-vehicle preference store (kind, lead value/unit, preferred local time, enabled flag); add per-vehicle privacy preference and global defaults/master state in explicit fields, not implicit code defaults.
- `scheduled_notifications` remains the journal: stable notification ID primary key, vehicle/cycle/rule IDs, desired and scheduled instants, due-date snapshot, payload version, platform state, last error/reconciliation time, plus the allocation generation identifier.
- The configured `platform_schedule_budget` (total 48, reserved 4, allocatable 44, or the lower verified value) is explicit configuration with its verification evidence referenced.
- Cancellation journaling precedes any native-ID reuse; an ID-generation namespace change is the documented alternative.

### Interfaces and events

- Inputs: per-vehicle due dates/revisions and reminder-state summaries from `DOS-M08-003`; garage lifecycle events from `DOS-M08-001` (archive/delete/disable); permission and time/timezone change signals; global master switch changes.
- Output events: `reminders_reconciled` carrying generation, per-vehicle statuses, and reason codes (no display strings).
- Notification payload schema: payload version, stable vehicle ID, logical plan ID; nickname/date copy included only when the privacy setting allows.
- Tap routing uses the `DOS-M08-002` deep-link contract: exact vehicle selection, current forecast revision render, safe garage path for deleted/archived/stale targets.

### Failure and recovery behavior

- Reconciliation applies one global generation under a lock; partial native failure marks only the affected operations failed, cannot mark unrelated operations successful incorrectly, and retry converges to the desired allocation.
- Idempotent cancellation: repeating a cancellation (after crash or double-trigger) is safe; archive/delete converge even if the app dies mid-reconciliation.
- Permission revocation moves all vehicles to Permission Needed without deleting desired candidates; re-grant restores allocation on the next reconciliation.
- Identical inputs must produce byte-identical allocation plans so reconciliation performs no churny cancel/reschedule cycles.

### Security and privacy

- Payloads carry stable IDs only by default; the privacy setting gates nickname/date copy on the lock screen. No VIN, odometer, or notes in any payload.
- No remote push, device tokens, or notification server (rule 8). All scheduling data stays on device; logs redact vehicle identifiers per master prompt §7.

### Accessibility and performance

- Overflow, native failure, and permission states are accessible: status labels are text (not color alone), announced by screen readers, and reachable in garage/reminder screens.
- Allocation and reconciliation for 50+ vehicles complete within the foreground-recompute budget established by M07; no perpetual background process.

## UX and content states

- Per-vehicle status: Scheduled / Permission Needed / Not scheduled—device limit / Needs Attention.
- Global capacity summary showing allocatable budget usage.
- Overflow state: clear in-app list of unscheduled coverage with guidance to reduce leads; never implies unscheduled reminders will fire.
- Global master switch and quiet-time settings; per-vehicle settings screens.
- Privacy setting controlling notification copy.
- Tap outcomes: exact vehicle dashboard at current truth, or the safe garage explanation for deleted/archived/stale targets.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC-1: For 0, 1, 15, 50, and over-budget vehicles, the allocator never exceeds the configured budget, duplicates an ID, or silently omits status.
- [ ] AC-2: When capacity is sufficient, every enabled eligible vehicle receives at least its nearest reminder before any vehicle receives a second.
- [ ] AC-3: Changes to one forecast, archive/delete, global disable, permission revocation, timezone change, and oil-change completion converge without affecting wrong-vehicle routes.
- [ ] AC-4: A notification tap in foreground/background/killed launch selects only the encoded existing vehicle and shows current forecast revision.
- [ ] AC-5: Overflow, native failure, and permission states are accessible and visible in garage/reminder screens.
- [ ] AC-6: The configured `platform_schedule_budget` is verified against current `mob_notify`/OS behavior before release, uses the lower of design (44 allocatable of 48 with 4 reserved) and verified platform capacity, and never relies on undocumented overflow eviction.
- [ ] AC-7: Identical inputs produce identical allocation plans (no cancel/reschedule churn), and native IDs are never reused before journaled cancellation or a namespace change.

## Test plan

### Automated

- Allocation property/golden tests: determinism, budget ceiling, fairness (nearest reminder per vehicle before any second), tie-breaking by stable keys, over-budget earliest-due selection.
- 50+/over-budget fake-adapter stress suite exercising reconciliation generations, partial failure, retry convergence, and idempotent cancellation.
- Cancellation/ID collision audit tests: forbid reuse before journaled cancellation; namespace-change path covered.
- Event-driven convergence tests for forecast change, archive/delete, global disable, permission revocation, timezone change, and oil-change completion.
- Payload schema tests including the privacy setting.

### Manual/device matrix

- Real-device pending schedule inspection at capacity on physical iOS and Android: fill to budget, inspect the OS pending list, verify count, IDs, and fire ordering.
- Killed-app cross-vehicle tap matrix: taps for multiple vehicles in foreground, background, and killed states on both platforms, including deleted/archived targets hitting the safe garage path.
- Permission-revocation and re-grant walk-through on both platforms.
- Accessibility snapshots of overflow, failure, and permission states with VoiceOver/TalkBack.

## Rollout and rollback

- Ships with the multi-vehicle release train; the allocator replaces the single-vehicle scheduling path atomically at first reconciliation after upgrade, journaled under one generation.
- Budget verification against `mob_notify`/OS behavior is a release gate; a lower verified capacity lowers the configured budget before release.
- Rollback is a corrective app release; because desired state and the journal live in `UserRepo`, a reconciliation pass under the corrected binary converges the native schedule.

## Dependency rationale

- `DOS-M06-005` and `DOS-M06-006` (blockers): this issue generalizes the v1 scheduling behavior and explicitly extends the `DOS-M06-006` reconciliation contract; building a parallel reconciler would violate it.
- `DOS-M08-001` (blocker): archive exclusion from scheduling, ownership of `reminder_rules`/`scheduled_notifications`, and garage lifecycle events are its outputs.
- `DOS-M08-002` (blocker): tap routing depends on the deep-link/safe-garage contract.
- `DOS-M08-003` (blocker): allocator inputs are per-vehicle due dates and revisions; without isolation, fairness ordering would be computed from unsound data.
- Blocks the multi-vehicle release: visible, correctly routed, budget-safe reminders are an epic exit-gate condition.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: actual platform pending-notification capacity is below the design budget of 48; mitigated by mandatory pre-release verification and taking the lower value.
- Risk: allocation instability causing cancel/reschedule churn and OS throttling; mitigated by determinism requirements and golden tests.
- Risk: `MobNotify`/bridge behavior differences across platforms for pending-request inspection; device evidence is required on both.
- Decision: quiet-time semantics interact with lead times (shift versus suppress); resolved in this issue with the settings specification.
- Open question: exact reserved-slot usage policy for the 4 test/recovery/system slots; documented with the budget configuration.
