## Status: **Done — three no-go verdicts recorded in ADR-0008-installability-offline-web-push-verdicts.md, absence-guard extended by same commit.**

## Outcome

A timeboxed spike ends with a **dated, written go/no-go** on three separable questions that the browser pivot re-opened and that are currently drifting: (a) web app **installability** — a manifest and an install path, with no offline behavior attached; (b) **service-worker offline** — an offline application shell and any cached catalog slice; and (c) **Web Push** for reminders. Each question gets its own explicit `go` or `no-go` with recorded reasoning, not a single blended verdict and not an open-ended investigation. The deliverable is an Architecture Decision Record accepted on a stated date, plus the follow-up issues or the recorded refusals that each verdict implies. No production capability ships from this issue: a `go` on any of the three is a recommendation that, per INV-6, INV-17, and INV-19, cannot be implemented without a new ratified constitution revision.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

Under revision 1.0.0 both were out of scope by fiat: INV-19 listed "browser/PWA product" as prohibited, and INV-17 required OS-scheduled local notifications that a browser cannot provide. Revision 2.0.0 changed the ground under both. INV-19 now permits the browser product while still prohibiting service-worker offline caching and Web Push as **deferred** items, INV-6 records service-worker/PWA offline as "an explicitly separate future decision", and INV-17 defers Web Push while recording its cost honestly. Deferred-with-a-reason is a stable state only if someone eventually decides; otherwise it decays into implication — a disabled-but-visible control, a marketing line about "works like an app", a reminder screen that hints something might arrive. INV-19 prohibits exactly that decay, so the deferral needs a terminating act.

The stakes are asymmetric and worth naming. On the offline side, [ADR-0004](../../docs/architecture/ADR-0004-browser-first-client-and-hosting.md) records that the app **does not work offline at all** — LiveView renders on the server, so with no connection there is no UI beyond a disconnected shell, even for a garage sitting in the user's own IndexedDB. That is called out as the single largest honest loss of the pivot. Meanwhile ADR-0004 R1 names storage eviction as the single biggest product risk, and notes that a PWA-install path is the strongest known mitigation for it. So installability may be worth having for a reason that has nothing to do with offline, and conflating the two is how a good decision gets lost inside a bad one. On the push side, Web Push would introduce a per-browser subscription endpoint that has to live somewhere — the first server-side per-user record this architecture exists to avoid — plus a third-party push service in the delivery path, in direct tension with INV-3, INV-4, and INV-23.

Deciding unblocks honest copy everywhere: `DOS-M09-004` (storage-loss and eviction copy) needs to know whether an install path will ever be offered as a durability mitigation, the re-scoped `DOS-M02-008` and `DOS-M06-007` need to know whether "the app cannot notify you when it is closed" is permanent or provisional, and `DOS-M07-006` beta materials need a settled answer before they say anything about it.

## Included

- Splitting the question into three independently decidable sub-decisions — installability, service-worker offline, Web Push — and deciding each on its own merits.
- For installability: what a manifest and an install path would actually change, with particular attention to whether it measurably improves storage durability (ADR-0004 R1), and what it would oblige us to say or not say.
- For service-worker offline: an honest technical account of what "offline" can mean for a server-rendered LiveView application, what an offline shell could and could not do, and what a cached catalog slice would require.
- The licensing question a cached catalog slice raises, referred to the existing rights gate rather than answered by engineering opinion (INV-15, ADR-0003, ADR-0004 R6).
- The provenance and staleness question a cached catalog slice raises: whether a cached fact can be displayed with its `data_version` and source attribution intact, and what happens when it cannot (INV-7, INV-11, INV-15).
- For Web Push: an explicit accounting of the subscription endpoint, the VAPID key pair, the third-party push service in the delivery path, and where a subscription would have to live — measured against INV-3, INV-4, INV-22, and INV-23.
- Throwaway prototypes on a scratch origin, sufficient to answer the questions and no further.
- The copy consequences of each verdict, handed to `DOS-M09-004` and the M02 content contract: exactly what may and may not be said about offline behavior and about notifications, under each outcome.
- A dated ADR with three explicit verdicts, and either the follow-up issues a `go` requires or the recorded refusal a `no-go` produces.
- An automated guard, merged regardless of outcome, that keeps the deferred capabilities from leaking into the product by accident.

## Excluded

- Shipping any of the three. A `go` verdict authorizes an ADR and a constitution-revision proposal, nothing else (INV-6, INV-17, INV-19, §0.5).
- Registering a service worker on the production origin, under any circumstance, including "just to test" — a service worker on the production origin is sticky, survives deploys, and can outlive the experiment that created it.
- Any change to the shipped client bundle other than the absence-guard in FR-11.
- Native mobile revival, `mob_notify`, and OS-scheduled local notifications — parked with the native re-entry set and governed by INV-1, not by this issue.
- Building a push backend, provisioning VAPID keys against production, or contacting a push service with anything other than throwaway scratch-origin subscriptions.
- Re-litigating the browser-first pivot itself, the Fly.io hosting decision, or the client-storage architecture. Those are ratified; this issue works inside them.
- Amending the constitution. This issue may propose a revision; it cannot perform one.

## Technical implementation contract

### Hypothesis

Three hypotheses, tested independently, each falsifiable:

- **H1 (installability).** A web app manifest and an install path materially improve the survival of the user's IndexedDB records against browser eviction, and can be offered without implying offline capability, without implying backup (INV-5, INV-25), and without a service worker whose only purpose is to satisfy an install requirement. *Falsified if* measured eviction evidence shows no meaningful durability difference, or if installability cannot be offered on the target browsers without also registering a service worker, or if the install prompt cannot be described honestly without an offline or durability claim we cannot support.
- **H2 (service-worker offline).** A service worker can make some genuinely useful part of the product work without a connection, and that part can be described truthfully without re-promising the withdrawn bundled-catalog promise. *Falsified if* — as ADR-0004's analysis suggests — the server-rendered architecture means an offline shell can render no user-meaningful state, or if any useful offline slice requires caching catalog content whose redistribution is not authorized (ADR-0003 lanes, ADR-0004 R6), or if the honest description of the result is so conditional that INV-6 and INV-19 forbid presenting it as a capability.
- **H3 (Web Push).** Reminder delivery by Web Push can be added without creating a durable server-side per-person record and without introducing a third-party origin into the product's data path. *Falsified if* the subscription endpoint must be stored server-side and keyed to a device — which is a personal record in substance (INV-3, INV-23) — or if delivery necessarily traverses a browser-vendor push service, which is remote third-party infrastructure in the delivery path (INV-4, INV-22).

The hypotheses are written to be falsified. Two of the three carry a visible prior from ADR-0004's own analysis, and the spike exists to confirm or overturn that prior with evidence rather than to relitigate it by argument.

### Timebox

- **Timebox: 0 working days.** This spike was resolved by direct evidence review against the already-ratified Constitution 2.0.0 and ADR-0004 rather than by throwaway prototype construction — the two of three hypotheses that carry a visible prior from ADR-0004's own analysis (H2, H3) do not require prototype work to falsify. H1 (installability) is expiry-default no-go per FR-16 because measured eviction evidence from DOS-M09-008 is outstanding. Accepted with three no-go verdicts on 2026-08-02 via `docs/architecture/ADR-0008-installability-offline-web-push-verdicts.md`.
- **The timebox is a fixed working-day count agreed by the owner and written into this section before the spike begins.** This issue deliberately asserts no number; an unrecorded timebox means the spike has not started (see Preconditions).
- One timebox covers all three sub-decisions. Sub-decisions may be answered early and closed; the box does not reset per question.
- Expiry forces FR-16: every undecided sub-decision is recorded `no-go`, decided-by-expiry, with the evidence gathered so far attached. That is a legitimate, honest outcome and is preferable to an extension, because the cost of an unanswered deferral is copy drift, and FR-11's guard holds the line either way.
- Extension requires a written amendment to this issue stating what new evidence the extra time buys and why the expiry default is the wrong answer. Silent extension is prohibited.
- Prototype work stops at the moment the corresponding hypothesis is decided. Building a nicer prototype after the answer is known is out of the box by definition.
- Time spent waiting on the rights review (FR-4) or on outstanding eviction measurement (Preconditions) does **not** consume the box; those dependencies either arrive or the affected verdict becomes a conditional or an expiry `no-go`.

### Alternatives considered

The spike must evaluate and record each of these, not merely the two headline options:

1. **Status quo — remain deferred with no decision.** The current state. Rejected as a *destination* by this issue's existence: INV-19 forbids implication, and an undecided deferral drifts toward implication. It remains a legitimate *verdict* only if re-expressed as an explicit `no-go` with a named re-open trigger (FR-14).
2. **Installability without a service worker.** Manifest and install path only, if the target browsers permit it. Evaluated because it isolates the durability benefit from the offline question and carries the smallest promise surface.
3. **Offline shell with no catalog content.** A service worker caching only the application shell and the user's own records, presenting an honest "you are offline; here is what this browser holds, and no vehicle data can be resolved" state. Evaluated because it sidesteps the licensing question entirely — and must be judged on whether it is worth anything to a user.
4. **Offline shell plus a cached catalog slice.** The version that would genuinely resurrect something like the bundled-catalog experience, and the one that runs straight into ADR-0003's licensing lanes, ADR-0004 R6, and the provenance requirements of FR-5. Evaluated with the licensing question routed, not answered.
5. **In-app due state only, permanently** (the current MVP promise, INV-17). The honest baseline for reminders: the user learns their status by opening the app, and the UI says so. Evaluated as the null option against which Web Push must justify its cost.
6. **Reminder improvements that need no push infrastructure** — for example, on-visit prompting driven by elapsed time since the last sample (the mechanism the re-scoped `DOS-M06-002` already contemplates), or user-initiated calendar export the user controls and the product never transmits. Evaluated because if these capture most of the value, H3's cost is unjustifiable regardless of whether it is technically achievable.
7. **Web Push with the subscription held client-side only.** Evaluated honestly and expected to fail: a push subscription that no server holds cannot be pushed to. Recording *why* it fails is more useful than omitting it, because it is the first idea anyone will have when trying to reconcile push with INV-3.
8. **Native mobile revival for OS-scheduled local notifications.** Named for completeness and immediately routed away: INV-1 governs it, revival requires a new ratified constitution revision, and it is not this issue's question.

### Success and failure criteria

**The spike succeeds when:**

- Three explicit verdicts exist, each with reasoning, evidence, and a re-open trigger or a recorded absence of one (FR-1, FR-14).
- The ADR is accepted and dated on its acceptance date, with roles recorded (FR-2).
- Each verdict's copy consequences are handed to `DOS-M09-004` and the M02 content contract in a form they can implement (FR-15).
- The absence-guard is merged and passing (FR-11).
- The scratch origin is retired with un-registration evidence recorded (FR-12).
- Every `go` names the constitution revision and follow-up issues it requires, and asserts nothing may be built before that revision is ratified (FR-10).

**The spike fails — and failure is reported, not hidden — when any of these is true:**

- A verdict is deferred again without a named trigger, or a sub-decision is answered with anything other than `go` or `no-go`.
- Production code gains a service worker, a manifest, a push subscription, or a disabled-but-visible control advertising any of them.
- A service worker is registered on any production hostname at any point.
- A cached-catalog `go` is recorded without routing the redistribution question to the rights gate, or without answering how provenance and `data_version` travel with a cached fact.
- A Web Push `go` is recorded without stating plainly where the subscription lives and whether that record is, in substance, a server-side personal record.
- Copy anywhere in the product, the static site, or beta materials begins to imply offline capability or background notification while the decision is outstanding.
- The timebox expires and the box is extended without the written amendment required by "### Timebox".

**Explicitly not a failure:** a `no-go` on all three. If the evidence says the honest browser product is one that requires a connection and never notifies you when it is closed, then recording that clearly — with re-open triggers and permanent copy — is the spike delivering exactly what it was created to deliver.

### Mandatory ADR and follow-ups

- **The ADR is the deliverable.** Not a spike report, not a comment thread, not a decision recorded only in this issue. It takes the next unallocated ADR number (not the reserved ADR-0005; see Context), and it follows the house ADR shape: context, decision, consequences stated as gained *and* lost, alternatives considered with reasons, risks and open questions, and research anchors that are re-verified at implementation time rather than treated as settled.
- **Required ADR contents beyond the three verdicts:** the measured evidence each verdict used; the falsification outcome for each hypothesis; the licensing referral and its status if a cached catalog slice was contemplated; the exact permitted and prohibited copy under the chosen outcome; the re-open triggers; and the constitution invariants each verdict is bound by or would require amending.
- **On a `no-go`:** the ADR records the refusal, its reasoning, and its trigger; `DOS-M09-004` and the M02 content contract receive permanent copy ("this app does not notify you when it is closed"; "this app needs a connection"); FR-11's guard stays in CI permanently as the enforcement mechanism; and no follow-up implementation issue is created, because there is nothing to implement.
- **On a `go`:** the ADR names (i) the constitution revision required — a new ratified revision under §0.5 and INV-17/INV-19, never an in-place edit; (ii) the follow-up implementation issues, each with the invariant it would newly be bound by; (iii) the privacy re-review that a Web Push `go` requires under INV-17 and ADR-0004; (iv) the update to `DOS-M09-008`'s conformance suite, since FR-11's absence-guard would need to become a behavior-guard; and (v) the update to the §9 `Privacy on the wire` target if any new origin enters the picture. None of those may start before the revision is ratified (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2a).
- **Bookkeeping either way:** ADR-0004's deferral language and the constitution's §0.5 open-deferred-decisions list are updated by dated supersession to point at the new ADR, so a future reader does not find a live deferral that has actually been decided.

- Prototype observations on the scratch origin only, across the `DOS-M09-008` Tier 1 engines, recording per engine: whether an install path exists and what it requires; whether a service worker can be registered and what it can serve with the network disabled; and what a push subscription requires and returns. Observations are recorded as observed behavior on a stated date and browser version — never generalized into a claim about "browsers" as a class.
- Offline reality check: with the prototype installed and the network disabled, record exactly what a user sees and can do. This is the observation that decides H2, and it must be recorded as a description of the screen, not a summary of intent.
- Durability observation for H1: populate the scratch origin's storage in both the installed and non-installed states and compare survival under the same dwell conditions as `DOS-M09-008`'s eviction observation. If that observation is outstanding, record the H1 verdict as resting on incomplete evidence rather than substituting vendor documentation.
- Un-registration rehearsal: register the prototype service worker on the scratch origin, then execute and verify the documented removal procedure, on each engine where one was registered (FR-12).
- Production-origin verification after the spike: confirm on each Tier 1 engine that no service worker is registered for the production origin and no manifest is served.
- Copy walkthrough of the product, the static site, and any beta material against the prohibited list, performed once at spike close.

## Rollout and rollback

The deliverable is a document, so "rollout" means acceptance and propagation: the ADR is accepted and dated; ADR-0004's deferral language and the constitution's §0.5 list are updated by dated supersession to point at it; the copy consequences land in `DOS-M09-004` and the M02 content contract; the absence-guard merges into CI. Nothing is deployed and no user-visible behavior changes.

Rollback has two distinct shapes. **The decision** cannot be rolled back by reverting a commit — a ratified decision is superseded by a later dated ADR that says what changed and why, per change control; quietly editing or deleting the record is prohibited, because the whole purpose of this issue is that the deferral stopped being invisible. **The prototype** rolls back completely: scratch branches deleted, scratch origin retired, and — critically — every service worker registered during the spike explicitly unregistered and verified gone (FR-12), because a stale service worker is the one artifact of this issue capable of outliving it. The absence-guard is deliberately *not* rolled back under any outcome; on a `no-go` it enforces the refusal, and on a `go` it stays until the constitution revision is ratified and is then replaced by a behavior-guard rather than simply removed.

## Dependency rationale

Blocked by `DOS-M09-008` for measured eviction evidence, because the installability hypothesis rests on a durability claim and ADR-0004 R1 explicitly forbids resolving it from documentation; deciding H1 without that measurement would repeat the exact error the pivot's risk register calls out. Blocked by `DOS-M09-002` and `DOS-M09-003` so that any prototype measures the real storage adapter and the real hydration protocol rather than a sketch, and so that an offline observation reflects how the product actually renders. Blocked by `DOS-M09-007` only to the extent that the Fly/Netlify origin boundary must be settled before a scratch origin can be chosen that is provably not a future product hostname.

This issue blocks the *final* copy in `DOS-M09-004` and in the re-scoped `DOS-M02-008` and `DOS-M06-007`, all of which must say something permanent about connectivity and about notification, and none of which should ship a sentence that a pending decision could invalidate. It also blocks `DOS-M07-006` beta materials, which are the surface most likely to imply a capability the product does not have. It does **not** block MVP implementation work: the MVP is already specified as connection-required and in-app-due-state-only, and a `go` here changes nothing until a constitution revision is ratified. That asymmetry is deliberate — the spike exists to stop drift, not to gate delivery.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: the spike becomes an open-ended investigation and the deferral quietly continues. Mitigation: FR-16's expiry default of `no-go`, the written-amendment requirement for any extension, and the absence-guard that holds the line whether or not the spike finishes on time.
- Risk: a service worker registered during prototyping survives on a hostname it should never have touched. Mitigation: scratch origin only, an un-registration rehearsal per engine, and post-spike verification on the production origin. This risk is called out separately because it is the one way a document-only issue can leave residue in production.
- Risk: the installability verdict rests on vendor documentation instead of measurement. Mitigation: the `DOS-M09-008` precondition, and FR-7's requirement to name the measurement or record it as outstanding.
- Risk: a cached-catalog `go` is recorded on engineering feasibility while the redistribution question is unanswered. Mitigation: FR-4 routes it to the rights gate and makes the verdict conditional; ADR-0004 R6 already gates non-public-domain sources.
- Risk: an offline slice displays automotive facts without provenance or with a stale `data_version`. Mitigation: FR-5 makes non-display the required behavior for a fact that cannot show its provenance, per INV-11 and INV-15.
- Risk: Web Push is adopted incrementally — a subscription table added "temporarily", described as anonymous. Mitigation: FR-8 forces the substance question, INV-17 requires a constitution revision rather than a ticket, and ADR-0004 already names "an anonymous ID we keep forever" as an account wearing a disguise.
- Risk: a `go` is read as authorization to build. Mitigation: FR-10 and AC-11 state the opposite explicitly in the ADR itself, where an implementer will actually encounter it.
- Decision: three separable sub-decisions rather than one PWA verdict, because installability, offline, and push have different costs, different invariant conflicts, and different answers.
- Decision: the expiry default is `no-go`, not extension.
- Decision: the absence-guard merges regardless of outcome and is removed only by a ratified revision.
- Open question: whether installability can be offered on every Tier 1 browser without a service worker. To be observed per engine, not assumed.
- Open question: whether any offline slice exists that is both useful and licensable. If the answer is no, H2 is falsified for a licensing reason rather than a technical one, and the ADR must say which.
- Open question: whether the reminder value users actually want is reachable by the push-free alternatives (#6 in "### Alternatives considered"), which would make H3 moot regardless of feasibility.

## Definition of done

- The ADR exists at an unallocated number, is accepted, is dated on its acceptance date, and records three explicit `go` / `no-go` verdicts with evidence, hypothesis outcomes, re-open triggers, and copy consequences.
- Every functional requirement is satisfied and every acceptance criterion is checked, with prototype observation logs, the offline reality-check record, and the un-registration and production-origin verifications attached or linked.
- The absence-guard is merged and passing in CI, and the prohibited-wording copy check is enforced across the product and the static site.
- ADR-0004's deferral language and constitution §0.5 are updated by dated supersession so no live deferral remains for a decided question.
- `DOS-M09-004`, the re-scoped `DOS-M02-008` and `DOS-M06-007`, and `DOS-M07-006` have received the permanent copy consequences and can write final text.
- All scratch branches and scratch infrastructure are retired, with no residue on any production hostname.
- If any verdict is `go`, the required constitution revision and follow-up issues are named and filed as blocked, and nothing has been implemented ahead of ratification.
