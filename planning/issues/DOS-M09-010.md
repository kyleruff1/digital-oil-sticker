## Outcome

Every source that can contribute a fact to the shipped catalog carries an explicit, evidence-backed disposition for **serving its facts over a public network from the hosted application**, recorded per fact domain and per distribution mode, and separate from the offline-bundle clearance those sources were evaluated for under constitution revision 1.0.0. `docs/data/SOURCE_REGISTER.md`, the `DOS-M03-001` rights matrix, and `docs/data/LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md` are re-reviewed and amended; `config/data_sources.yml` gains a hosted-serving gate that CI and the deploy pipeline fail closed on; required attribution is specified with an explicit web placement and single-sourced from the register; and any source without hosted-serving permission degrades to an honest `identity_only`/`unsupported` state or user-entered data rather than being served.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

Constitution 2.0.0 amends INV-15's emphasis: the required right is now "a license permitting **serving the data over a network from a hosted service**, which is not automatically implied by a right to redistribute inside an installed application bundle," and it states that any source cleared under 1.0.0 for offline bundle redistribution MUST be re-reviewed against the hosted-serving use before it ships. `ADR-0004` records the same obligation as a hard gate (R6) and notes the reason it is materially different: broader reach, scrapable endpoints, and no packaging boundary. Every rights artifact in the repository today is written in the superseded language — `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` opens with "a license permitting offline redistribution," and `LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md` requires "offline end-user storage" and "inclusion in signed build/catalog artifacts," none of which describes what the product now does.

This is therefore a release-blocking gate, not documentation hygiene: **no production automotive claim may be served from the hosted app until this review completes for the source behind it.** It unblocks production enablement of the licensed adapters (`DOS-M03-007` schedules/fluids, `DOS-M03-008` EOLCS products, `DOS-M03-009` filter fitment), production completion of the catalog release (`DOS-M03-010`), the read-only catalog query layer that will actually serve those claims, the Fly deploy that bakes the catalog artifact into an image, and the attribution surface required by INV-27. Without it the product would either serve facts under a right it does not hold or stall the entire vendoring thesis on an unanswered legal question — and under CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 4 an unresolved licensing question is never allowed to become an implementation assumption.

## Functional requirements

- FR-1: Every source in `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` and every row of the `DOS-M03-001` matrix carries a `hosted_serving` disposition from the enum `permitted`, `permitted_with_conditions`, `pending`, `prohibited`, `not_required_public_domain`. Ambiguity, missing terms, and "no explicit terms found" all map to `pending`, never to `permitted`. (INV-15; CHANGE_CONTROL Rules 2b and 4.)
- FR-2: The disposition is recorded **per fact domain**, using the nine domains established by `DOS-M03-001` FR-2 (vehicle identity/configuration; normal/severe oil interval; time/mileage/condition-based schedule; OEM lubricant specification; viscosity; capacity/context; EOLCS license/product claim; filter brand/product; vehicle-filter fitment), so a source may be servable for one field and prohibited for another. (INV-15, INV-11.)
- FR-3: The disposition is recorded **per distribution mode**: `bundle_redistribution`, `hosted_render` (server-side query and rendering of derived facts to a browser), `bulk_export`, `machine_readable_serving`. `bulk_export` and `machine_readable_serving` default to `prohibited` unless terms expressly permit them, preserving the ADR-0004 alternative-4 rejection. The pre-existing `bundle_redistribution` answer is retained unchanged, not overwritten. (INV-15, INV-1.)
- FR-4: Every non-`pending` disposition is backed by captured evidence: terms locator, the specific clause relied on, `terms_captured_at`, `terms_sha256`, and the reviewer. Payment, public visibility without a login, a prior offline clearance, or a marketing or sales statement is never sufficient evidence. (INV-15; CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2b.)
- FR-5: Green-lane U.S. Government sources (NHTSA vPIC API and standalone downloads, EPA/DOE FuelEconomy.gov, EPA annual certification data) are re-reviewed for network serving specifically: the 17 U.S.C. §105 position is re-verified and recorded, and the agency disclaimer, attribution guidance, and any terms-of-service, acceptable-use, or automation clause on the API or download page is captured separately from the copyright position, because those clauses are not answered by §105. Existing register notes are re-verified, not inherited. (INV-15.)
- FR-6: Documented-facts-lane sources are re-reviewed on the explicit basis that what is served is discrete normalized facts in the project's original schema with original UI wording, and that no source prose, table layout, PDF, or protected image is served. Where a publisher's terms restrict onward publication of extracted values, the source records `prohibited` or `permitted_with_conditions` with the restriction stated. (INV-15, INV-16.)
- FR-7: Permission-needed-lane sources may reach `permitted` only where an offered download or API, written permission, or a supplier-provided file carries terms that name consumer-application or web serving. Absent that, the affected fact domain stays user-entered or `unsupported`; "visible without a login" remains not permission. (INV-15, INV-11.)
- FR-8: Attribution is specified for a web page per source: exact required text, whether it must appear adjacent to each claim or may be aggregated, its placement across the in-app provenance surface and the INV-27 static attribution page, trademark and logo constraints, and required links. `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` is the single source of truth for that text, and both surfaces are generated from or verified against it. (INV-15, INV-27, INV-21.)
- FR-9: API and automation terms are recorded with their binding point distinguished — acquisition-time versus serving-time. A term that requires transmitting a per-request identifier, a user identifier, or usage telemetry as a condition of serving is recorded as an architectural conflict and forces `prohibited` for hosted serving, with the conflicting invariants named; it is not worked around. (INV-3, INV-4, INV-26.)
- FR-10: Any access constraint implied by an approved term — rate limits, no-bulk clauses, caching limits, attribution-in-response — is translated into a named, implementable control with an owning issue, so the constraint reaches the query layer and the hardening work rather than living only in prose. (INV-15, INV-26; ADR-0004 R7.)
- FR-11: `config/data_sources.yml` is extended with `hosted_serving`, `hosted_serving_conditions`, `hosted_serving_evidence`, `distribution_modes` (the FR-3 map), `web_attribution_text`, `web_attribution_placement`, `attribution_adjacency`, `serving_time_terms`, `reviewed_for_hosted_at`, and `terms_sha256`. Every enum or boolean requires an evidence reference; none may be inferred from payment or public availability. (INV-15.)
- FR-12: The CI policy lint fails a production catalog build when an enabled source is `pending` or `prohibited` for `hosted_render`, is missing hosted-serving evidence, or has expired terms; the deploy gate applies the same predicate to the image about to ship, so a catalog artifact containing non-permitted rows cannot reach the hosted origin. Both fail closed and name the source and the failing predicate. (INV-15, INV-11.)
- FR-13: Every source that does not clear hosted serving has a recorded product fallback: the affected fact domain renders as INV-11 `identity_only` or `unsupported`, or as user-entered data labeled per INV-21 ("Source unavailable", "Exact configuration not verified", `User entered`). No generic interval, viscosity, capacity, product claim, or fitment is substituted, and no oil or filter product is presented as compatible on brand or viscosity alone. (INV-11, INV-15, INV-16, INV-20, INV-21.)
- FR-14: `SOURCE_REGISTER.md` and `LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md` are amended so the governing right is stated as the distribution the product actually performs, with hosted serving added to the per-lane checklists and to the one-time-snapshot rights list. The superseded 1.0.0 wording is preserved as a dated supersession with the reason it no longer holds, not silently edited away. (INV-15; CHANGE_CONTROL Rules 2b and 6.)
- FR-15: A coverage-delta report quantifies what the review removes: fact domains, makes, and model years that lose servable data versus the pre-review matrix, and the honest UI state each loss maps to. Any advertised coverage claim is bounded by this report. (INV-8, INV-10, INV-11, INV-15.)
- FR-16: The completed review is signed by the product owner and the authorized legal/business reviewer and recorded as a Rule 2b change-control artifact. Engineers contribute evidence capture and tooling only. (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2b.)

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

- `docs/data/SOURCE_REGISTER.md` — human-readable register and the single source of truth for attribution text. Dispositions owned by the product owner plus the authorized legal/business reviewer; engineers contribute evidence, never legal conclusions.
- `docs/data/LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md` — per-lane checklists; gains a hosted-serving section and hosted-serving items in the one-time-snapshot list.
- `docs/data/source-rights-matrix.md` (`DOS-M03-001`) — the per-source, per-domain matrix this issue extends with the FR-3 mode axis.
- `config/data_sources.yml` — machine-readable mirror consumed by the ingest adapters, the catalog compiler, the CI policy lint, and the deploy gate. Owned by the data workstream with product/legal review on status changes.
- CI policy lint and the deploy gate — owned by the pipeline boundary; this issue supplies the predicate, the deploy pipeline issue consumes it.
- Catalog compiler (`DOS-M03-010`) — its license/attribution report gains the hosted-serving disposition and the web attribution placement per included source.
- Attribution surfaces — the in-app provenance rendering on the Fly origin and the static attribution page on the Netlify origin (INV-27); both consume register-derived text and neither may restate an automotive fact lacking a provenance record.

### Data and persistence

- No personal data is created, read, or stored anywhere in this issue; there is no user-facing write path and no server-side personal row (INV-3, INV-23).
- Persistence is Git-versioned documents plus `config/data_sources.yml` plus the sanitized terms-evidence archive, each evidence artifact stored with `terms_captured_at` and `terms_sha256` so a later dispute can prove which terms were reviewed.
- Downstream, the `data_sources` catalog table carries the hosted-serving disposition and the web attribution text alongside the existing provenance columns, so a served claim can demonstrate its right and its attribution at render time rather than by reference to a document.

### Interfaces and events

- Adapters and the compiler resolve a `source_key` and now also its `hosted_serving` and `distribution_modes` values before any row may compile into a released catalog.
- The catalog query layer returns attribution and provenance alongside every claim; adding attribution must not add any personal parameter, identifier, or free-text field to a query (INV-26).
- The deploy gate evaluates the same predicate against the artifact baked into the image; a non-permitted row blocks the release rather than being filtered at request time.
- The static attribution page is regenerated from the register on change; a drift check is a release gate (INV-27).
- Status transitions are ordinary reviewed pull requests referencing evidence. There are no runtime events and no runtime license check against any provider.

### Failure and recovery behavior

- Fail closed everywhere: missing evidence, ambiguous terms, expired terms, or an unreviewed source stops the production build and the deploy with a diagnostic naming the source, the fact domain, and the failing predicate.
- Terms change or revocation after launch re-gates the affected domain: the flag flips to the documented fallback in the same change, the affected rows are excluded from the next `data_version`, and the change ships as a versioned catalog correction with an affected-record report and a user-impact note — never an invisible edit (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 6).
- A review that concludes a source cannot be served produces a coverage reduction with honest UI states. It never produces a substituted default value (INV-15).
- A takedown or rights complaint is handled by disabling the source flag and redeploying; the register keeps the row with its dated disposition and the reason.
- Loss of approval evidence is treated as `pending`, never as tacit approval.

### Security and privacy

- No provider credential or API key exists at runtime: acquisition runs at build time in GitHub Actions, not in the request path (ADR-0004). A source whose terms require live runtime fetching is architecturally incompatible and is recorded as such.
- Attribution must introduce no third-party origin. Vendor-hosted logos, fonts, scripts, and beacons are prohibited; required marks are self-hosted first-party assets or text, consistent with the CSP (INV-22, INV-4).
- A licensor requirement for usage reporting, per-request identification, or any callback that would correlate visits is unsatisfiable under INV-3, INV-4, and INV-26 and forces `prohibited` rather than a negotiated exception.
- The terms-evidence archive is sanitized: no credentials in URLs, no personal contact data beyond what procurement requires. A secret scan runs over the register, the YAML, and the archive.
- The static attribution page collects no personal data, carries no tracker, and implies no account or stored profile (INV-27, INV-3, INV-5).

### Accessibility and performance

- This issue ships no new interactive surface, but it constrains two that exist: attribution text must be short and structured enough to render within the first-visit payload budget (constitution §9: initial HTML + CSS + JS ≤ 250 KiB compressed, zero third-party origins), and every attribution and provenance surface it specifies must meet the §9 accessibility row — WCAG 2.2 AA, full keyboard operability with a visible focus indicator, usable at 200% zoom and 320 CSS px reflow width, and screen-reader operable.
- Aggregated attribution must be reachable from every claim surface by a persistent link, so an adjacency-optional source still satisfies its terms without inflating each claim.

### Data change specifics

- **Source/version/license:** each register row records provider, dataset or document name and revision, license or terms name and version or `not_found`, canonical locator, `terms_captured_at`, `terms_sha256`, and the FR-1/FR-2/FR-3 dispositions. Green-lane rows additionally record the §105 position and, separately, any terms-of-service or acceptable-use clause captured at review time.
- **Provenance fields:** a served claim carries source id, source version or document revision, retrieved and verified dates, market and effective model-year context, the ADR-0003 confidence tier, and the attribution string. Provenance is rendered to the user, not merely stored (INV-15, INV-20).
- **Rejected-source policy:** a source that fails hosted-serving review is never deleted from the register. It keeps its row with `prohibited` or `pending`, the reason, the evidence, and the dated disposition; its facts are excluded from compilation by the policy lint; the affected fact domain records its fallback. Rejected rows appear in the review report and in the license/attribution report — nothing is silently dropped.
- **Mapping:** no new normalization or crosswalk work. The review changes only which already-normalized rows may compile into a served catalog, and adds the hosted-serving and attribution columns to the `data_sources` table.
- **Constraints:** the policy lint enforces that every enabled source has a non-`pending` `hosted_render` disposition with evidence and a non-empty attribution record where its terms require attribution.
- **Idempotency:** re-running the lint over the same YAML and the same evidence hashes yields an identical pass/fail result and an identical report.
- **Checksums:** SHA-256 per captured terms artifact; catalog payload hashing is unchanged in mechanism and simply covers a possibly smaller row set.
- **Coverage report:** pre- and post-review servable coverage by fact domain and by make and model year, with the delta attributable to this review, per FR-15.
- **Update cadence:** the review re-runs on any terms change, on any new source, on `expires_on`, and at each catalog `data_version` release; it is not a one-time act.
- **Rollback/recovery:** revert to the previous approved revision of the register and YAML; a status downgrade flips the fallback flag in the same change and produces a new catalog version rather than editing a released one (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 6).

## UX and content states

This issue ships no screens but fixes content states other issues must render. Every served claim shows its source attribution in the placement its terms require, with the aggregated attribution page always reachable (INV-15, INV-27). A fact domain whose source did not clear hosted serving renders as `unsupported` or the vehicle renders as `identity_only`, with "Source unavailable" and, where configuration could not be resolved, "Exact configuration not verified" — never a fabricated interval, viscosity, capacity, product claim, or fitment (INV-11, INV-15, INV-21). User-entered substitutes are labeled `User entered` and never "Manufacturer recommended" or "Compatible" (INV-16, INV-21). A rights-driven gap and a failed catalog query are visually distinct: a connection problem is shown as a connection problem, never as "no data for this vehicle" (INV-7). Attribution and legal copy on the static boundary collect nothing, imply no account, and use "stored in this browser" framing wherever they touch user data at all (INV-5, INV-21, INV-25, INV-27).

## Rollout and rollback

Rollout is merging the approved revision of `SOURCE_REGISTER.md`, `LICENSING_CHECKLIST.md`, the rights matrix, and `config/data_sources.yml`, which immediately governs CI and the deploy gate; the first catalog release compiled afterward carries only servable rows. Because the gate fails closed, the practical sequence is that adapters and the compiler continue to run fixture-only for any domain still `pending`, and production enablement follows each disposition individually rather than waiting for the whole set. Rollback is reverting to the previous approved revision, which re-tightens rather than loosens the gate — a revert can only remove permissions, never grant them. A post-launch downgrade of any source flips its fallback flag in the same change and ships as a new catalog `data_version` with an affected-record report; already-served content is withdrawn by release, not by an in-place edit (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 6).

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: the review shrinks servable coverage, possibly materially, for schedules, fluids, product claims, or fitment. Mitigation: FR-13 and FR-15 make the loss explicit and route it to honest INV-11 states and user-entered fallbacks; the alternative — serving facts under a right the product does not hold — is not available. The measured loss is an input to procurement, not a reason to soften the gate.
- Risk: a source permits redistribution inside an application bundle but not network serving, which would have been invisible under the 1.0.0 framing. Mitigation: FR-3's per-mode record makes exactly this case expressible and preserves the bundle answer for a future native revival (INV-1).
- Risk: public availability of a government dataset is mistaken for permission to serve without limits. Mitigation: FR-5 forces the terms-of-service and acceptable-use capture to be separate from the §105 copyright position, since the two answer different questions.
- Risk: a licensor's conditions are technically satisfiable only by identifying requests or reporting usage. Mitigation: FR-9 records this as an architectural conflict against INV-3, INV-4, and INV-26 and forces `prohibited`; the product does not acquire a durable identifier to satisfy a license.
- Risk: public endpoints are scrapable in a way an app bundle was not (ADR-0004 R7), which may itself change a licensor's answer. Mitigation: FR-10 requires every access constraint to become an implementable control with an owning issue; rate limiting is recorded as a mitigation and not as a solution.
- Risk: attribution obligations drift out of sync with the register over time. Mitigation: FR-8 makes the register the single source and the drift check a release gate (INV-27).
- Decision: dispositions are per source **and** per fact domain **and** per distribution mode. A single project-wide "web rights cleared" flag was rejected because it cannot express the common case of a source that is servable for identity but not for intervals.
- Decision: `bulk_export` and `machine_readable_serving` are prohibited by default, consistent with ADR-0004's rejection of publishing the normalized catalog as static JSON.
- Decision: the superseded offline-redistribution wording is preserved as a dated supersession rather than edited away, matching the constitution's own amendment discipline and CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 6.
- Open question: whether any documented-facts publisher's terms distinguish between reproducing a document and publishing discrete extracted values. The review must record the answer per publisher rather than assume the favorable reading; `pending` is the correct state until it is answered (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 4).
- Open question: whether a commercial fallback vendor will grant hosted-serving rights on one-time-snapshot terms, or only on subscription terms that create an ongoing runtime or contractual dependency. That question belongs to procurement in `DOS-M03-001`; this issue records only the disposition and the conflict.
- Open question (bookkeeping, not scope): the pivot document's proposed-issue table listed a different subject at the identifier `DOS-M09-010`. The re-plan assigns this identifier to the hosted-serving rights re-review. The ledger and `planning/roadmap.yml` must record the final assignment explicitly so the earlier proposal text does not resolve to the wrong issue; the other proposed subject needs its own identifier before it is filed.
