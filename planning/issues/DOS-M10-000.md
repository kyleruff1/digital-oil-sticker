## Outcome

A repair shop or quick-lube captures the vehicle and service values it already holds in its own system, and the customer walks out with a QR code printed on their receipt. They scan it on their own phone, and their digital sticker is populated in their own browser. Nobody types a VIN at a service counter, and nobody picks one of seven Camry builds while a customer waits.

At epic close, that experience exists and the mechanism that delivers it is split in two — a design decision ADR-0006 must record, not a conclusion the constitution forces:

- **The resolution half is ours and is impersonal.** A shop sends year, make, model, and an engine descriptor — nothing else — and receives exactly one `configuration_key` with its INV-11 support status, or an explicit refusal naming why. No VIN, odometer reading, note, free-text field, record identifier, or stable client identifier crosses the wire in the path, query string, body, or headers (INV-26) — and the contract refuses service dates as well, a field INV-26 does not enumerate. A request carrying one is rejected with a typed error, and the offending value is never echoed and never logged (INV-4).
- **The encoding half does not run on our server by default.** The sticker code is a pure function of `{configuration_key, changed_on, odometer_m, grade, base_stock}`, and three of those five are the customer's personal data. The codec and the QR renderer ship *to the integrator* as a vector-conformant library, so the shop's own system composes the personal values locally and our server never receives them. This is the argument the browser port already makes for the browser (`app/assets/js/sticker_code.js`), applied to a second client. A hosted encoding lane — the same values received transiently, never persisted — is not constitutionally impossible: INV-26's text governs catalog requests, and INV-24.1 already lets personal values transit socket assigns. `DOS-M10-002` specifies exactly that lane, compile-flag-off and absent from `tools/list` unless a ratified revision and accepted ADR turn it on. Distributing the codec is this epic's chosen default because it keeps personal values off our wire entirely, not because no other design exists.

Also at epic close: a ratified constitution revision has amended INV-19, whose prohibited-scope list ("shop booking, commerce"; "fleet administration") this surface must clear; ADR-0006 records the boundary; the source register carries a third redistribution disposition for machine-to-machine reuse by a commercial integrator (INV-15); the printed artifact has a fixed, lint-tested template that carries its own honesty because there is no tooltip on thermal paper; the copy-lint scan surface has been widened so shop-facing modules cannot ship unlinted claims; and the deployed application still runs exactly one Ecto repo, opened read-only, with no submission log, no print queue, and no per-shop history (INV-3, INV-23).

If the revision is not ratified, this milestone closes unstarted with the disposition recorded on the proposal, per CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 1, so the question does not recur unanswered. That is a legitimate outcome of this epic, not a failure of it.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

The product's hardest moment is the first one. A person who has just paid for an oil change holds every value the sticker needs — and has no reason to re-enter them into a website. The shop, meanwhile, already typed them once. This is the only channel in the roadmap where the data is captured by someone paid to be accurate, at the moment it is freshest, on a surface the customer keeps. Every other path asks a stranger to do data entry about their own car from memory.

It is also, and this governs how it must be reviewed, **the first surface in the product where data crosses a network boundary from a third party.** Everything shipped so far is structurally impersonal: the catalog selector accepts a closed vocabulary and cannot hold free text; the rate limiter is a pure token bucket in the caller's own socket assigns, so there is nothing to correlate and nothing to log; the sticker code exists specifically so there is nothing to store. The privacy posture here is *structural*, not policy — the server cannot leak a garage because it was never given one. A shop-facing surface inverts that by construction: an identified caller, sending a specific vehicle plus a specific service event, about a person who is not the caller.

What that costs in review is not a checklist item. It is the largest governance load any milestone in this roadmap carries:

1. **A new ratified constitution revision.** INV-19 lists "telematics/OBD integration, shop booking, commerce, ads, and social features" and "fleet administration" as prohibited MVP scope, alongside "cloud sync, shared garages, or any cross-device continuity other than user-initiated export/import" and "user-generated content that is published, shared, or transmitted off the device." A shop-to-customer handoff touches several of those clauses — "shop booking, commerce" most directly, the continuity and off-device-content clauses more arguably — though no clause names this feature: a printed handoff is neither commerce nor fleet administration, and the content clause covers data leaving the device, not arriving onto it. Requiring a revision is the conservative reading this epic adopts; the preamble requires a wholly new revision with fresh sign-off, never an in-place edit. This is not a ticket.
2. **A new ADR.** ADR-0002 §6 established that a new product surface requires its own approved decision; ADR-0004 satisfied that clause narrowly, approving a hosted browser application and fencing the grant explicitly. A machine-to-machine surface is outside it.
3. **A third pass at data rights.** ADR-0003 assumed redistribution inside an application bundle; ADR-0004 R6 made hosted network serving a hard gate, closed by DOS-M09-010. Handing catalog facts to a commercial third party for reprint on paper we do not control is a **third** distribution mode and reopens the register again (INV-15, CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 2b).
4. **A claim surface with no revision path.** Every mitigation the browser app leans on — the provenance panel, the confidence label, the details view — does not exist on a receipt. Once it prints it is unrevisable, uncontextualized, and sitting next to a shop's own branding.

What it unblocks is narrower than it looks, and should be stated honestly: this is a distribution channel, not a platform capability. Nothing else in the roadmap waits on it. The refusing resolver (`DOS-M10-001`) is the one component with reuse value beyond this milestone, because the browser app's vehicle picker currently makes the human resolve the one-to-many problem by hand.

## Excluded

- **Any reachable server-side receipt of a customer's personal values.** The odometer, service date, VIN, note text, and any free-text field are excluded from every reachable request surface this milestone deploys. On the resolution path this is INV-26's enumeration, extended by contract to service dates, which INV-26 does not name. The one specified exception is `DOS-M10-002`'s compile-flag-off encoding lane, absent from `tools/list` and unreachable until ratification turns it on — hence the encoding half is distributed rather than hosted.
- **Any server-side store keyed to a shop or a submission.** No print queue, retry or backoff buffer, delivery receipt, idempotency-key table, per-shop configuration store, submission log, or analytics counter that is per-shop and per-vehicle at once (INV-3, INV-19, INV-23; ADR-0004 Decision §4 and rejected alternative 5). The first migration adding one fails an existing release gate, and that gate is correct.
- **A lookup service of any kind.** No endpoint accepting a sticker code and returning a record, and no table mapping a code to anything. The code already carries its values, so there is nothing to look up; an endpoint that resolved one would be a server-side garage in a single step (INV-3, INV-23).
- **Reading, receiving, or trusting a telemetry or OBD feed, including a shop DMS odometer stream treated as authoritative.** A value supplied by a shop is a recorded value, never a vehicle reading (INV-12, whose "no future connectivity feature may change this promise without a new ratified revision" clause names the only path that would change this).
- **Fleet administration, shop booking, appointment scheduling, commerce, payment, marketing to the shop's customers, and any customer-list capability** (INV-19) — excluded explicitly rather than by omission, because a shop hub is one step from each.
- **Any claim that we notify the customer, remind the customer, or hold their record.** The MVP uses in-app due state only and must not imply background alerting of any kind (INV-17), and there is no server copy to hold (INV-23, INV-25).
- **Any interval lengthening, and any shop-supplied "next service" value overriding a sourced schedule.** A shop-supplied interval enters at the user's-own-interval tier at best, never the manufacturer tier (INV-13, ADR-0005).
- **Free-text resolution inside `DigitalOilSticker.Catalog`.** The resolver in `DOS-M10-001` is a separate refusing layer above the facade; relaxing `Selector`'s "structurally cannot hold free text" property is out of scope and would be an owner decision recorded in ADR-0006, not a refactor.
- **Any change to the sticker code wire format.** No new format version, no reordering or reuse of a frozen grade or base-stock index, no epoch change, no checksum byte, no additional field. Every code already printed came from the current vectors.
- **Any disabled-but-visible shop control, "coming soon" line, or partner logo** in the application or the static content prior to ratification (INV-19 closing clause).
- **UK/EU territory expansion**, which requires its own review under the marks policy and is not closed here.
- Work not stated in a child issue's Included scope. Discoveries become a new issue or ADR rather than silent scope growth (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 3).

## Preconditions

- M09 is closed: the hosted application exists, is hardened, serves an impersonal read-only catalog query layer, and has a proven deploy and rollback path. There is nothing for a shop to integrate with otherwise, and the hardening evidence is the baseline this milestone must not regress.
- The sticker code format is shipped and frozen at version 1, with its emitted vector set and round-trip property tests passing in both implementations. The codec is the contract this milestone distributes; it is not something this milestone designs.
- DOS-M09-010's source-disposition model exists, so a third redistribution dimension is an added disposition rather than a schema redesign.
- The owner has answered the foundational question ahead of design work, per CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 4: whether distributing the codec to integrators is acceptable. If it is not, the fallback is `DOS-M10-002`'s flag-off hosted lane, which only ratification could turn on — and the milestone does not ship as designed here. Guessing this to keep the epic moving is a change-control violation even if the guess is right.

Exit-gate conditions:

- FR-7: The surface is split. The resolution half accepts only impersonal selectors — year, make, model, engine descriptor — under INV-26's discipline, and the encoding half runs in the integrator's system. No reachable deployed endpoint accepts a sticker's personal values or returns a sticker code composed from values a third party supplied; `DOS-M10-002`'s encoding lane is proven flag-off and unreachable unless the ratifications enabling it exist.
- FR-8: A request carrying a VIN, an odometer or mileage value, a service date, note text, any free-text field beyond the declared selectors, a record identifier, or a stable client identifier — in the path, query string, body, or headers — is rejected with a single typed error. Validation rejects; it never strips, coerces, substitutes, or echoes the offending input.
- FR-9: No personal value appears in any log line, error payload, stack trace, telemetry event, response body, or committed fixture originating from the shop path. `debug_errors` is proven false for that path, no third-party error reporting exists, and the scripted log-scanning release gate is extended to cover this traffic (INV-4).
- FR-10: Resolution returns exactly one configuration or an explicit refusal. There is no confidence-scored fuzzy match, no nearest-trim fallback, no silent dropping of an engine qualifier, and no inference of an interval from a generic model family. Ambiguity is a named result carrying the candidate count and the discriminating field, not an arbitrary pick.
- FR-11: A model year outside the rolling window is refused rather than normalized into the nearest in-window row (INV-8). A heavy commercial, motorcycle, powersports, off-highway, or non-U.S.-schedule vehicle is refused with a distinct reason (INV-9). A battery-electric vehicle resolves to `not_applicable` with the "Engine oil service not applicable" framing and yields no interval.
- FR-12: Every response carries one of the five INV-11 statuses with its qualifier codes, and the printed artifact carries the status. A well-formed but unknown configuration returns `unsupported` with its qualifier rather than an empty success; the shop path must not treat a successful envelope with absent data as a resolved vehicle.
- FR-13: When catalog resolution fails or the catalog is unavailable, the failure is explicit at the shop and nothing prints. There is no best-effort artifact, no stale or partial result presented as authoritative, and no fabricated fallback interval (INV-7, INV-15). A customer waiting at a counter is told the sticker could not be produced; they are not handed a plausible one.
- FR-14: The distributed codec produces byte-identical codes to `DigitalOilSticker.StickerCode` across the entire emitted vector set, verified by a conformance run against the integrator's own build. The integrator's implementation is held to the vectors, not to a re-reading of the documentation.
- FR-15: The frozen wire format is unchanged: format version 1, the grade and base-stock index tables append-only with no reordering and no reuse of a retired index, the epoch unchanged, no checksum byte, no additional field. The manual-grade tail stays capped at 20 UTF-8 bytes and is treated as attacker-controlled input when it originates from a shop's system.
- FR-16: The printed block renders from a fixed template carrying, in print: the estimate framing; the basis sentence identifying whose interval this is; the INV-11 status; the source, or the explicit absence of one; the "Exact configuration not verified" line where the build was not resolved exactly; the oil-life-monitor deference line where INV-14 applies; and the verbatim no-affiliation statement. If the receipt cannot carry them all, the template degrades to printing the QR alone with no interval, date, or grade claim — never to a shortened claim (INV-20, INV-21, ADR-0005).
- FR-17: The printed block is self-contained and carries no third-party logo, trade dress, packaging reproduction, or certification-mark graphic, and no manufacturer name appears in the product's own lockup. We do not control the surrounding receipt, which is precisely why the no-affiliation statement travels inside our block.
- FR-18: The build-failing claim vocabulary fails on shop-facing files exactly as on browser files, and "Meets the recorded requirements" appears only where every mandatory requirement has a source-backed match.
- FR-19: The customer path lands in the garage through the existing client-side write path: the code arrives in the URL fragment and is never sent to the server, is decoded in the browser, and is written to client storage with the mutation rendered as committed only after the hook confirms the write (INV-18, INV-23, INV-24.1, INV-24.2). Nothing about a shop-originated record is committed server-side.
- FR-20: The deployed application still runs exactly one Ecto repo, opened read-only, and a schema and code audit finds no queue, retry buffer, delivery-receipt store, idempotency table, per-shop configuration store, or submission log anywhere in the deployment (INV-3, INV-23; ADR-0004 Decision §4).
- FR-21: If the surface is identified at all, the identification is named as a business-account system in the ratified revision rather than disguised as an anonymous token, and no artifact exists from which a customer's service history could be reconstituted: no per-shop submission history, no retained request stream, and no counter that is simultaneously per-shop and per-vehicle (INV-3).
- FR-22: Cross-request rate limiting exists on the shop path, keyed by the salted hash the existing `ClientIP` plug produces, which is never logged, stored, returned, or exposed in a header. The per-socket token bucket does not apply to a stateless request, so an unprotected endpoint is the default outcome unless this is built (ADR-0004 R7 records that rate limiting is a mitigation, not a solution).

- FR-25: The affiliate and sponsorship fields remain false, asserted by test, unless a commercial relationship exists — in which case disclosure is clear, conspicuous, and adjacent to the recommendation on the printed artifact itself, not in a linked policy.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

N/A — epic-level tracking issue. Ownership is specified per child: the refusing resolver, which sits **above** the catalog facade and does not relax `Selector`'s no-free-text property (`DOS-M10-001`); the MCP transport — the mounted `ClientIP` plug, rate limiting, the retention gate, and the flag-off encoding lane (`DOS-M10-002`); the packaged codec and its conformance runner, the resolution contract, wire mapping, and refusal taxonomy, the print contract and printed claim block, and the widened scans (`DOS-M10-003`); the scan-matrix measurement and its geometry gate (`DOS-M10-004`); the ratified revision and ADR-0006 (`DOS-M10-005`); the third source-disposition dimension and its build gate (`DOS-M10-006`); the identification decision (`DOS-M10-007`); the scan-to-garage arrival path (`DOS-M10-008`); and the conformance and evidence suite (`DOS-M10-009`).

The epic enforces two cross-cutting boundaries directly. The existing rule that no module in the `Catalog` namespace references Phoenix, Plug, or client-storage code survives unchanged — the shop edge is a new transport over the same transport-agnostic facade, following the existing web edge's pattern of allowlist, rate-limit, validate, dispatch. And the encoding boundary: no module reachable from a shop request may call the codec with values a third party supplied.

### Data and persistence

N/A — epic-level tracking issue. Two persistence facts bind every child and are why the design is shaped this way: the deployment holds exactly one repository, the catalog, opened read-only, and acquires no second repository here; and there is no personal row anywhere for a shop-originated record — the customer's record comes into existence in their own browser when they scan, and not before. The only new persisted data is a source-disposition row and, if `DOS-M10-007` decides identification is required, whatever that decision explicitly ratifies — a configuration record about a business, not about a person.

### Failure and recovery behavior

N/A — epic-level tracking issue. Aggregate expectation, and the part of this milestone deserving the most design time: the happy path is one function call, and everything interesting is a refusal. No child closes without evidence for its own failure paths, and the union must cover — an ambiguous vehicle with several candidate builds; a year outside the window; an excluded vehicle class; a battery-electric vehicle; a well-formed but unknown configuration; a catalog that is unavailable or rate-limited; a request carrying a personal field; a manual grade string that is hostile, over-length, or non-UTF-8; a base stock the codec cannot encode at all; an implausible or non-numeric odometer; a printer that jams or truncates mid-block; a terminal that cannot render the QR; a code that scans but decodes to a vehicle the customer does not recognize; and a code that does not scan because the receipt smudged.

The recovery posture is uniform and blunt: the shop's system owns the retry, because we hold no queue. A failure returns to the caller synchronously and is theirs to handle. There is no server-side record to reconcile, no partial artifact to clean up, and no support path that recovers a lost sticker, because the customer's record only ever existed in their own browser (INV-25).

### Security and privacy

Aggregate expectation, enforced by child acceptance criteria and audited by `DOS-M10-009`: personal fields are rejected rather than accepted-and-redacted; the log allowlist and prohibition list cover the shop path, enforced by configuration plus a release-gating scan; cross-request rate limiting is keyed by a salted per-boot hash never logged, stored, or returned; the manual-grade tail is treated as untrusted input from a system we do not control, bounded, validated, and never rendered into markup unescaped; no third-party error reporting exists; the repository list is unchanged; and if identification exists it is named honestly rather than presented as anonymous. The privacy claim stays scoped precisely — no personal data is stored on the server — and is not overclaimed into a promise that no operator could observe a live request.

The threat no prior milestone had is a caller who is not the data subject. A shop clerk cannot consent on the customer's behalf and may be entering the wrong vehicle. The design's answer is that nothing the shop sends is retained by us, and nothing becomes the customer's record until the customer scans it on their own device and their own browser writes it.

### Accessibility and performance

Aggregate expectation: the scan-to-garage arrival inherits the standing accessibility bar in full, including the honest pre-hydration state — a scanned arrival must not render a false empty garage while the client store is still being read (INV-24.1, INV-24.2). The printed artifact carries physical constraints no CSS can fix: the QR must scan at the module size a thermal printer actually produces, on receipt stock, under service-bay lighting, from a phone camera at arm's length, and after the paper has been folded into a wallet. The QR renderer's merged-run output, chosen for underpowered terminal browsers, is inherited rather than revisited. Print legibility and scan reliability are measured on real hardware in `DOS-M10-004`, not asserted from documentation. Resolution latency is budgeted against the counter rather than the browser — a clerk with a customer waiting abandons a call that does not answer promptly — and is set and measured in `DOS-M10-003` and `DOS-M10-002`.

## UX and content states

N/A — epic-level tracking issue with no screens of its own. Two surfaces carry state, both specified by children. The **printed block** (`DOS-M10-003`) has one variant per INV-11 status plus the degenerate QR-only variant: `schedule_supported`; `full_product_supported`; `identity_only`, where an our-model interval renders only as an overlay with its basis sentence, since `our_model` sits **outside** the INV-11 ladder and never upgrades the status; `not_applicable`, with the "Engine oil service not applicable" framing and no interval; and `unsupported`, where nothing prints. The **scan arrival** (`DOS-M10-008`) reuses the existing client-storage state vocabulary — hydrating, storage-unavailable, quota-exceeded — and adds three of its own: code unreadable, code decodes to an unknown configuration, and code decodes to a vehicle that matches nothing the person expects. None of these states may be softened into implied durability, and none may imply that the shop, or we, hold a copy of the record (INV-5, INV-25).

## Rollout and rollback

Gated planning epic. Nothing becomes reachable until the entry gate closes, and that gate is a ratification rather than a build. Sequencing follows the dependency edges: `DOS-M10-005` and `DOS-M10-006` gate every reachable surface — though transport-free `DOS-M10-001` and measurement-only `DOS-M10-004` may proceed as evidence for that gate; `DOS-M10-003` gates `DOS-M10-002`'s tool surface — the contract precedes its transport; `DOS-M10-008` needs only the codec; `DOS-M10-009` is last because it consumes all of them. `DOS-M10-007` is deliberately timeboxed and deliberately early, because a decision to identify shops changes the deployment boundary and therefore the shape of `DOS-M10-002`.

Rollback is asymmetric, and the asymmetry should be stated plainly. Server-side work rolls back the way M09's does: revert the commits, or redeploy the previous immutable image. The distributed codec does not — once an integrator has embedded a version we do not control when it updates, and a code they printed is on a customer's receipt permanently. That is why the wire format is frozen and why FR-14 holds the integrator to the emitted vectors rather than to prose; a defect in the distributed half is mitigated by property tests and a frozen format, not by a rollback. Printed artifacts have no rollback at all: a receipt cannot be recalled, corrected, or amended, which is why the template degrades to a bare QR rather than to a shortened claim.

A pilot is the only sane rollout: one integrator, a bounded vehicle set, a bounded volume, and the ability to stop serving the shop path without touching the browser application. That separability is itself a requirement of `DOS-M10-002`.

## Dependency rationale

**Blocked by `DOS-M09-000` in full, not in part.** This milestone consumes the hosted platform as a finished thing: the deployed origin and its TLS and header posture, the read-only catalog query layer and its typed facade, the deploy and rollback pipeline, the client storage schema and the hydration protocol a scanned arrival writes through, and — most importantly — the hardening evidence M09 produced. That evidence is the baseline this milestone must not regress: the repo-list assertion, the log-scanning gate, the payload audit, and the idle capture all have to still pass afterwards, and each is a plausible casualty of a new request surface. Starting shop work against a platform whose privacy gates are not yet green would leave those gates unable to distinguish a shop-path regression from an unfinished platform.

**Blocked by the sticker code format, which is a contract rather than a dependency edge.** `DigitalOilSticker.StickerCode` is why this milestone is architecturally possible and why it is architecturally constrained. Possible, because the code is a bijection carrying its own values — the customer's phone reconstructs the whole record from the printed symbol with no lookup, no account, and no server round trip, exactly the property a receipt needs. Constrained, because the module states its own scope rule in source: the payload may never grow to include notes, a VIN, or anything a person would not tape to their own window. A shop integration is the most obvious pressure to break that rule, and the rule is what makes the integration safe. This milestone therefore inherits the format frozen — version 1, the epoch, the append-only index tables, the 20-byte manual-grade tail, no checksum — and adds a second implementation in someone else's system, which is why the emitted vector set rather than the documentation is the conformance contract.

Two further edges are consumed rather than created. From **M03 and `DOS-M09-010`** it takes the catalog and its source-disposition model, adding a third redistribution dimension; it introduces no automotive fact of its own. From **M02** it takes the terminology and claims contract, which the printed template must satisfy rather than restate — with the honest caveat that the mandated vocabulary was written for a surface where the user owns the storage, so the shop surface needs its own ratified vocabulary rather than an improvised one.

**M10 blocks nothing.** No other milestone waits on it, which makes the displacement question in the Rule 1 proposal real rather than a formality: every week spent here is a week not spent on the post-MVP expansion already committed to. The one component with reuse value beyond this milestone is the refusing resolver, which would let the browser app resolve a typed vehicle instead of making the user walk the four-step cascade by hand.

Per the pivot document's stable-ID contract, this milestone takes a sequence band placed by its execution position — as M09 took 250–299 to execute third — and no existing roadmap ID is renumbered. The dependency edges recorded in prose here are not machine-checked against the catalog's own edges; they must be kept consistent by hand, and the epic's Included scope requires them recorded in the tracker before the first child starts.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- **Risk (the one that decides whether this milestone exists):** the owner may not accept distributing the codec to integrators. If not, the fallback is `DOS-M10-002`'s flag-off hosted lane: odometer, service date, and grade transit our server transiently, never persisted. INV-26's text governs catalog requests and does not by itself foreclose that lane, and INV-24.1 already lets personal values transit socket assigns — so the split surface is this epic's design position, not the only lawful reading, and enabling the hosted lane is an owner-plus-ratification decision. Mitigation: ask before designing (CHANGE_CONTROL Rule 4) and record the answer in ADR-0006 either way.
- **Risk:** "normalizes them into a canonical sticker code" is where fabrication would enter. A shop DMS emits a make, a model, and a displacement string; the codec needs the catalog's UUID for one exact build, and a single year-make-model can carry several. Bridging those is inference, and the claims policy forbids inferring an interval from a generic model family or a nearest engine match. Mitigation: `DOS-M10-001` is a *refusing* resolver whose property test asserts no input produces a key unless exactly one candidate matched.
- **Risk:** a shop under time pressure will pick whichever option makes the printer fire. A refusal costing the clerk thirty seconds gets worked around — by choosing an arbitrary build, or by abandoning the integration. This is the failure mode most likely to make the feature quietly wrong rather than visibly broken, and no server-side test detects it. Mitigation: the counter walk in `DOS-M10-009` runs a real workflow with a stopwatch, and the ambiguity result names the discriminating field so the clerk can answer it from the car in front of them.
- **Risk (ADR-0004 R7, extended):** a machine-readable, commercially-reachable endpoint is more scrapable than a browser app, and rate limiting is a mitigation rather than a solution. The catalog's value is its normalization, which is exactly what makes bulk extraction attractive. Mitigation: the disposition gate in `DOS-M10-006` decides what may be served at all on this path, before any throttle is tuned.
- **Risk:** the printed artifact is an unrevisable claim surface next to a third party's branding. We control neither the layout around our block nor whether the shop adds its own interval advice beside it. Mitigation: the block is self-contained, carries its own no-affiliation statement, and degrades to a bare QR rather than a shortened claim — but the residual risk that a customer reads the shop's adjacent copy as ours cannot be engineered away and should be accepted explicitly or not at all.
- **Risk:** the copy-lint gate passes while shipping unlinted claims, because its scan surface reaches only the web namespace. A silent failure, not a loud one. Mitigation: FR-6 makes widening it a precondition and AC-16 proves it by planting a term.
- **Risk:** the first support question this feature generates is "the shop said it's on there, why can't I see it?" — and there is no answer, because we hold nothing. Mitigation: the printed block and the scan-arrival copy must set the expectation before the failure, since there is no recovery afterwards (INV-25).
- **Decision (recorded, not reopened):** the sticker code stays a bijection and never becomes a key. No endpoint accepts a code and returns a record, and no table maps a code to anything. The codec's property tests are what make a lookup table unnecessary — the code already carries the values.
- **Decision (recorded, not reopened):** shop identification, if adopted, is named as a business-account system rather than an anonymous token. ADR-0004 already rejected the disguised form in terms this milestone inherits.
- **Open question (owned by `DOS-M10-005`, blocking):** which INV-19 clauses the revision relaxes and which it retains, and what the new invariant for a third-party boundary actually says. Until this is written, every downstream child is speculative.
- **Open question (owned by `DOS-M10-006`, blocking `DOS-M10-003`):** whether any currently-served source permits machine-to-machine redistribution to a commercial integrator at all. If none does, the resolution half returns identity and status only and the printed artifact carries no interval — a materially smaller product than this epic describes, which would need re-proposing rather than quiet shipping.
- **Open question (owned by `DOS-M10-007`):** whether the surface is authenticated at all. An unauthenticated impersonal endpoint has no account problem and no correlation problem, at the cost of no abuse control beyond rate limiting; an authenticated one inverts both.
- **Open question (owned by `DOS-M10-005`, with `DOS-M10-002` as its subject):** whether ADR-0006 accepts MCP as the transport. The `DOS-M10-002` draft answers the design question in the affirmative — an MCP server on the existing origin — so what remains open is ratification, not existence; adopting MCP touches ADR-0004's no-third-party-services stance, and `DOS-M10-003`'s contract stays transport-independent either way.
- **Open question (owned by `DOS-M10-003`, informed by `DOS-M10-004`):** whether a receipt line is physically long enough to carry the mandated content at all, on the printers integrators actually use. If not, the QR-only variant is the product, and the epic's outcome statement narrows to match rather than the content requirements relaxing.
