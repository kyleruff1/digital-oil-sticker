## Outcome

The scannability of the sticker artifact is measured rather than assumed. A ratified `SCAN_MATRIX.md` names every **medium × scanner class × tier** the product makes a claim about — direct thermal receipt stock at 203 dpi, laser-printed sticker/label stock, and an LCD screen at low device-pixel-ratio, read by a phone camera, a handheld 2D imager, and a linear (1D laser) scanner — and states per cell what was measured, on what dated hardware, at what settings, and what remains unmeasured. Pass is a number, not an opinion: an integer module edge in printer dots (device pixels on screen), a first-read success rate over a stated attempt count, a decode-time ceiling, and a recorded working range of distance and off-axis angle. The `~1.6 device pixels per module` figure — shipped in three places: the ECC comment in `app/assets/js/hooks/qr_symbol.js`, the moduledoc of `DigitalOilStickerWeb.Components.StickerQr`, and the `.dos-qr` sizing comment in `app/assets/css/app.css`, whose 160px width and 120px floor are derived from it — is treated as a single-observation provisional value, re-derived under a written protocol, and confirmed or corrected in every shipped location, including the derived width floors. Degradation cells — thermal fade, crease, scuff, dead print-head dots, print-darkness extremes, feed skew, glare — are characterized to the point of failure, and the one hard requirement across all of them is that the printed forty-character code stays human-readable everywhere the symbol stops decoding — with a single, matrix-recorded exception: a degradation state that destroys symbol and text alike is recorded as a dated hard limit of the medium, beyond which the product claims nothing. Evidence lands in `conformance/` as a machine-readable record with `pass` / `fail` / `unproven` and nothing softer, and a deterministic CI gate fails the build when the shipped symbol geometry has no matching, non-stale, passing record.

## Why this matters and what it unblocks

The QR is the whole point of the artifact and it is the part the product cannot see. Everything else on a sticker degrades visibly: faded text is obviously faded. A symbol that has drifted below its decode floor looks identical to one that works, right up until a technician at a counter with a customer waiting points a scanner at it three times and gives up. That failure is silent, it happens at the shop rather than in our test suite, and the shop blames the product.

The geometry is already fixed by decisions made for good reasons and never measured across media. The payload is a URL with the code in its fragment — a privacy property (INV-26: a fragment is never sent to the server, so the odometer, service date, and grade the code carries stay off every request line and access log between the browser and us) that costs modules, because `#` is outside QR's alphanumeric set and pushes the whole payload into byte mode, growing the symbol from 29 modules to 37. The screen renderer then chose `medium` error correction over `quartile` on the reasoning that both "decode down to the same ~1.6 device pixels per module". That figure came from one observation on one piece of hardware, and it now underwrites three different shipped statements: the `medium`-over-`quartile` ECC choice in `qr_symbol.js`, the 29-versus-37-module size-cost argument in the `StickerQr` moduledoc, and the 160px width and 120px floor derived from it in `app.css`. `qr_symbol.js` says plainly that print "is a different decision and is made where the print artwork is generated." The M10 print-contract issue has since made that decision on paper it did not measure — 3 dots per module at ECC medium, by derivation plus margin from this same screen figure. This issue supplies the measurement that decision is currently missing: it finds the cliff, validates the specified margin, and files a defect against the print contract if paper disagrees with the derivation.

It unblocks any claim about the artifact scanning off a screen or off paper, which every downstream surface in this milestone depends on. It gives the M10 printed-artifact template issue its physical constraints — minimum module edge, minimum legible stroke width for the mandated disclosure lines, quiet-zone margin against the surrounding receipt — under an explicitly two-phase contract: that issue's dimensioned draft is this issue's input, this issue's measured constraints gate that template's final ratification, and neither direction is circular because each consumes a different phase of the other. And it produces the scope statement any shop-facing documentation has to carry: we measure our artwork on named, dated hardware; we do not control a shop's printer, its darkness setting, its paper supplier, its scanner firmware, or the windshield the sticker ends up on.

## Preconditions

- The M10 printed-artifact template exists as a dimensioned draft — explicitly a draft, not a final. The dependency is two-phase by design: the draft supplies the surrounding layout this issue measures against, and this issue's measured constraints then gate the template's final ratification. A module edge measured against no surrounding layout tells you nothing about whether the symbol fits beside the text it has to sit next to.
- The `DOS-M09-008` conformance harness, report schema, and tier semantics are merged, so this issue extends an existing evidence pipeline rather than starting a second one with different words for the same statuses.
- Physical hardware is procured and recorded by model and firmware: at minimum one direct-thermal receipt printer at 203 dpi, one laser printer with label/sticker stock, one low-device-pixel-ratio LCD, one current phone per mobile platform, one handheld 2D imager, and one linear laser scanner. A borrowed device is acceptable; an unrecorded device is not.
- A print pipeline exists that gets the SVG onto each medium deterministically, with its rasterization settings recorded. Without that, a failed cell cannot be attributed to the symbol rather than to whatever the print path did to it.
- Synthetic fixture stickers are available from the emitted vector set, with values obviously synthetic (INV-4), because every print and every photograph of a print is a decodable record.
- The `SCAN_MATRIX.md` tier definitions are agreed before any measurement is taken, so that a run cannot be retroactively classified into whichever tier it happened to pass.

## Functional requirements

- FR-1 (INV-15): `SCAN_MATRIX.md` names each cell as **medium × scanner class × tier** and records, per cell, the hardware model and firmware, the settings, the operator, the date, and the measured values. A cell with no run is marked unmeasured. No cell may be filled from a datasheet, a vendor claim, or an adjacent cell's result.
- FR-2 (INV-15): Scanner classes are defined by **verified symbology support with a verification date**, never by the product name. A device marketed as a "scanner" that decodes only linear symbologies is classified as linear whatever the box says; a device marketed as a laser scanner that is in fact a 2D imager is classified as an imager.
- FR-3: The linear-laser class is recorded as **structurally unable to decode the symbol**. That is a property of the symbology, not a defect and not a tuning problem. The assertion carried on that row is not "the QR decodes" — it is FR-9's fallback assertion.
- FR-4: The screen decode floor is re-derived under the written protocol with a stated sample size and named hardware. If it differs materially from the recorded `~1.6 device pixels per module`, every shipped location stating it — the comment in `app/assets/js/hooks/qr_symbol.js`, the moduledoc in `DigitalOilStickerWeb.Components.StickerQr`, and the `.dos-qr` sizing comment in `app/assets/css/app.css` — is corrected in the same change; the 160px width and 120px minimum in `app.css`, whose per-module arithmetic derives from the figure, are re-derived rather than left standing; and the `medium`-over-`quartile` screen decision is re-made rather than inherited.
- FR-5: The print floor is expressed as an **integer number of printer dots per module**, plus the derived module edge in millimetres and the symbol span including the four-module quiet zone on every side. A non-integer dots-per-module ratio is prohibited in shipped artwork: fractional module boundaries let the rasterizer distribute dots unevenly, so some modules print at three dots and their neighbours at four, and the resulting failures are position-dependent and unreproducible.
- FR-6: Each print medium is swept across integer dots-per-module values down to the cliff where the pass criterion stops being met, and both the cliff and the shipped value are recorded — the cliff is the measurement, the margin is the promise. The shipped value itself is owned by the print-contract issue, which specified **3 dots per module at ECC medium** by derivation plus margin before any paper was measured; this sweep validates that the specified value sits **at least one integer step above the measured cliff**, and a specified value that does not is a defect filed against the print contract — never a number this issue quietly replaces or re-decides in a second place.
- FR-7: A cell passes only when all of the following hold at the nominal distance and at both ends of the recorded working range: every attempt in a run of at least twenty consecutive attempts decodes; every decode returns a string **byte-identical** to the printed payload; each decode completes inside the recorded decode-time ceiling. Nineteen successes and one retry is a fail, not a 95%.
- FR-8: Every cell records a **working range**, not a point: minimum and maximum distance and maximum off-axis angle at which FR-7 still holds. `SCAN_MATRIX.md` states a **numeric ergonomic range per scanner class** — distance bounds and off-axis angle, agreed alongside the tier definitions before any measurement is taken — for which a phone at arm's length in front of a windshield and an imager at counter distance are the motivating cases. A cell whose working range does not cover the stated range for its class fails even if it passes at one convenient distance.
- FR-9 (INV-15): In every cell and every degradation state where the symbol fails to decode, the forty printed characters MUST remain human-readable and MUST transcribe back to a code that decodes to the fixture sticker — with exactly one exception: a degradation state that destroys the symbol and the text alike is recorded in the matrix as a dated **hard limit of the medium**, beyond which the product claims nothing. This is the product's stated durability argument, tested here rather than asserted, and AC-11 and the definition of done apply this same rule, so the requirement and its acceptance cannot drift apart.
- FR-10: A pass may only be recorded from the scanner-class hardware itself. Decoding a photograph or flatbed scan of a print in desktop software proves the artwork and nothing about the scanner; such a result is recorded separately as an artwork check, never as a cell result.
- FR-11: Degradation cells are characterized to failure and recorded as measurements rather than gated: thermal fade under accelerated conditions and under real dwell; a fold through the symbol centre and through a finder pattern; abrasion at a defined pass count and load; simulated dead print-head dots at one, two, and three adjacent dot columns, across a data region and across a finder pattern; print darkness at the lowest and highest settings the printer offers; feed skew; specular glare; and mounting on a curved surface.
- FR-12: The real-dwell thermal observation — a printed artifact left in a sun-exposed vehicle for a recorded period — cannot fail a CI run and cannot be waited out. It is scheduled separately, its design recorded, and until it completes `SCAN_MATRIX.md` and every downstream document state that thermal durability on a windshield is **unmeasured**. Paper documentation may inform the design; only the measured result may be written down as fact.
- FR-13 (INV-11, INV-14, INV-20, ADR-0005): The mandated disclosure lines are measured for legibility per medium as a minimum point size and stroke width at which a reader at reading distance can read them. A line printed below that threshold is **absent in practice**, and the response is a larger artifact or fewer printed claims — never a smaller font.
- FR-14 (INV-7): Where resolution failed and there is no configuration key, no symbol is generated and the code region prints empty rather than carrying a plausible-looking symbol — matching the browser hook, which leaves nothing behind when encoding fails because "a broken or stale image next to it would be worse than an empty box, because it looks scannable."
- FR-15 (INV-4): Every fixture is synthetic and obviously so. No real VIN, odometer reading, service date, or note may be printed, photographed, or retained. Photographs of printed symbols are treated as decodable records: produced only from synthetic fixtures, retained for a stated period, never published to a public location.
- FR-16 (INV-26): The rig performs no network transmission of any decoded payload. Decoding is on-device or through a named offline decoder; a cloud decode service, an image-uploading scanner app, and an app that auto-opens the URL through a redirector are all prohibited. Where a platform camera resolves the URL, the run asserts the fragment appears in no server-side access log.
- FR-17: The scan-conformance record is machine-readable, versioned, and keyed by the FR-18 fingerprint, the medium, the scanner class, the hardware identity, the settings, the operator, and the date. Assertion statuses are `pass`, `fail`, and `unproven` only; a fourth status, or any status meaning "warn", is prohibited.
- FR-18: A **geometry fingerprint** is computed deterministically from the inputs that decide scannability: sticker-code format version, payload template, resulting module count, error-correction level per medium, quiet-zone module count, dots-per-module per print medium, print template version, and the print pipeline's rasterization settings. It is committed alongside the record.
- FR-19: A CI check runs on every build with no hardware attached and **fails the build** when the current fingerprint has no matching scan-conformance record, when the matching record is not a pass, or when it is older than the staleness window `SCAN_MATRIX.md` sets. It does not warn, does not annotate, and has no bypass flag; the only way past it is a fresh measurement or a dated supersession in the matrix.
- FR-20: An unrun Tier 1 cell is `unproven`, and `unproven` fails, reusing the rule the conformance report already enforces. No list of known-failing cells accumulates: a failure is fixed, or the matrix is amended by a dated supersession stating what is no longer claimed and why.
- FR-21 (INV-15, INV-19): The matrix carries a scope statement bounding the claim to the media, scanner classes, hardware, and settings measured, and stating that the shop's printer, darkness setting, paper supplier, scanner firmware, and mounting surface are outside our control. Universal-scanning language is prohibited, as is any word from the prohibited claims vocabulary.
- FR-22 (INV-19): Shop-media rows are recorded as blocked until the scope change they depend on is ratified. They may be measured as evidence for that proposal; they may not gate an MVP release, appear in any product surface, or be cited as an implemented capability.

## Technical implementation contract

### Components and ownership boundaries

- `docs/quality/SCAN_MATRIX.md` is the single authority for cells, tiers, hardware identities, and the staleness window. No test, CI job, document, or product surface may claim a medium or scanner class the matrix does not list, and the matrix may not list a cell that was not run.
- The measurement protocol is a versioned document, not tribal knowledge in a test file. A protocol revision invalidates prior runs for the assertions it touches, and each record names the protocol version that produced it.
- The print pipeline is a recorded, deterministic path from the existing SVG to each medium. It is a test-tree artifact, ships no production code, and does not become a second renderer: `app/assets/js/sticker_qr.js` remains the only thing that turns a payload into modules.
- The scan-conformance record writer lives beside the existing conformance report writer in `conformance/` and reuses its status vocabulary. It is the only producer of release evidence here; CI consumes its output and does not parse console text.
- The geometry fingerprint calculator is a small, dependency-free module that reads shipped values rather than restating them, so a change to error-correction level or quiet zone moves the fingerprint automatically instead of relying on someone remembering to bump it.
- Not owned here: the artifact template, the disclosure copy, the resolution path, and the codec. This issue measures and files defects.

### Data and persistence

- Fixtures are drawn from `conformance/fixtures/sticker-code-vectors.json` and are synthetic by construction. The set covers at least a fully populated code, a code with an absent date, a code with an absent odometer, and a manual-grade code with a full twenty-byte tail — the last because it is the longest payload the format produces and therefore the densest symbol the artifact will ever print.
- The record set is append-only and permanent. A superseded record is marked superseded and kept, because it is the only evidence of what was true when a given artifact was printed, and artifacts printed under an old geometry stay on windshields long after the geometry moves.
- Photographs and decode logs are evidence artifacts under `RELEASE_GATES.md`'s redaction rule and FR-15, retained for a stated period in a non-public location.
- The record stores measured numbers, not verdicts alone: the cliff value and the shipped value, the working-range endpoints, the attempt counts, and the decode times. A future reader must be able to see how close a shipped cell was to failing.
- Nothing here enters a database, a server-side store, or any user-visible storage. There is no runtime component and therefore no runtime persistence question.

### Interfaces and events

- The record schema is a versioned JSON document: schema version, protocol version, geometry fingerprint, and an array of cell results carrying `{medium, scanner_class, hardware, settings, tier, assertions[], measurements{}, operator, measured_at}`, where each assertion is `{id, requirement, status, detail, evidence_ref}` and `status` is `pass`, `fail`, or `unproven`.
- The CI contract is a single deterministic check: compute the fingerprint from the tree, look for a matching record, and exit non-zero on absent, non-pass, or stale. Its failure message names which of the three it was and what would fix it.
- `SCAN_MATRIX.md` and the record are cross-checked in both directions, the way `SUPPORT_MATRIX.md` and the conformance suite already are: a cell in the matrix with no record is a matrix error, and a record for a cell the matrix does not list is a record error.
- The shipped dots-per-module values are read by the print pipeline from a single source that the fingerprint also reads, so the artwork and the fingerprint cannot disagree.

### Failure and recovery behavior

- **A cell fails during a run.** Recorded as `fail` with the measurement that failed and the value observed. The response is a geometry change and a re-run, or a dated supersession narrowing the claim. Re-running until it passes without recording the failures is prohibited; a cell that needs three attempts to produce a green run has a margin problem.
- **Hardware is unavailable.** The cell is `unproven`, which at Tier 1 fails. Borrowing different hardware produces a different cell, not the same one — the record is keyed by hardware identity for exactly this reason.
- **The protocol changes.** Runs under the prior protocol version stay in the record marked superseded, and the cells they covered revert to `unproven` for the assertions the change touches.
- **The geometry changes without a re-run.** FR-19's build gate is the backstop, deliberately the cheapest, dumbest check in the set: no hardware, no network, no flake surface, just a fingerprint comparison. A gate that can only fail for one reason is a gate people trust.
- **The gate itself breaks.** It has no bypass flag. If it is wrong it is fixed, or the record is refreshed; disabling it would restore the build while removing the only thing standing between a geometry change and an unscannable artifact.
- **A symbol decodes to something other than what was printed.** A severe defect against the print pipeline or the renderer rather than a scanner quirk, because it is the one failure mode worse than not scanning: a wrong record that looks right. FR-7's byte-identity assertion catches it, and the renderer geometry tests in `conformance/test/sticker-qr.test.mjs` are the first line of defence.
- **The real-dwell thermal observation shows unacceptable fade.** A finding, not a bug: it means direct thermal is a receipt medium and not a windshield medium, and the matrix says so rather than softening it into a caveat.

### Security and privacy

- The rig transmits nothing. FR-16 bars cloud decode services and upload-happy scanner applications; a payload leaving the rig would put an odometer and a service date on somebody else's server, which is exactly what the fragment placement exists to prevent (INV-26).
- Fixtures are synthetic and marked, so a leaked value in a photograph, a log, or an evidence bundle is identifiable as ours and harmless (INV-4).
- Photographs of printed symbols are handled as machine-readable records rather than pictures: not attached to public issues, not committed to a public repository, not included in any artifact that leaves the evidence store.
- Where a platform camera resolves the payload URL, the run confirms the fragment reached no server log — a positive check on a property the architecture already provides by construction, cheap to assert and expensive to discover you had lost.
- No runtime code ships from this issue, so it adds no attack surface and no new origin to the CSP.

### Accessibility and performance

- FR-13's legibility measurement is an accessibility requirement as much as a claims requirement: a disclosure printed below the resolvable stroke width of the medium is unreadable for everyone and worse for anyone with reduced acuity. The measured minimum is recorded per medium and becomes a hard constraint on the artifact template.
- Contrast is measured on the actual medium rather than assumed from the source colours, because thermal print density, laser toner coverage, and label stock brightness each move it.
- The forty-character code's typography is measured for transcription accuracy, not just legibility. It is printed without separators deliberately, so that what is printed is exactly what the decoder accepts; the measurement asks whether a person reads it back correctly, not whether it looks fine.
- Decode time is a user-facing performance number with a real cost — a technician holding a scanner over a windshield with someone waiting — so FR-7's ceiling is treated as a product budget and recorded per cell rather than averaged away.
- The build-time gate is O(1) and hardware-free by design, so the expensive part of this issue stays out of the per-commit path.

## UX and content states

This issue ships no screens. It owns one product-visible behavior and constrains several others.

- **Owned — no symbol rather than a doubtful one.** Where a configuration key is absent or encoding fails, the artifact prints the symbol region empty. The browser already behaves this way and states why: an empty box is honest, a broken or stale image looks scannable and is not (INV-7).
- **Owned — the scope statement.** Wherever scannability is described, the wording is bounded to the media, scanner classes, hardware, and settings measured. Prohibited: any claim of universal scanning, and the prohibited claims vocabulary generally — nothing here is "guaranteed" and nothing "works with every" scanner.
- **Constrained — the disclosure block.** FR-13 sets a minimum legible size per medium. If the mandated lines do not fit at that size, the artifact carries fewer claims, not smaller type. In the limiting case that means printing the symbol and the code and no interval, date, or grade claim at all.
- **Constrained — storage framing.** Nothing on the artifact may imply a stored, synced, backed-up, or recoverable record (INV-5, INV-21, INV-25). The on-screen wording already says the scan shows the same values and does not move what is stored in this browser; the printed surface has no equivalent line yet and the template issue owns writing one. This issue proves that whatever it says is legible on the medium.
- **Constrained — the linear-scanner path.** For a shop whose only scanner is linear, the product behavior is the printed code, read by a person. That is documented as the path rather than discovered at a counter, and the shop-facing documentation says so plainly instead of implying the symbol will work.

## Test plan

### Automated

- Fingerprint calculator: a change to each individual input moves the fingerprint; identical trees produce identical fingerprints; mutating a shipped value moves it, proving the calculator reads rather than restates.
- Build gate: absent record fails; non-pass record fails; stale record fails; a matching fresh passing record succeeds; no environment variable, flag, or file makes it pass otherwise. Staleness is tested against back-dated records at, just inside, and just outside the window.
- Record-schema validation: required fields present, statuses restricted to the three permitted values, an attempt to write a fourth rejected. Matrix/record correspondence tested in both directions, mirroring the existing matrix-integrity test.
- Renderer geometry regression: the `conformance/test/sticker-qr.test.mjs` grid round-trip runs unchanged and gains cases for the chosen print error-correction levels, asserting module count and quiet zone at each.
- Print-pipeline determinism: the same fixture rasterized twice produces byte-identical output; a fractional dots-per-module configuration is rejected rather than silently rounded.
- Fixture-synthetic check: every fixture printed or photographed is drawn from the emitted vector set and carries the synthetic marker.
- Codec round-trip on every transcription result: a human-transcribed forty-character string decodes to a sticker equal to the fixture, asserted in software rather than judged by eye.

### Manual/device matrix

Every cell below is a physical run under the written protocol, recorded with hardware model, firmware, settings, operator, and date. A cell not run is `unproven`, and at Tier 1 that fails.

- **Direct thermal receipt stock at 203 dpi**, at each integer dots-per-module value in the sweep, read by phone camera, 2D imager, and linear laser scanner. Repeated at the printer's lowest and highest darkness settings, and with a visibly worn print head if one is available.
- **Laser-printed sticker/label stock**, same sweep and same three classes, on both matte and gloss stock — gloss plus a scanner's own illumination is a glare problem matte does not have.
- **LCD screen at low device-pixel-ratio**, swept by rendered symbol size, read by phone camera and imager, under normal indoor light, under direct light producing specular reflection, and at both brightness extremes. This is the cell the `~1.6 device pixels per module` figure belongs to and the one that re-derives it.
- **Working-range sweep** per passing cell: approach from too close to too far and rotate off-axis until decoding stops, recording the endpoints rather than one comfortable position.
- **Degradation series** on the print media: thermal fade under accelerated heat and light; a fold through the symbol centre and through a finder pattern; abrasion at a defined pass count and load; masked dot columns simulating dead print-head elements at one, two, and three adjacent columns, across a data region and across a finder pattern; feed skew; and mounting on a curved surface approximating a windshield.
- **Transcription run** in every failed state: a person who has not seen the fixture reads the forty printed characters aloud, a second types them, and the result is decoded in software. This is FR-9, and the reason the code is printed at all.
- **Legibility run**: the mandated disclosure lines printed across a range of point sizes per medium and read at reading distance, recording the smallest reliably readable size — including a reader with reduced acuity where one is available, since the point of the line is that someone reads it.
- **Real-dwell thermal observation**: printed artifacts left in a sun-exposed vehicle for a recorded period, re-scanned and re-read on return. Scheduled separately, cannot fail a CI run, unmeasured until it completes.
- **Red-run rehearsal**: change one fingerprint input, push, and confirm the build fails with the expected message and cannot be bypassed.

## Rollout and rollback

Land the matrix and the record schema first, with the build gate present but not yet enforcing, so that the shape of the evidence is agreed before any hardware is booked. Then measure the screen cell, because it needs no printer, it re-derives the number three shipped source locations already state, and it will either confirm the existing error-correction decision or invalidate it — better to know that before drawing artwork against it. Then measure the laser-stock cells, which are the most controllable print path. Then the thermal cells, which have the most variables and the least control. Bring cells up to Tier 1 one at a time, and only after each has produced a clean run under the final protocol version.

Flip the build gate to enforcing once the Tier 1 cells for the media the product actually uses are green, and rehearse the red run immediately, because a gate nobody has seen fail is a gate nobody knows works. Schedule the real-dwell thermal observation as early as possible: it is the longest pole and it is the one result that could change a medium decision after the artwork is drawn.

Rollback is asymmetric and worth stating plainly. Reverting this issue's tooling orphans no production behavior, because it ships none. But **the gate is not a rollback candidate**: if it fails, the correct response is a fresh measurement or a dated supersession narrowing the claim, because turning it off restores the build while removing the only check between a geometry change and an artifact that does not scan. And the deepest limitation is that artifacts already printed cannot be rolled back at all. A sticker on a windshield was printed under whatever geometry was current that day, and it stays there for months. That is why records are permanent and superseded rather than replaced, and why the shipped dots-per-module value carries a full integer step of margin rather than sitting on the cliff.

## Dependency rationale

Blocked by the M10 printed-artifact template issue **at draft stage only** — the dependency is two-phase: that issue's dimensioned draft is this issue's input, this issue's measured constraints gate the template's final ratification, and neither direction is circular because each consumes a different phase of the other. The draft is needed because a module edge is meaningless without the layout it has to fit inside: measuring a symbol in isolation and then discovering it does not fit beside the disclosure block means measuring twice. Blocked by `DOS-M09-008`, because this issue extends that suite's report semantics, tier definitions, staleness discipline, and matrix-integrity pattern rather than starting a parallel evidence pipeline with different words for the same three statuses. Blocked by hardware procurement, which is a real dependency and is stated as one rather than assumed away — a cell measured on an unrecorded borrowed device is not a cell.

This issue blocks every downstream M10 surface that makes a scannability claim, and it blocks the milestone's pilot and rollout work, because a shop cannot be told the artifact scans on its equipment until the equipment class has been measured. It supplies the M10 printed-artifact template issue with the minimum module edge, the quiet-zone margin, and the minimum legible stroke width — constraints that document cannot derive for itself, delivered before its final ratification rather than after it is printed. It supplies the shop-facing documentation with the linear-scanner path and the bounded scope statement. And it feeds the scope proposal that INV-19 requires with something that proposal otherwise would not have: measured evidence about whether the physical artifact at the centre of the feature actually works on the hardware shops own.

## Risks, decisions, and open questions

- Risk: the `~1.6 device pixels per module` figure is a source comment from a single observation, and treating it as a constant is how a measurement becomes folklore. Mitigation: FR-4 re-derives it with a stated sample size and corrects all three shipped locations — including the derived width floors in `app.css` — if it moves.
- Risk: the `medium`-over-`quartile` screen decision rests on that figure, so if the two levels do not in fact bottom out at the same floor, a shipped decision was made on a wrong premise. Mitigation: FR-4 requires the decision to be re-made rather than inherited. This is recorded as a real possible outcome, not an unlikely one.
- Risk: measurement drifts into demonstration — running until a cell passes and recording the good run. Mitigation: FR-7 makes a single retry a failure, the record stores attempt counts and the cliff rather than a verdict, and the shipped value carries a mandatory step of margin.
- Risk: a fractional dots-per-module ratio produces position-dependent failures that look like scanner flakiness. Mitigation: FR-5 prohibits it and the print pipeline rejects it rather than rounding.
- Risk: thermal paper on a sun-exposed windshield may fade past decoding within a period nobody has measured, which would make direct thermal a receipt medium and not a sticker medium. Mitigation: FR-12 schedules the observation and forbids stating durability as fact before it completes. This risk may end a medium; that is an acceptable outcome, recorded rather than argued away.
- Risk: a symbol decodes to something other than what was printed — the only failure mode worse than not scanning, because it is a wrong record that looks correct. Mitigation: FR-7's byte-identity assertion plus the existing renderer grid round-trip test.
- Risk: the build gate is the only continuously running part of this work, so it is what someone will want to bypass under deadline. Mitigation: no bypass flag exists, and the red-run rehearsal is an acceptance criterion.
- Risk: evidence photographs are decodable records. Mitigation: synthetic fixtures only, stated retention, non-public storage.
- Risk: shop-media rows create momentum for a capability INV-19 currently prohibits. Mitigation: FR-22 blocks them from gating, surfacing, and being cited as implemented, and Excluded states the amendment path rather than assuming it.
- Decision: scanner classes are defined by verified symbology support with a date, never by the device's marketing name.
- Decision: the linear-laser class is recorded as structurally unable to decode the symbol, and its row asserts the printed-code fallback instead — a boundary to publish, not a defect to fix.
- Decision: the printed forty characters are the durability argument, and FR-9 makes that a tested requirement in every state where the symbol fails.
- Decision: print error correction is decided per medium from the sweep, separately from the screen decision, closing the deferral in `qr_symbol.js` and the open question the print-contract issue defers to these damage runs.
- Decision: the shipped dots-per-module value must sit at least one integer step above the measured cliff, and both numbers are recorded; the value itself is specified by the print-contract issue and validated or corrected here through a filed defect — it is never decided in two places.
- Decision: the build gate is a fingerprint comparison with no hardware, no network, and no bypass — deliberately the dumbest check in the set, so its failures are always real.
- Open question: whether a certified symbol-quality verifier is worth its cost. Until one exists no ISO print-quality grade may be stated; this issue reports empirical decode behavior only, and says so.
- Open question: whether a one-dimensional symbology carrying the same forty characters should be added for linear-only shops. It is a new payload surface and a separate decision; a human reading the printed code is the alternative that already exists.
- Open question: the staleness window for a record. `SUPPORT_MATRIX.md` uses 30 days for a browser engine, but printers and scanners do not silently auto-update, so the right window is probably longer and probably keyed to hardware and stock changes rather than elapsed time. Set in the matrix with its reasoning, not inherited by analogy.
- Open question: how a change of paper supplier or label stock is detected at all, given that it changes a physical input the record depends on and nothing in CI can observe it. Recorded as an unsolved gap rather than papered over with a procedural promise.
